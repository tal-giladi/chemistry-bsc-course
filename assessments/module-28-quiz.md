# Module 28 quiz — Structure and Reactivity in Organic Chemistry

This quiz checks whether you can reason like a physical-organic chemist with this module's tools: read a potential energy surface and an energy profile, find the rate-determining transition state, turn barriers and product ratios into free energies, predict early or late transition states with the Hammond postulate, use Hammett σ and ρ (including σ+ and σ−) to predict and interpret rates and pKa values, rank carbocations, radicals and carbenes, and choose and read isotope-effect and trapping experiments. It has 10 scenario questions. The pass mark is 70 %.

Review these lessons first:

- [28.1 · Potential energy surfaces and reaction coordinates](../lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- [28.2 · The Hammond postulate and selectivity](../lessons/module-28/lesson-02.md)
- [28.3 · Linear free-energy relationships: Hammett](../lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- [28.4 · Reactive intermediates: carbocations, radicals, carbenes](../lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md)
- [28.5 · Probing mechanisms: isotope effects and trapping](../lessons/module-28/lesson-05.md)

Have paper and a calculator ready. A few questions need a short calculation (a rate from a barrier, a selectivity, a Hammett prediction or a radical-clock rate constant); the wrong options are the answers that common slips produce, such as using ln instead of log₁₀, forgetting to count hydrogens, taking the largest single barrier as rate-determining, or using σp where σ+ is needed.
