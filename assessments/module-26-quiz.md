# Module 26 quiz — Bio-inorganic Chemistry

This quiz checks whether you can reason like a bio-inorganic chemist with this module's tools: explain why life uses particular metals and how it gets scarce ones, match metals to donor sets, predict spin states and sizes of haem iron and read them from structures, interpret O₂ binding curves and Hill plots, predict how effectors move the curve, and reason about metalloenzymes, electron transfer and the energetics of respiration. It has 10 scenario questions. The pass mark is 70 %.

Review these lessons first:

- [26.1 · Metals in biology: why these elements](../lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- [26.2 · Bonding in transition-metal complexes revisited](../lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- [26.3 · Oxygen transport: haemoglobin and myoglobin](../lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- [26.4 · Metalloenzymes, electron transfer and respiration](../lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)

Have paper and a calculator ready. A few questions need a short calculation (a free-ion concentration, a saturation, a Gibbs energy from potentials or an electron current); the wrong options are the answers that common slips produce, such as reading the Hill coefficient as the number of sites, forgetting the number of electrons in $\Delta G = -nF\Delta E$, using the pH 0 potential of O₂ at pH 7 or assuming more driving force is always faster.
