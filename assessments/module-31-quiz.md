# Module 31 quiz — Computational Chemistry Lab

This quiz checks whether you can make the decisions a computational chemist makes every day with Avogadro, xtb and ORCA: what the ORCA licence allows, how to search conformers and turn energies into populations, how to choose a method and basis and tell method error from basis error, how to prove that a structure is a minimum or a transition state, how to scale calculated frequencies and convert NMR shieldings into shifts, how to turn a barrier into a rate, and how far to trust TD-DFT excitation energies. It has 10 scenario questions. The pass mark is 70 %.

Review these lessons first:

- [31.1 · Setting up Avogadro, xtb and ORCA](../lessons/module-31/lesson-01.md)
- [31.2 · Molecular mechanics and conformer search](../lessons/module-31/lesson-02.md)
- [31.3 · Hartree-Fock and DFT: geometry optimisation](../lessons/module-31/lesson-03.md)
- [31.4 · Calculated IR and NMR spectra versus experiment](../lessons/module-31/lesson-04.md)
- [31.5 · Transition states and reaction barriers](../lessons/module-31/lesson-05.md)
- [31.6 · Excited states with time-dependent DFT](../lessons/module-31/lesson-06.md)

Have a calculator ready. A few questions need a short calculation (a Boltzmann population, a ZPE-corrected barrier, a rate ratio); the wrong options are the answers that common slips produce, such as forgetting a degeneracy, adding a correction with the wrong sign or confusing ln with log₁₀.
