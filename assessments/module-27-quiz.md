# Module 27 quiz — Electromagnetism and Matter

This quiz checks whether you can use electrostatics to reason about molecules and materials: apply Gauss's law and Maxwell's relation, compute and interpret multipole moments, turn dielectric data into polarisabilities and dipole moments, and decide which intermolecular force dominates in a real case. It has 9 scenario questions. The pass mark is 70 %.

Review these lessons first:

- [27.1 · Maxwell's equations for chemists](../lessons/module-27/lesson-01.md)
- [27.2 · Multipoles and molecular charge distributions](../lessons/module-27/lesson-02.md)
- [27.3 · Polarisability and dielectrics](../lessons/module-27/lesson-03.md)
- [27.4 · Intermolecular forces from electrostatics](../lessons/module-27/lesson-04.md)

Have a calculator ready ($1/4\pi\varepsilon_0$ = 8.988 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; 1 D = 3.336 × 10⁻³⁰ C m; $k_B$ = 1.381 × 10⁻²³ J/K; $N_A$ = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹). The wrong options are the answers that common slips produce, such as a forgotten factor of two, the static permittivity used for light, an ion's dipole quoted without its origin, or a Keesom average used where molecules are locked in place.
