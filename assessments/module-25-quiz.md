# Module 25 quiz — Analytical Chemistry Lab 2

This quiz checks whether you can run and judge instrumental analyses: plan a measurement so the instrument works in its valid range, recognise the matrix effect or interference behind a strange result, choose the calibration that corrects it, and decide what a result with its uncertainty means for a label or a limit. It has 9 scenario questions covering mixtures and the phone spectrometer, fluorescence, atomic absorption, GC-MS, HPLC method development and cyclic voltammetry. The pass mark is 70 %.

Review these lessons first:

- [25.2 · Fluorescence: quinine in tonic water](../lessons/module-25/lesson-02.md)
- [25.3 · Atomic absorption and emission](../lessons/module-25/lesson-03.md)
- [25.4 · Gas chromatography and GC-MS](../lessons/module-25/lesson-04.md)
- [25.5 · HPLC method thinking](../lessons/module-25/lesson-05.md)
- [25.6 · Cyclic voltammetry in practice](../lessons/module-25/lesson-06.md)

Have a calculator ready ($F/RT$ = 38.92 V⁻¹ at 25 °C; quinine–chloride $K_\mathrm{SV}$ ≈ 130 L/mol; a DVD's track spacing is 0.74 µm). The wrong options are the answers that common slips produce, such as ignoring a second absorber, trusting the top library hit, averaging two calibrations that disagree or confusing resistance with kinetics.
