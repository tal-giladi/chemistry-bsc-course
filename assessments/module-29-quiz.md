# Module 29 quiz — Biochemistry and Enzymology

This quiz checks whether you can reason like an enzymologist with this module's tools: judge how stable a protein is from its melting data, read and fit Michaelis–Menten kinetics without falling into the linear-plot traps, tell inhibition types apart and correct IC50 values, explain a catalytic mechanism from structure and pH data, and decide which steps of a pathway are far from equilibrium and what an inhibitor will do there. It has 10 scenario questions. The pass mark is 70 %.

Review these lessons first:

- [29.1 · Protein structure and stability](../lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- [29.2 · Enzyme kinetics: Michaelis-Menten in depth](../lessons/module-29/lesson-02.md)
- [29.3 · Inhibition and regulation](../lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- [29.4 · Enzyme mechanisms: catalytic strategies](../lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- [29.5 · Metabolic pathways as chemistry](../lessons/module-29/lesson-05.md)

Have paper and a calculator ready. A few questions need a short calculation (a fraction unfolded, a specificity ratio, an inhibition constant, a barrier lowering or a reaction Gibbs energy); the wrong options are the answers that common slips produce, such as using log10 instead of ln, trusting a Lineweaver–Burk line, comparing IC50 values from different assays or judging reversibility from a standard Gibbs energy.
