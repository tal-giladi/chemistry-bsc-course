# Glossary

1003 terms used in the course, each with the lesson that introduces it.

## A

- **A0** — The ISO 216 paper size of 841 × 1189 mm (1 m²), the common size for scientific posters; A4 is one quarter of it in each direction ([33.3](lessons/module-33/lesson-03.md))
- **absorbance** — A = −log₁₀ T; proportional to concentration for dilute solutions ([19.1](lessons/module-19/lesson-01.md))
- **abstract** — A self-contained summary of a report in about five sentences: context, gap, what was done, result with uncertainty, meaning ([33.3](lessons/module-33/lesson-03.md))
- **abundance** — The mole fraction of an element's atoms that belong to one isotope ([07.1](lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md))
- **π acceptor** — A ligand such as CO or CN⁻ with empty π* orbitals that take electron density from the metal's t₂g orbitals, increasing Δ ([18.4](lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md))
- **accuracy** — How close a result is to the true value ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **acetal** — R₂C(OR′)₂, formed from an aldehyde or ketone and two alcohol groups with an acid catalyst and water removal ([16.2](lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md))
- **acetylide** — The anion of a terminal alkyne, a strong base and good nucleophile ([15.7](lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md))
- **acid–base extraction** — Moving an acid or base between organic and aqueous layers by changing the pH of the water ([24.1](lessons/module-24/lesson-01.md))
- **acid chloride** — RCOCl, the most reactive carboxylic acid derivative, made from the acid with SOCl₂ ([16.3](lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md))
- **acid ionisation constant (Ka)** — The equilibrium constant for an acid giving its proton to water; pKa = −log₁₀ Ka ([08.4](lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md))
- **activation energy** — The energy barrier Ea in the Arrhenius equation, in J/mol ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **activity (a)** — The effective concentration that makes μ = μ° + RT ln a exact; a = γ × concentration relative to the standard state, and 1 for a pure solid or liquid ([13.6](lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md))
- **activity coefficient (γ)** — The factor relating activity to concentration; 1 for ideal behaviour ([13.6](lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md))
- **acyl-enzyme** — The ester intermediate formed between the substrate's acyl group and the catalytic serine ([29.4](lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md))
- **adding in quadrature** — Combining independent uncertainties as the square root of the sum of their squares ([02.5](lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md))
- **adiabatic** — With no heat exchanged (q = 0); for an ideal gas, a reversible adiabatic change keeps pV^γ constant ([13.1](lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md))
- **AFFN** — ASCII free-format numeric, the uncompressed number form in JCAMP-DX data lines ([05.4](lessons/module-05/lesson-04.md))
- **AICc** — Corrected Akaike information criterion; compares models fitted to the same data and penalises extra parameters ([29.3](lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md))
- **aldol reaction** — Addition of an enolate to an aldehyde or ketone, giving a β-hydroxy carbonyl compound; with heat it dehydrates to an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl ([16.4](lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md))
- **aliasing** — A frequency above the Nyquist limit appearing at a false lower frequency |f − m f_s| ([14.5](lessons/module-14/lesson-05.md))
- **alkoxide** — The conjugate base RO⁻ of an alcohol, a strong base and good nucleophile ([16.1](lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md))
- **allosteric effector** — A molecule that binds away from the active site and changes its affinity or activity ([26.3](lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md))
- **alpha-cleavage** — Breaking of the bond next to the carbon that carries O or N in a mass spectrometer, giving an ion stabilised by the heteroatom ([17.1](lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md))
- **amino acid** — A molecule with an amino group, a carboxyl group and a side chain on one carbon; 20 kinds build proteins ([06.2](lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md))
- **amperometry** — Measuring the current at a fixed potential, as in glucose and oxygen sensors ([19.5](lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md))
- **amphiphilic** — Having a water-loving (polar) part and a water-fearing (nonpolar) part, like phospholipids and soap ([06.1](lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md))
- **amphiprotic** — Able to act as an acid or a base, like water or HCO₃⁻ ([08.4](lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md))
- **amphoteric** — Able to react both as an acid and as a base, as Al₂O₃ and ZnO do ([18.1](lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md))
- **analysis plan** — The result to report, the allowed exclusions and the deciding test, written before the main data are seen ([33.2](lessons/module-33/lesson-02.md))
- **anharmonicity** — Departure of a real bond from a parabola, which makes vibrational gaps shrink with v and allows overtones ([12.4](lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md))
- **anode and cathode** — The electrode where oxidation happens and the electrode where reduction happens ([08.6](lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md))
- **anodic stripping voltammetry** — A trace-metal method that first deposits metal ions on the electrode, then oxidises them in one scan ([19.5](lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md))
- **anomeric carbon** — The carbon of a cyclic sugar that was the carbonyl carbon; it carries the hemiacetal OH ([16.6](lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md))
- **anomers** — Sugar diastereomers that differ only at the anomeric carbon (α and β) ([16.6](lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md))
- **anti** — A conformation with two groups at a 180° dihedral angle ([15.2](lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md))
- **anti-periplanar** — H and leaving group in one plane on opposite sides (180°); trans-diaxial in a cyclohexane ([15.6](lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md))
- **antiaromatic** — Describes a planar, fully conjugated ring with 4n π electrons, less stable than an open chain ([16.5](lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md))
- **Antoine equation** — Log10 p = A − B/(T + C), the empirical vapour-pressure fit used by the NIST WebBook ([13.5](lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md))
- **aromatic** — Describes a cyclic, planar, fully conjugated ring with 4n + 2 π electrons, which is unusually stable ([16.5](lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md))
- **array** — A numpy grid of numbers of one type, described by its shape and dtype ([05.2](lessons/module-05/lesson-02.md))
- **Arrhenius equation** — K = A e^(−Ea/RT), the temperature dependence of a rate constant; ln k against 1/T is a straight line with slope −Ea/R ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **atom economy** — The percentage of the reactants' mass that ends up in the desired product ([24.4](lessons/module-24/lesson-04.md))
- **atomic number** — Z, the number of protons in the nucleus; it fixes the element ([07.1](lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md))
- **atomic units** — Units with ħ = m_e = e = 4πε₀ = 1; lengths in bohr, energies in hartree (27.211 eV) ([12.5](lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md))
- **atomic weight** — The abundance-weighted mean relative atomic mass of an element's isotopes, as tabulated by IUPAC ([07.1](lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md))
- **ATP** — Adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy currency; hydrolysis to ADP and phosphate releases about −30.5 kJ/mol standard, about −50 to −57 kJ/mol in cells ([06.4](lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md))
- **attended time** — The time you yourself spend on each item of a study, as opposed to machine time while a calculation runs unattended ([33.1](lessons/module-33/lesson-01.md))
- **aufbau principle** — Electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy in the ground state ([07.2](lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md))
- **autocorrelation** — How well a signal matches a time-shifted copy of itself; its first peak after zero shift gives the period ([14.1](lessons/module-14/lesson-01.md))
- **Avogadro constant** — N_A = 6.022 140 76 × 10²³ mol⁻¹, exact by definition ([01.2](lessons/module-01/lesson-02.md))
- **axial / equatorial** — The two kinds of positions on a chair, parallel to the ring axis or out to the side ([15.2](lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md))
- **azeotrope** — A liquid mixture that boils without changing composition and cannot be separated by ordinary distillation ([24.2](lessons/module-24/lesson-02.md))

## B

- **B₀ and B_e** — The rotational constant of the vibrational ground state and of the hypothetical molecule at the potential minimum, B_v = B_e − α_e(v + ½) ([20.2](lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md))
- **band** — A near-continuous range of electron energy levels formed when many atomic orbitals combine in a solid; width about 4|β| for a chain ([32.4](lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md))
- **band gap (E_g)** — The energy range between the top of the highest filled band and the bottom of the next empty band ([32.4](lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md))
- **base pair** — A with T (two hydrogen bonds) or G with C (three) across the two strands of DNA ([06.3](lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md))
- **base peak** — The tallest peak in a mass spectrum, set to 100 %; often a fragment, not the molecular ion ([17.1](lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md))
- **basis set** — The set of atom-centred functions used to build molecular orbitals, such as def2-SVP or def2-TZVP ([31.3](lessons/module-31/lesson-03.md))
- **Bateman function** — The difference of two exponentials describing an intermediate in A → B → C, used for drug absorption and elimination ([03.5](lessons/module-03/lesson-05.md))
- **beats** — The slow rise and fall of amplitude when two close frequencies add; in coupled oscillators it moves energy back and forth ([14.3](lessons/module-14/lesson-03.md))
- **Bell–Evans–Polanyi relation** — Within a family of reactions the activation energy varies linearly with the reaction enthalpy, Ea = E0 + αΔH ([28.2](lessons/module-28/lesson-02.md))
- **benzilic acid rearrangement** — Conversion of a 1,2-diketone into an α-hydroxy acid by hydroxide, with a 1,2-shift of an aryl group ([24.6](lessons/module-24/lesson-06.md))
- **bias** — The mean of many results minus the reference value; a measure of systematic error ([09.1](lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md))
- **Birge–Sponer extrapolation** — Estimating a dissociation energy from the area under a plot of vibrational gaps ΔG(v + ½) against v ([20.4](lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md))
- **blind prediction** — A model prediction for data not used to fit the model; the strongest test of a model ([30.4](lessons/module-30/lesson-04.md))
- **Bloch's theorem** — In a periodic lattice, electron wavefunctions are a plane wave e^{ikx} times a function with the lattice period ([32.4](lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md))
- **Bohr effect** — The fall in haemoglobin's O₂ affinity when pH falls or CO₂ rises ([26.3](lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md))
- **Bohr radius** — A₀ = 52.9 pm, the most probable electron–nucleus distance in hydrogen's 1s state and the atomic unit of length ([12.1](lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md))
- **Boltzmann distribution** — The fraction of molecules in level i at temperature T, p_i = g_i e^(−E_i/k_BT)/q ([21.1](lessons/module-21/lesson-01.md))
- **bomb calorimeter** — A sealed steel vessel in which a sample burns in oxygen at constant volume, measuring ΔU after calibration with benzoic acid ([13.2](lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md))
- **σ bond** — A bond with its electron density on the line between the nuclei; every single bond is one ([15.1](lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md))
- **bond alternation** — The pattern of short and long bonds in a polyene; it opens an energy gap that the flat box model lacks ([30.4](lessons/module-30/lesson-04.md))
- **bond order** — The number of shared electron pairs between two atoms, possibly fractional in a resonance hybrid ([07.3](lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md))
- **boolean mask** — An array of True/False values that selects elements of another array ([05.2](lessons/module-05/lesson-02.md))
- **Born–Haber cycle** — Hess's-law cycle from the elements to the gas-phase ions that gives a lattice enthalpy from measured formation, atomisation, ionisation and electron-affinity data ([18.2](lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md))
- **Born–Oppenheimer approximation** — Treating nuclear motion on a potential energy curve set by the much faster electrons ([20.4](lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md))
- **Brønsted acid and base** — A proton (H⁺) donor and a proton acceptor ([08.4](lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md))
- **Bragg's law** — Nλ = 2d sin θ, the condition for X-rays reflected from planes of spacing d to add up ([32.3](lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md))
- **Breit–Rabi formula** — The exact energies of an electron spin coupled to a nuclear spin in any field; it gives the second-order shifts of large hyperfine patterns ([30.2](lessons/module-30/lesson-02.md))
- **Brillouin zone** — The range of wave vectors from −π/a to π/a (in one dimension) that labels all distinct electron states of a lattice ([32.4](lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md))
- **brine** — Saturated aqueous sodium chloride, used to break emulsions and to pull water out of an organic layer ([24.1](lessons/module-24/lesson-01.md))
- **broadband decoupling** — Irradiation of all protons while a ¹³C spectrum is recorded, which collapses each carbon signal to one line ([17.4](lessons/module-17/lesson-04.md))
- **broadcasting** — Numpy's rule for combining arrays of different shapes by stretching axes of length 1 ([05.2](lessons/module-05/lesson-02.md))
- **bromonium ion** — A three-membered ring of two carbons and Br⁺, the intermediate of Br₂ addition ([15.7](lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md))
- **buffer** — A solution of a weak acid and its conjugate base that resists changes in pH ([08.4](lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md))
- **buffer capacity** — The amount of strong base (mol/L) needed to raise a buffer's pH by one unit ([09.6](lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md))
- **bulk metals** — The metal ions present in cells at millimolar levels (Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺), used for charge, signalling and phosphate binding ([26.1](lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md))
- **bumping** — Sudden violent boiling of a superheated liquid; prevented with a stirrer bar or boiling chips added before heating ([24.1](lessons/module-24/lesson-01.md))
- **burette** — A graduated glass tube with a tap that delivers a measured volume of titrant, read to 0.01 mL ([10.1](lessons/module-10/lesson-01.md))
- **Butler–Volmer equation** — I = i0[exp(αa fη) − exp(−αc fη)], the current of an electrode reaction as a function of overpotential ([32.1](lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md))

## C

- **calibration** — Measuring something with a known value to find and correct an instrument's bias ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **calorimeter** — An insulated container in which heat from a process is measured through a temperature change ([08.1](lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md))
- **calorimeter constant** — The heat capacity of a whole calorimeter in kJ/K, found by calibration with a standard ([13.2](lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md))
- **canonical partition function (Q)** — The partition function of a whole system of N molecules at fixed T and V; q^N for distinguishable and q^N/N! for indistinguishable (gas) molecules ([21.2](lessons/module-21/lesson-02.md))
- **carbene** — A neutral carbon with two bonds and two non-bonding electrons, as in CH2; singlet (paired) or triplet (unpaired) ([28.4](lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md))
- **carbinol carbon** — The carbon atom that carries the OH group of an alcohol ([16.1](lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md))
- **carbocation** — A positively charged, sp², planar carbon with only six valence electrons ([15.5](lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md))
- **α-carbon** — The carbon atom next to a carbonyl group ([16.4](lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md))
- **carbonic anhydrase** — The zinc enzyme that hydrates CO₂ to bicarbonate, with a turnover near 10⁶ s⁻¹ ([26.4](lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md))
- **carboxylate** — The conjugate base RCOO⁻ of a carboxylic acid, with the charge shared by two oxygens ([16.3](lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md))
- **Carnot efficiency** — The maximum fraction of heat any engine can turn into work, 1 − Tc/Th with kelvin temperatures ([13.3](lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md))
- **catalyst** — A substance that speeds a reaction by a new mechanism and is regenerated, leaving the equilibrium constant unchanged ([22.5](lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md))
- **catalytic triad** — The Ser–His–Asp group of serine proteases (Ser195, His57, Asp102 in chymotrypsin) ([29.4](lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md))
- **central difference** — The derivative estimate (y[i+1] − y[i−1]) / (2h); error proportional to h² ([02.2](lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md))
- **centrifugal barrier** — The ℓ(ℓ+1)ħ²/(2μr²) term in the radial equation that keeps high-ℓ electrons away from the nucleus ([12.5](lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md))
- **centrifugal distortion constant** — D, the small correction −DJ²(J+1)² to rotational levels caused by the bond stretching as the molecule spins ([20.2](lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md))
- **centrifugal distortion constant (D)** — The small constant in F(J) = BJ(J+1) − DJ²(J+1)² that describes how a rotating bond stretches; for HCl about 5 × 10⁻⁴ cm⁻¹ ([30.1](lessons/module-30/lesson-01.md))
- **certified reference material (CRM)** — A material whose content is certified by a national lab or accredited producer, with a stated uncertainty, used to check bias ([09.1](lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md))
- **chain reaction** — A mechanism in which reactive carriers are regenerated by propagation steps, with initiation and termination steps ([22.3](lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md))
- **chain rule** — The derivative of f(g(x)) is f′(g(x)) times g′(x) ([02.2](lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md))
- **chair conformation** — The strain-free folded shape of cyclohexane ([15.2](lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md))
- **character table** — The table of characters of the irreducible representations of a point group, with the functions that transform like each ([20.6](lessons/module-20/lesson-06.md))
- **characteristic equation** — Det(A − λI) = 0 for a matrix, or m r² + b r + k = 0 for an oscillator; its roots give eigenvalues or exponential rates ([03.2](lessons/module-03/lesson-02.md))
- **charge balance** — The condition that the total positive and negative charge in a solution are equal ([09.5](lessons/module-09/lesson-05.md))
- **charge-transfer band** — An intense absorption in which an electron moves between ligand and metal orbitals, as in permanganate ([18.5](lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md))
- **charge-transfer resistance (R_ct)** — The slope dη/di near equilibrium, R_ct = RT/(F i0) ([32.1](lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md))
- **chartjunk** — Figure elements that are neither data nor needed to read the data, such as 3D effects or heavy grids ([11.3](lessons/module-11/lesson-03.md))
- **check standard** — A standard re-measured during and at the end of a run to show that the calibration has not drifted ([25.3](lessons/module-25/lesson-03.md))
- **chelate** — A complex in which one ligand binds the metal through two or more donor atoms, forming a ring ([18.3](lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md))
- **chelate effect** — The much larger formation constant of a chelate than of the comparable complex with monodentate ligands, mainly an entropy effect ([18.3](lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md))
- **chelating agent** — A ligand that binds one metal ion through several donor atoms at once, such as EDTA ([10.3](lessons/module-10/lesson-03.md))
- **chemical potential (μ)** — The partial molar Gibbs energy of a component; matter moves from high to low μ ([13.4](lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md))
- **chemical shift** — The position of an NMR signal relative to TMS, in parts per million of the spectrometer frequency ([17.3](lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md))
- **chemically equivalent** — Describes nuclei that symmetry or fast rotation can interchange; they give one NMR signal ([17.3](lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md))
- **chemoselectivity** — A reagent's preference for one functional group over another in the same molecule ([16.7](lessons/module-16/lesson-07.md))
- **Cheng–Prusoff relation** — For a competitive inhibitor, Ki = IC50/(1 + [S]/Km) ([29.3](lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md))
- **chi-square** — The sum of squared residuals each divided by its standard uncertainty ([05.3](lessons/module-05/lesson-03.md))
- **chiral** — Not superimposable on its mirror image ([15.3](lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md))
- **CID** — PubChem compound identifier, the number of a compound's PubChem page ([01.4](lessons/module-01/lesson-04.md))
- **CIP rules** — The Cahn–Ingold–Prelog rules that rank groups by atomic number to assign R/S and E/Z ([15.3](lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md))
- **citation chasing** — Finding related work backward through a paper's references and forward through the papers that cite it ([11.2](lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md))
- **Clément–Desormes method** — A measurement of γ from manometer readings before and after a brief adiabatic expansion of a gas in a bottle: γ ≈ h1/(h1 − h2) ([23.1](lessons/module-23/lesson-01.md))
- **Claisen condensation** — Reaction of an ester enolate with a second ester to give a β-keto ester ([16.4](lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md))
- **Claisen–Schmidt condensation** — A crossed aldol condensation between an aromatic aldehyde without α-hydrogens and a ketone ([24.5](lessons/module-24/lesson-05.md))
- **Clapeyron equation** — Dp/dT = ΔtrsH/(T ΔtrsV), the slope of any phase-coexistence line ([13.5](lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md))
- **Class A glassware** — Volumetric glassware made to the tightest tolerance; Class B is allowed twice the tolerance ([07.7](lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md))
- **classical least squares (CLS)** — Fitting a measured spectrum as a sum of the pure-component spectra, each scaled by its concentration, over many wavelengths ([25.1](lessons/module-25/lesson-01.md))
- **Clausius–Clapeyron equation** — Ln(p2/p1) = −(ΔvapH/R)(1/T2 − 1/T1), the vapour-pressure curve when one phase is an ideal gas ([13.5](lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md))
- **Clausius–Mossotti equation** — (εr − 1)/(εr + 2) = Nα/3ε₀, linking a material's permittivity to its molecules' polarisability ([27.3](lessons/module-27/lesson-03.md))
- **closed system** — A system that exchanges energy but not matter with its surroundings; an open system exchanges both, an isolated system neither ([13.1](lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md))
- **co-elution** — Two or more compounds leaving the column at the same time, giving one peak with a mixed spectrum ([25.4](lessons/module-25/lesson-04.md))
- **co-precipitation** — Impurities carried down with a precipitate, inside or on its crystals ([09.6](lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md))
- **co-spot** — A TLC lane in which two samples are spotted on top of each other to test whether they are the same compound ([24.3](lessons/module-24/lesson-03.md))
- **codon** — Three bases of mRNA that specify one amino acid or a stop ([06.3](lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md))
- **cold denaturation** — Unfolding of a protein on cooling, predicted by the stability curve, usually below 0 °C ([29.1](lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md))
- **combination differences** — Differences of two lines that share a level, used to get the rotational constant of the other level independently ([20.3](lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md))
- **common-ion effect** — The lower solubility of a salt in a solution that already contains one of its ions ([09.6](lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md))
- **competitive inhibitor** — Binds free enzyme only; raises the apparent Km and leaves Vmax unchanged ([29.3](lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md))
- **complementary colour** — The colour seen when a band of visible light is absorbed from white light, opposite the absorbed colour on the colour wheel ([18.5](lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md))
- **σ-complex (arenium ion)** — The cationic intermediate formed when an aromatic ring attacks an electrophile ([16.5](lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md))
- **composition matrix** — A matrix with one row per element plus a charge row and one column per species; balanced coefficients lie in its null space ([08.5](lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md))
- **compressibility factor** — Z = PVm/RT; 1 for an ideal gas, below 1 when attractions dominate, above 1 when molecular size dominates ([07.6](lessons/module-07/lesson-06.md))
- **condition number** — How much a linear system can amplify relative errors in its data; near 1 is good, large means ill-conditioned ([03.1](lessons/module-03/lesson-01.md))
- **conditional formation constant** — A formation constant corrected for side reactions at a given pH, K′f = α(Y⁴⁻) Kf for EDTA ([10.3](lessons/module-10/lesson-03.md))
- **conductivity** — Κ = 1/ρ, the inverse of resistivity, in S/m; what a conductivity meter reports for a solution ([04.4](lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md))
- **confidence interval** — A range x̄ ± t s/√n that, over many repeated experiments, contains the true mean at the stated rate ([09.2](lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md))
- **configuration** — The fixed 3D arrangement at a stereocentre or double bond; changing it needs a bond broken ([15.3](lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md))
- **conformation** — A shape a molecule takes by rotation about single bonds, without breaking bonds ([15.2](lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md))
- **conformer search** — A systematic, stochastic or metadynamics exploration that finds the distinct low-energy conformers of a flexible molecule ([31.2](lessons/module-31/lesson-02.md))
