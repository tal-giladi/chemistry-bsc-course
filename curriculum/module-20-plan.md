# Module 20 plan — Molecular Spectroscopy and Symmetry

Technion: 01240417 Physical Chemistry: Molecular Spectroscopy (3.5 points). Covers absorption and emission, rotational and vibrational spectra, Raman, electronic spectra and Franck–Condon, symmetry and group theory (plan.md 2.6). Depth: a first physical-chemistry spectroscopy course (Atkins-level), with real NIST data in every lesson.
Builds on: 12.4 (harmonic and Morse oscillator, HCl constants), 12.5 (rigid rotor, populations), 12.6 (transition dipole, parity, selection rules), 14.4 (CO2 normal modes, IR activity, Fermi resonance), 17.2 (IR band positions, gas vs liquid), 07.4 (VSEPR shapes), 18.3 (isomers by symmetry), 18.5 (Laporte rule, colour). Forward: 21 (partition functions from these constants), 25 (fluorescence, instruments), 30 (HCl rovibrational lab, ESR), 31 (computed IR).
Simulation (outline §5): ir-esr preset `hcl`, linked in 20.3 and 20.6.
Hands-on: Python labs in `labs/module-20/` (numpy, scipy, matplotlib only, pinned in `labs/common/requirements.txt`), real NIST data, pencil sets with answers in `<details>`, one kitchen model-building activity (20.5) and one optional highlighter fluorescence observation (20.4). Shared helper `spec20.py`: CODATA 2022 constants, NIST atomic masses, NIST WebBook diatomic constants (HCl, CO, N2, I2), term values, a finite-difference solver for Morse wavefunctions, a symmetry-element finder and character tables.
Data: `co_rotational_lines.csv` (12C16O and 13C16O v = 0 microwave lines, NIST Diatomic Spectral Database SRD 114), `hcl_band_peaks.csv` (absorption maxima read from the NIST WebBook gas-phase HCl spectrum, Coblentz No. 8758, 4 cm⁻¹ resolution), `nist_fundamentals.csv` (fundamental counts and wavenumbers from the NIST WebBook vibrational tables, Shimanouchi 1972). No SDBS data anywhere.
Module practical: lesson 20.6 Part B — (1) HCl and CO side by side from NIST data: ν0, B0, B1 (HCl), r0, force constant, and the CO branch-maximum check; (2) point groups of 10 molecules with the finder and the flowchart; (3) IR/Raman band counts for six of them by the reduction formula, checked against NIST. Report with the lab-report template.
