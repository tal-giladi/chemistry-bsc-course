- **Maxwell's equations** — the four laws of electromagnetism: Gauss's law for E and for B, Faraday's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law; together they predict light travelling at c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀) (lessons/module-27/lesson-01.md)
- **Gauss's law** — the electric flux out of any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by ε₀; charges outside add no net flux (lessons/module-27/lesson-01.md)
- **flux** — the amount of a field passing through a surface, outward minus inward (lessons/module-27/lesson-01.md)
- **displacement field (D)** — D = ε₀E + P; its flux counts only free charges, and in a linear medium D = εrε₀E (lessons/module-27/lesson-01.md)
- **Maxwell's relation** — n² = εr for a non-magnetic medium, valid only when both are taken at the same frequency (lessons/module-27/lesson-01.md)
- **multipole moment** — one of the numbers (charge, dipole, quadrupole, ...) that summarise a charge distribution as seen from far away (lessons/module-27/lesson-02.md)
- **quadrupole moment (Θ)** — the traceless tensor Θab = ½Σq(3rarb − r²δab); for a linear molecule a single number, negative for CO₂ (−14.3 × 10⁻⁴⁰ C m²) (lessons/module-27/lesson-02.md)
- **multipole expansion** — writing the potential of a charge distribution as charge, dipole and quadrupole terms falling as 1/r, 1/r², 1/r³; valid only far from the distribution (lessons/module-27/lesson-02.md)
- **polarisability (α)** — the induced dipole per unit field, μind = αE; usually quoted as the polarisability volume α′ = α/4πε₀ in Å³ (lessons/module-27/lesson-03.md)
- **Clausius–Mossotti equation** — (εr − 1)/(εr + 2) = Nα/3ε₀, linking a material's permittivity to its molecules' polarisability (lessons/module-27/lesson-03.md)
- **molar polarisation** — Pm = (εr − 1)/(εr + 2)·M/ρ; with n² in place of εr it is the molar refraction (lessons/module-27/lesson-03.md)
- **Debye equation** — Pm = (NA/3ε₀)(α + μ²/3kT) = A + B/T; a plot against 1/T gives the dipole moment from the slope (lessons/module-27/lesson-03.md)
- **Onsager equation** — a correction to Debye for polar liquids that includes the reaction field of the surrounding dipoles (lessons/module-27/lesson-03.md)
- **Keesom interaction** — the thermally averaged attraction between two permanent dipoles, proportional to μ⁴/(kT r⁶) (lessons/module-27/lesson-04.md)
- **Debye (induction) interaction** — the attraction between a permanent dipole and the dipole it induces in a neighbour, proportional to μ²α′/r⁶ (lessons/module-27/lesson-04.md)
- **London dispersion coefficient (C₆)** — the strength of the r⁻⁶ attraction from correlated electron fluctuations, approximately ¾Iα′² for like molecules (lessons/module-27/lesson-04.md)
- **Lennard-Jones potential** — 4ε[(σ/r)¹² − (σ/r)⁶], a steep repulsion plus an r⁻⁶ attraction used in force fields (lessons/module-27/lesson-04.md)
