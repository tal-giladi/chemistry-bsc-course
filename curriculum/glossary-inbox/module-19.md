- **transmittance** — the fraction of light that passes through a sample, T = P_T/P_0 (lessons/module-19/lesson-01.md)
- **absorbance** — A = −log₁₀ T; proportional to concentration for dilute solutions (lessons/module-19/lesson-01.md)
- **molar absorptivity** — ε in A = εbc, in L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹; how strongly a species absorbs at a given wavelength (lessons/module-19/lesson-01.md)
- **stray light** — light that reaches the detector without passing through the sample; it caps the highest readable absorbance (lessons/module-19/lesson-01.md)
- **diode-array detector** — a row of photodiodes behind a grating that records a whole spectrum at once (lessons/module-19/lesson-01.md)
- **Stokes shift** — the difference between the absorbed and the emitted wavelength in fluorescence; emission is at longer wavelength (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **quantum yield** — the fraction of excited molecules that emit a photon (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **inner-filter effect** — loss of linearity in fluorescence when the sample absorbs enough light to shade the rest of the cell (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **Stern–Volmer equation** — F₀/F = 1 + K_SV[Q], describing collisional quenching of fluorescence (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **hollow-cathode lamp** — an AAS light source whose cathode is made of the analyte element, so it emits that element's lines (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **graphite furnace** — an electrically heated graphite tube that atomises a few microlitres of sample for AAS at µg/L levels (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **releasing agent** — an element such as La or Sr added in AAS to bind an interferent (for example phosphate) and free the analyte (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **ionisation suppressor** — an easily ionised element (K, Cs) added in excess to keep the analyte atoms neutral in a flame (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **inductively coupled plasma (ICP)** — an argon plasma at 6000–10 000 K used to atomise and excite samples for emission (ICP-OES) or mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) (lessons/module-19/lesson-02.md)
- **partition coefficient** — K_D, the ratio of the concentrations of one chemical form of a solute in two immiscible phases (lessons/module-19/lesson-03.md)
- **distribution ratio** — D, the ratio of the total concentrations of a solute in two phases, all forms included; depends on pH for acids and bases (lessons/module-19/lesson-03.md)
- **retention factor** — k = (t_R − t_M)/t_M, the time a solute spends in the stationary phase relative to the mobile phase (lessons/module-19/lesson-03.md)
- **selectivity factor** — α = k_B/k_A for two neighbouring peaks; α = 1 means no separation is possible (lessons/module-19/lesson-03.md)
- **theoretical plates** — N = 16(t_R/w)², a measure of column efficiency; plate height H = L/N (lessons/module-19/lesson-03.md)
- **resolution** — R_s = 2Δt_R/(w_A + w_B); 1.5 means baseline separation (lessons/module-19/lesson-03.md)
- **van Deemter equation** — H = A + B/u + Cu, plate height as the sum of multiple-path, longitudinal-diffusion and mass-transfer terms (lessons/module-19/lesson-03.md)
- **flame ionisation detector (FID)** — a GC detector that burns the eluate in a hydrogen flame and measures the ions; responds to almost all organic compounds (lessons/module-19/lesson-04.md)
- **retention index** — a peak's position relative to the n-alkanes run under the same conditions, 100 × the carbon number for each alkane (lessons/module-19/lesson-04.md)
- **reversed-phase chromatography** — HPLC with a non-polar stationary phase (C18) and a polar mobile phase; polar compounds elute first (lessons/module-19/lesson-04.md)
- **gradient elution** — raising the eluting strength of the mobile phase during an HPLC run (lessons/module-19/lesson-04.md)
- **internal standard** — a compound added in the same amount to every standard and sample, so that the area ratio cancels injection and preparation errors (lessons/module-19/lesson-04.md)
- **potentiostat** — an instrument that controls the potential of a working electrode against a reference electrode and measures the current (lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md)
- **supporting electrolyte** — an inert salt in excess that carries the current and suppresses migration of the analyte (lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md)
- **cyclic voltammetry** — sweeping the electrode potential linearly forward and back while recording the current (lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md)
- **Randles–Ševčík equation** — i_p = 2.69 × 10⁵ n^(3/2) A D^(1/2) C v^(1/2), the peak current of a reversible, diffusion-controlled couple at 25 °C (lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md)
- **anodic stripping voltammetry** — a trace-metal method that first deposits metal ions on the electrode, then oxidises them in one scan (lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md)
- **amperometry** — measuring the current at a fixed potential, as in glucose and oxygen sensors (lessons/module-19/lesson-05.md)
