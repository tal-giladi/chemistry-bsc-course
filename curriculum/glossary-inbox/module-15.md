- **hybrid orbital** — a mix of atomic orbitals on one atom (sp³, sp², sp) chosen to point along its bonds (lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)
- **σ bond** — a bond with its electron density on the line between the nuclei; every single bond is one (lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)
- **π bond** — a bond from side-on overlap of parallel p orbitals, above and below the bond axis; it blocks rotation (lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)
- **functional group** — a group of atoms that reacts in much the same way in every molecule that carries it (lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)
- **skeletal formula** — a drawing in which corners and line ends are carbons and H on carbon is not shown (lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)
- **SMILES** — a line of text that encodes a molecular structure, such as CCO for ethanol (lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)
- **SMARTS** — a pattern language, extending SMILES, used to search for substructures (lessons/module-15/lesson-01.md)
- **conformation** — a shape a molecule takes by rotation about single bonds, without breaking bonds (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **Newman projection** — a view straight down one C–C bond showing the front and back substituents (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **dihedral angle** — the angle between bonds on two adjacent atoms, seen along the bond that joins them (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **torsional strain** — the energy cost of eclipsed bonds; 12 kJ/mol for eclipsed ethane (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **steric strain** — repulsion when atoms are pushed closer than their van der Waals radii allow (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **gauche** — a conformation with two groups at a 60° dihedral angle (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **anti** — a conformation with two groups at a 180° dihedral angle (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **ring strain** — extra energy of a ring from bent angles, eclipsing and crowding; 115 kJ/mol for cyclopropane (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **chair conformation** — the strain-free folded shape of cyclohexane (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **axial / equatorial** — the two kinds of positions on a chair, parallel to the ring axis or out to the side (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **ring flip** — the interconversion of two chairs, which swaps axial and equatorial positions (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **1,3-diaxial interaction** — steric strain between an axial substituent and the axial H's two carbons away (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **force field** — a set of simple fitted energy formulas for bonds, angles, torsions and contacts, such as MMFF94 (lessons/module-15/lesson-02.md)
- **isomers** — different compounds with the same molecular formula (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **constitutional isomers** — isomers whose atoms are bonded in a different order (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **stereoisomers** — isomers with the same bonding order but a different arrangement in space (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **configuration** — the fixed 3D arrangement at a stereocentre or double bond; changing it needs a bond broken (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **chiral** — not superimposable on its mirror image (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **stereocentre** — an atom, usually an sp³ carbon with four different groups, where swapping two groups gives a stereoisomer (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **enantiomers** — stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **diastereomers** — stereoisomers that are not mirror images, such as cis- and trans-2-butene (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **meso compound** — an achiral compound with stereocentres, which is its own mirror image (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **CIP rules** — the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog rules that rank groups by atomic number to assign R/S and E/Z (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **R / S** — configuration labels: priorities 1→2→3 clockwise (R) or anticlockwise (S) with the lowest group away (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **E / Z** — double-bond labels: higher-priority groups on opposite sides (E) or the same side (Z) (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **specific rotation** — [α] = α/(l c), the optical rotation per dm path length and g/mL concentration (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **racemic mixture** — a 50 : 50 mixture of enantiomers; it shows no optical rotation (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **enantiomeric excess (ee)** — |x_R − x_S| as a percentage; equal to [α]obs/[α]pure (lessons/module-15/lesson-03.md)
- **curved arrow** — a symbol showing one electron pair moving from a lone pair or bond to an atom or bond (lessons/module-15/lesson-04.md)
- **nucleophile** — an electron-pair donor that attacks an electron-poor atom; a Lewis base (lessons/module-15/lesson-04.md)
- **electrophile** — an electron-pair acceptor with a δ+ or positive atom; a Lewis acid (lessons/module-15/lesson-04.md)
- **Lewis acid / Lewis base** — an electron-pair acceptor / donor (lessons/module-15/lesson-04.md)
- **leaving group** — the group that departs with the bonding pair in substitution or elimination; good ones are weak bases (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **SN2** — one-step bimolecular nucleophilic substitution with backside attack and inversion; rate = k[RX][Nu] (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **SN1** — two-step unimolecular nucleophilic substitution through a carbocation; rate = k[RX] (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **carbocation** — a positively charged, sp², planar carbon with only six valence electrons (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **rate-determining step** — the slowest step of a mechanism, which sets the overall rate (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **transition state** — the highest-energy arrangement along one reaction step (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **inversion of configuration** — the umbrella-like flip of a carbon in SN2 (Walden inversion) (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **polar aprotic solvent** — a polar solvent without O–H or N–H bonds, such as DMSO or acetone; it speeds SN2 (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **hyperconjugation** — stabilisation of a carbocation or alkene by electron donation from neighbouring C–H and C–C bonds (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **hydride shift** — a rearrangement in which H moves with its electron pair to a neighbouring carbocation (lessons/module-15/lesson-05.md)
- **β-elimination** — loss of H from one carbon and a leaving group from the next, forming C=C (lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md)
- **E2** — one-step bimolecular elimination needing anti-periplanar H and leaving group; rate = k[RX][base] (lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md)
- **E1** — two-step elimination through a carbocation; rate = k[RX] (lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md)
- **anti-periplanar** — H and leaving group in one plane on opposite sides (180°); trans-diaxial in a cyclohexane (lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md)
- **Zaitsev's rule** — small bases give mainly the more substituted, more stable alkene (lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md)
- **Hofmann product** — the less substituted alkene, favoured by bulky bases such as tert-butoxide (lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md)
- **kinetic isotope effect** — a change in rate when an atom is replaced by an isotope; C–D breaks about 7 times slower than C–H in E2 (lessons/module-15/lesson-06.md)
- **electrophilic addition** — addition to C=C in which the π bond first attacks an electrophile (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **Markovnikov's rule** — in HX addition, H goes to the carbon with more H's, via the more stable carbocation (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **Hammond postulate** — the transition state of a step resembles the species (reactant or product) closer to it in energy (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **bromonium ion** — a three-membered ring of two carbons and Br⁺, the intermediate of Br₂ addition (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **syn / anti addition** — two groups added to the same face / opposite faces of a double bond (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **hydroboration–oxidation** — BH₃ then H₂O₂/NaOH: syn, anti-Markovnikov addition of H and OH (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **enthalpy of hydrogenation** — the enthalpy change when H₂ adds to a C=C; a smaller release means a more stable alkene (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **acetylide** — the anion of a terminal alkyne, a strong base and good nucleophile (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
- **Lindlar catalyst** — poisoned palladium that hydrogenates alkynes to cis alkenes (lessons/module-15/lesson-07.md)
