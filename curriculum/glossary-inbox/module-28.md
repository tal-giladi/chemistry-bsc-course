# Glossary inbox — Module 28

- **potential energy surface (PES)** — the energy of a set of atoms as a function of the positions of their nuclei; minima are molecules, saddle points are transition states (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **stationary point** — a geometry where the gradient of the energy is zero: a minimum, a saddle point or a maximum (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **Hessian** — the matrix of second derivatives of the energy; the signs of its eigenvalues classify a stationary point (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **first-order saddle point** — a stationary point with exactly one direction of negative curvature (one imaginary frequency): a transition state (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **minimum-energy path (intrinsic reaction coordinate)** — the steepest-descent path from a transition state down to the two minima it connects; distance along it is the reaction coordinate (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **intermediate** — a species in a local energy minimum along a reaction path, with a finite lifetime, unlike a transition state (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **kinetic control** — product ratio set by the relative heights of the transition states, when products do not interconvert (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **thermodynamic control** — product ratio set by the relative Gibbs energies of the products, after they equilibrate (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **Curtin–Hammett principle** — when starting conformers interconvert faster than they react, the product ratio depends only on the difference between the transition states, not on the conformer populations (lessons/module-28/lesson-01.md)
- **Hammond postulate** — neighbouring states on a reaction path that are close in energy are close in structure: exothermic steps have early, reactant-like transition states; endothermic steps late, product-like ones (lessons/module-28/lesson-02.md)
- **Bell–Evans–Polanyi relation** — within a family of reactions the activation energy varies linearly with the reaction enthalpy, Ea = E0 + αΔH (lessons/module-28/lesson-02.md)
- **Leffler α** — the fraction of a change in reaction free energy that appears in the barrier; a measure of transition-state position from 0 (early) to 1 (late) (lessons/module-28/lesson-02.md)
- **intrinsic barrier** — the barrier of a reaction with zero driving force; λ/4 in Marcus theory (lessons/module-28/lesson-02.md)
- **reactivity–selectivity principle** — the rule of thumb that more reactive reagents are less selective; fails at the diffusion limit and for many reaction families (lessons/module-28/lesson-02.md)
- **linear free-energy relationship (LFER)** — a linear relation between the logarithms of rate or equilibrium constants of two reaction series, i.e. between their free-energy changes (lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- **Hammett equation** — log(kX/kH) = ρσ for meta- and para-substituted benzene derivatives (lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- **substituent constant σ** — log(KX/KH) for the ionisation of a substituted benzoic acid in water at 25 °C; positive for electron-withdrawing groups (lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- **reaction constant ρ** — the slope of a Hammett plot; its sign shows which charge develops near the ring, its size how strongly (lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- **σ+ (sigma-plus)** — substituent constants for reactions in which a positive charge is conjugated with a para donor, defined from cumyl chloride solvolysis (lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- **σ− (sigma-minus)** — substituent constants for reactions in which a negative charge or lone pair is conjugated with a para acceptor, as in phenoxides (lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- **Yukawa–Tsuno equation** — log(kX/kH) = ρ[σ + r(σ+ − σ)], interpolating between σ and σ+ with a resonance parameter r (lessons/module-28/lesson-03.md)
- **hyperconjugation** — stabilisation of an empty or half-filled p orbital by an adjacent C–H or C–C σ bond lined up with it (lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md)
- **1,2-shift** — migration of a hydride or alkyl group with its bonding pair to an adjacent cationic carbon, giving a more stable carbocation (lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md)
- **radical stabilisation energy (RSE)** — BDE(CH3–H) − BDE(R–H): how much more stable a radical R· is than methyl (lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md)
- **persistent radical** — a radical that survives long enough to be handled because bulk or resonance blocks its reactions, such as TEMPO (lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md)
- **carbene** — a neutral carbon with two bonds and two non-bonding electrons, as in CH2; singlet (paired) or triplet (unpaired) (lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md)
- **Skell test** — cyclopropanation of cis- and trans-2-butene: stereospecific addition shows a singlet carbene, scrambled stereochemistry a triplet (lessons/module-28/lesson-04.md)
- **kinetic isotope effect (KIE)** — the ratio of rate constants for isotopically light and heavy molecules, usually kH/kD (lessons/module-28/lesson-05.md)
- **primary isotope effect** — an isotope effect from a bond to the labelled atom that is made or broken in the rate-determining step; kH/kD typically 2–7 (lessons/module-28/lesson-05.md)
- **secondary isotope effect** — a small isotope effect (close to 1) from a labelled atom whose bonds are not broken (lessons/module-28/lesson-05.md)
- **radical clock** — a radical that rearranges at a known rate, used to detect radicals and to time competing reactions (lessons/module-28/lesson-05.md)
- **crossover experiment** — running two differently labelled reactants together to see whether fragments exchange between molecules (lessons/module-28/lesson-05.md)
