# Glossary inbox — Module 29

- **two-state model** — description of protein unfolding as a single equilibrium between native N and unfolded U, with no populated intermediate (lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- **Gibbs energy of unfolding** — ΔG_U = −RT ln([U]/[N]), the stability of a folded protein; positive for a stable protein (lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- **melting temperature (Tm)** — the temperature at which half of a protein is unfolded and ΔG_U = 0 (lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- **stability curve** — ΔG_U against temperature from the Gibbs–Helmholtz equation with ΔCp; it has a maximum and predicts heat and cold denaturation (lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- **cold denaturation** — unfolding of a protein on cooling, predicted by the stability curve, usually below 0 °C (lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- **m value** — the slope of ΔG_U against denaturant concentration, proportional to the surface exposed on unfolding (lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- **linear extrapolation method** — estimating ΔG_U in water by extrapolating ΔG_U = ΔG_U(H₂O) − m[D] to zero denaturant (lessons/module-29/lesson-01.md)
- **turnover number (kcat)** — substrate molecules converted per active site per second at saturation (lessons/module-29/lesson-02.md)
- **specificity constant (kcat/Km)** — second-order rate constant of free enzyme with substrate; decides between competing substrates (lessons/module-29/lesson-02.md)
- **Lineweaver–Burk plot** — 1/v against 1/[S]; useful for seeing inhibition patterns, poor for estimating constants (lessons/module-29/lesson-02.md)
- **Hanes–Woolf plot** — [S]/v against [S], the least error-distorting of the linear Michaelis–Menten plots (lessons/module-29/lesson-02.md)
- **Eadie–Hofstee plot** — v against v/[S], slope −Km and intercept Vmax (lessons/module-29/lesson-02.md)
- **integrated Michaelis–Menten equation** — Vmax t = ([S]₀ − [S]) + Km ln([S]₀/[S]), the time course without product inhibition (lessons/module-29/lesson-02.md)
- **competitive inhibitor** — binds free enzyme only; raises the apparent Km and leaves Vmax unchanged (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **uncompetitive inhibitor** — binds the enzyme–substrate complex only; lowers apparent Km and Vmax by the same factor (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **mixed inhibitor** — binds both free enzyme (Ki) and the ES complex (Ki′); called noncompetitive when Ki = Ki′ (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **IC50** — inhibitor concentration that halves the rate in a given assay; it depends on [S] (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **Cheng–Prusoff relation** — for a competitive inhibitor, Ki = IC50/(1 + [S]/Km) (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **AICc** — corrected Akaike information criterion; compares models fitted to the same data and penalises extra parameters (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **feedback inhibition** — inhibition of an early pathway enzyme by the pathway's end product, usually allosteric (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **zymogen** — inactive enzyme precursor switched on by cutting the chain, such as chymotrypsinogen (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **transition-state analogue** — stable molecule shaped like a reaction's transition state; a very tight enzyme inhibitor (lessons/module-29/lesson-03.md)
- **rate enhancement** — kcat/kuncat, the factor by which an enzyme speeds its reaction; ΔΔG‡ = RT times its natural log (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **general acid–base catalysis** — catalysis by a group that donates or accepts a proton during the reaction (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **covalent catalysis** — catalysis through a temporary covalent bond between enzyme and substrate (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **effective molarity** — the concentration of a reactant that would give the same rate as holding it next to its partner in an active site (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **catalytic triad** — the Ser–His–Asp group of serine proteases (Ser195, His57, Asp102 in chymotrypsin) (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **oxyanion hole** — backbone N–H groups that hydrogen-bond to the negatively charged oxygen of the tetrahedral intermediate (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **acyl-enzyme** — the ester intermediate formed between the substrate's acyl group and the catalytic serine (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **pH-rate profile** — a rate constant plotted against pH; a bell shape gives the pKa values of groups the reaction needs (lessons/module-29/lesson-04.md)
- **near-equilibrium step** — a pathway step with ΔG close to zero in the cell, whose net flux follows small concentration changes (lessons/module-29/lesson-05.md)
- **far-from-equilibrium step** — a pathway step with strongly negative ΔG in the cell; effectively irreversible and a control point (lessons/module-29/lesson-05.md)
- **substrate-level phosphorylation** — ATP made by direct transfer of phosphate from a high-energy substrate, as in phosphoglycerate kinase (lessons/module-29/lesson-05.md)
