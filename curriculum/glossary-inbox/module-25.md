- **classical least squares (CLS)** — fitting a measured spectrum as a sum of the pure-component spectra, each scaled by its concentration, over many wavelengths (lessons/module-25/lesson-01.md)
- **diffraction grating** — a surface with many equally spaced lines that sends each wavelength off at its own angle, d sin θ = mλ (lessons/module-25/lesson-01.md)
- **diffraction order** — the integer m in the grating equation; m = 1 is the first, brightest rainbow on each side of the direct beam (lessons/module-25/lesson-01.md)
- **wavelength calibration** — the fit that converts detector pixel positions into wavelengths using lines of known wavelength (lessons/module-25/lesson-01.md)
- **Stokes shift** — the difference between the absorption (excitation) and emission wavelengths of a fluorescent molecule (lessons/module-25/lesson-02.md)
- **inner-filter effect** — loss of fluorescence linearity because the sample absorbs so much excitation (or emitted) light that the viewed region is under-excited (lessons/module-25/lesson-02.md)
- **standard addition** — calibration by adding known amounts of analyte to portions of the sample itself and extrapolating to zero signal, which cancels matrix effects that scale the signal (lessons/module-25/lesson-02.md)
- **releasing agent** — an element added in excess (often La or Sr) that binds an interfering anion so the analyte metal atomises freely in a flame (lessons/module-25/lesson-03.md)
- **ionisation suppressor** — an easily ionised element (K or Cs) added in excess to keep the analyte as neutral atoms in a hot flame (lessons/module-25/lesson-03.md)
- **hollow-cathode lamp** — an AAS light source made of the analyte element that emits that element's narrow lines (lessons/module-25/lesson-03.md)
- **spike recovery** — the fraction of a known added amount of analyte that a method finds, used to check accuracy in a real matrix (lessons/module-25/lesson-03.md)
- **check standard** — a standard re-measured during and at the end of a run to show that the calibration has not drifted (lessons/module-25/lesson-03.md)
- **total ion current (TIC)** — a GC-MS chromatogram that sums all ions in each scan (lessons/module-25/lesson-04.md)
- **extracted ion chromatogram (EIC)** — a GC-MS chromatogram of one m/z value only, used to find compounds hidden under other peaks (lessons/module-25/lesson-04.md)
- **match factor** — a 0–999 similarity score between a measured and a library mass spectrum, typically a weighted dot product (lessons/module-25/lesson-04.md)
- **solvent delay** — the first minutes of a GC-MS run during which the ion source is off while the solvent elutes (lessons/module-25/lesson-04.md)
- **co-elution** — two or more compounds leaving the column at the same time, giving one peak with a mixed spectrum (lessons/module-25/lesson-04.md)
- **linear solvent strength (LSS) model** — the approximation log k = log kw − Sφ for reversed-phase retention against the organic-solvent fraction φ (lessons/module-25/lesson-05.md)
- **resolution map** — the resolution of the worst-separated pair plotted against a method variable such as % organic solvent (lessons/module-25/lesson-05.md)
- **system-suitability test (SST)** — replicate injections of a reference mix before a sequence, checked against limits for resolution, tailing, precision and plates (lessons/module-25/lesson-05.md)
- **tailing factor** — a measure of peak asymmetry; 1.0 is symmetric, values above about 2 make integration unreliable (lessons/module-25/lesson-05.md)
- **method validation** — the documented evidence that an analytical procedure is fit for its purpose: specificity, range, accuracy, precision, detection and quantitation limits (lessons/module-25/lesson-05.md)
- **uncompensated resistance (iR drop)** — solution resistance between reference and working electrode that shifts peak potentials by the current times the resistance (lessons/module-25/lesson-06.md)
- **standard heterogeneous rate constant (k⁰)** — the electron-transfer rate constant of a couple at its formal potential, in cm/s (lessons/module-25/lesson-06.md)
- **Nicholson's method** — obtaining k⁰ from the CV peak separation through the dimensionless parameter ψ = k⁰/√(πDnFv/RT) (lessons/module-25/lesson-06.md)
- **expanded uncertainty (U)** — the standard uncertainty multiplied by a coverage factor, usually k = 2, giving an interval of about 95 % (lessons/module-25/lesson-06.md)
