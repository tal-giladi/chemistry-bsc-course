- **quantisation** — the restriction of a quantity such as energy to certain separate values (lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md)
- **energy level** — one of the allowed energies of a bound quantum system (lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md)
- **Rydberg formula** — 1/λ = R_H(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²), the wavelengths of hydrogen's spectral lines (lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md)
- **Rydberg constant** — R∞ = 10 973 731.568 157 m⁻¹ (CODATA 2022); for hydrogen R_H = R∞/(1 + m_e/m_p) (lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md)
- **de Broglie wavelength** — the wavelength λ = h/p of a moving particle; quantum effects matter when it is comparable to the confinement size (lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md)
- **uncertainty principle** — Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2: position and momentum cannot both be sharp, so a confined particle has a minimum kinetic energy (lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md)
- **Bohr radius** — a₀ = 52.9 pm, the most probable electron–nucleus distance in hydrogen's 1s state and the atomic unit of length (lessons/module-12/lesson-01.md)
- **wavefunction** — ψ(x), the function that describes a quantum state; |ψ|² dx is the probability of finding the particle between x and x + dx (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **Schrödinger equation (time-independent)** — Ĥψ = Eψ, the eigenvalue equation whose eigenvalues are the allowed energies (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **Hamiltonian** — the operator for total energy, kinetic plus potential (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **normalisation** — scaling a wavefunction so that the total probability ∫|ψ|² dx equals 1 (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **particle in a box** — a particle confined between infinite walls, with E_n = n²h²/(8mL²) (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **node** — a point (or surface) where a wavefunction is zero; more nodes means higher energy (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **zero-point energy** — the lowest energy of a confined system, always above the bottom of the potential (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **HOMO / LUMO** — highest occupied and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital; the first absorption usually links them (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **finite-difference method** — replacing derivatives by differences on a grid so that the Schrödinger equation becomes a matrix eigenvalue problem (lessons/module-12/lesson-02.md)
- **tunnelling** — passage of a particle through a barrier higher than its energy, with probability about e^(−2κd) (lessons/module-12/lesson-03.md)
- **decay constant (barrier)** — κ = √(2m(V₀ − E))/ħ, the rate at which a wavefunction decays inside a barrier (lessons/module-12/lesson-03.md)
- **transmission probability** — the fraction of particles that pass through a barrier (lessons/module-12/lesson-03.md)
- **scanning tunnelling microscope** — an instrument that images surfaces from the tunnelling current between a sharp tip and the sample (lessons/module-12/lesson-03.md)
- **tunnelling splitting** — the energy gap between the symmetric and antisymmetric states of a double well; divided by h it is the flipping frequency (lessons/module-12/lesson-03.md)
- **harmonic oscillator (quantum)** — a particle in V = ½kx² with equally spaced levels E_v = (v + ½)ħω (lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md)
- **vibrational quantum number** — v = 0, 1, 2, …, labelling the vibrational levels (lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md)
- **anharmonicity** — departure of a real bond from a parabola, which makes vibrational gaps shrink with v and allows overtones (lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md)
- **Morse potential** — V = D_e(1 − e^(−ax))², a model bond potential with exact levels G(v) = ω_e(v + ½) − ω_e x_e(v + ½)² (lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md)
- **overtone** — a vibrational transition with Δv = 2 or more, weak and below the multiple of the fundamental (lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md)
- **fundamental (vibrational)** — the v = 0 → 1 transition, the main band in an IR spectrum (lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md)
- **kinetic isotope effect** — the change in a reaction rate when an atom is replaced by an isotope, often from zero-point energy differences (lessons/module-12/lesson-04.md)
- **rigid rotor** — a model of molecular rotation with fixed bond length and levels F(J) = BJ(J+1) (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **rotational constant** — B = h/(8π²cI), in cm⁻¹; the rotational lines are spaced 2B (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **moment of inertia** — I = μr² for a diatomic molecule (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **degeneracy** — the number of distinct states with the same energy; 2J + 1 for a rotor, n² for hydrogen (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **spherical harmonics** — Y_ℓm(θ, φ), the angular wavefunctions of any rotating system; they give the s, p, d shapes (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **radial node** — a sphere on which the radial wavefunction is zero; there are n − ℓ − 1 of them (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **centrifugal barrier** — the ℓ(ℓ+1)ħ²/(2μr²) term in the radial equation that keeps high-ℓ electrons away from the nucleus (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **atomic units** — units with ħ = m_e = e = 4πε₀ = 1; lengths in bohr, energies in hartree (27.211 eV) (lessons/module-12/lesson-05.md)
- **spin** — an intrinsic angular momentum of the electron, m_s = ±½ (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **variational principle** — any trial wavefunction gives an energy at or above the true ground-state energy (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **orbital approximation** — treating each electron as moving in the average field of the nucleus and the other electrons (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **LCAO** — linear combination of atomic orbitals, the way molecular orbitals are built from atomic ones (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **bond order** — half the difference between bonding and antibonding electrons (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **paramagnetic** — attracted into a magnetic field because of unpaired electrons, as O₂ is (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **transition dipole** — μ_fi = ∫ψ_f* μ̂ ψ_i dτ; a transition absorbs light only if it is non-zero, with intensity proportional to its square (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **selection rule** — a rule saying which transitions have a non-zero transition dipole, such as Δv = ±1, ΔJ = ±1 or Δℓ = ±1 (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
- **parity** — whether a wavefunction is symmetric (even) or antisymmetric (odd) under x → −x (lessons/module-12/lesson-06.md)
