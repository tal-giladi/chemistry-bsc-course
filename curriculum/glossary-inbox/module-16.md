# Glossary inbox — Module 16

- **oxidation level (of carbon)** — a count for one carbon atom: −1 per attached H, +1 per bond to O, N or halogen; each rung up is a two-electron oxidation (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **carbinol carbon** — the carbon atom that carries the OH group of an alcohol (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **alkoxide** — the conjugate base RO⁻ of an alcohol, a strong base and good nucleophile (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **Williamson ether synthesis** — making an ether by SN2 attack of an alkoxide on a methyl or primary alkyl halide (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **epoxide** — a three-membered cyclic ether (oxirane); ring strain makes it open with nucleophiles (lessons/module-16/lesson-01.md)
- **nucleophilic addition** — a nucleophile adds to the carbon of C=O, giving a tetrahedral alkoxide that is then protonated (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **hydrate (gem-diol)** — the product R₂C(OH)₂ of adding water to an aldehyde or ketone (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Grignard reagent** — an organomagnesium halide RMgBr, made from R–Br and Mg in dry ether, that reacts as the carbanion R⁻ (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **work-up** — the step after a reaction, usually adding water or acid, that protonates intermediates and separates the product (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **imine** — a C=N–R compound formed from an aldehyde or ketone and a primary amine with loss of water (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **enamine** — a C=C–NR₂ compound formed from an aldehyde or ketone and a secondary amine (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **hemiacetal** — the product R₂C(OH)(OR′) of adding one alcohol to an aldehyde or ketone (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **acetal** — R₂C(OR′)₂, formed from an aldehyde or ketone and two alcohol groups with an acid catalyst and water removal (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **protecting group** — a group put on temporarily to stop a functional group reacting, and removed later (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **Wittig reaction** — conversion of C=O into C=C with a phosphorus ylide Ph₃P=CHR (lessons/module-16/lesson-02.md)
- **carboxylate** — the conjugate base RCOO⁻ of a carboxylic acid, with the charge shared by two oxygens (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **nucleophilic acyl substitution** — replacement of the group Y in RC(=O)Y by a nucleophile through a tetrahedral intermediate (addition, then elimination) (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **tetrahedral intermediate** — the sp³ species formed when a nucleophile adds to a carbonyl carbon (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **Fischer esterification** — acid-catalysed, reversible formation of an ester from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol (K about 4 for acetic acid and ethanol) (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **saponification** — hydrolysis of an ester by hydroxide, giving a carboxylate and an alcohol; effectively irreversible (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **acid chloride** — RCOCl, the most reactive carboxylic acid derivative, made from the acid with SOCl₂ (lessons/module-16/lesson-03.md)
- **enol** — the tautomer C=C–OH of a carbonyl compound (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **tautomers** — isomers that interconvert by moving a proton and a double bond, such as keto and enol forms (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **enolate** — the anion formed by removing an α-hydrogen from a carbonyl compound, with the charge shared by carbon and oxygen (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **α-carbon** — the carbon atom next to a carbonyl group (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **LDA (lithium diisopropylamide)** — a strong, hindered base (conjugate acid pKa 36) that forms enolates completely and kinetically (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **kinetic enolate** — the less substituted enolate, formed fastest with LDA at low temperature (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **thermodynamic enolate** — the more substituted, more stable enolate, favoured when enolates can equilibrate (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **aldol reaction** — addition of an enolate to an aldehyde or ketone, giving a β-hydroxy carbonyl compound; with heat it dehydrates to an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **Claisen condensation** — reaction of an ester enolate with a second ester to give a β-keto ester (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **Michael addition** — conjugate (1,4-) addition of a stabilised enolate to the β-carbon of an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **Robinson annulation** — a Michael addition followed by an intramolecular aldol condensation, forming a new six-membered ring (lessons/module-16/lesson-04.md)
- **aromatic** — describes a cyclic, planar, fully conjugated ring with 4n + 2 π electrons, which is unusually stable (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **Hückel's rule** — a planar, fully conjugated ring is aromatic with 4n + 2 π electrons and antiaromatic with 4n (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **antiaromatic** — describes a planar, fully conjugated ring with 4n π electrons, less stable than an open chain (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **electrophilic aromatic substitution** — replacement of a ring H by an electrophile through a σ-complex, keeping the ring aromatic (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **σ-complex (arenium ion)** — the cationic intermediate formed when an aromatic ring attacks an electrophile (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **Friedel–Crafts acylation** — attachment of an acyl group to an aromatic ring with an acid chloride and AlCl₃ (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **directing effect** — the influence of an existing ring substituent on where the next electrophile attacks (ortho/para or meta) (lessons/module-16/lesson-05.md)
- **pKaH** — the pKa of the conjugate acid of a base; the higher it is, the stronger the base (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **reductive amination** — formation of an amine from an aldehyde or ketone and an amine via an imine reduced as it forms (for example with NaBH₃CN) (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **anomeric carbon** — the carbon of a cyclic sugar that was the carbonyl carbon; it carries the hemiacetal OH (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **anomers** — sugar diastereomers that differ only at the anomeric carbon (α and β) (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **mutarotation** — the change in optical rotation as one anomer of a sugar equilibrates with the other through the open chain (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **glycoside** — an acetal formed at the anomeric carbon of a sugar; it does not mutarotate (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **reducing sugar** — a sugar with a free hemiacetal that can open to an aldehyde and reduce mild oxidants (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **Maillard reaction** — browning of food when amino groups react with reducing sugars, starting with imine formation (lessons/module-16/lesson-06.md)
- **retrosynthesis** — planning a synthesis backwards from the target by disconnections and functional-group interconversions (lessons/module-16/lesson-07.md)
- **disconnection** — an imagined bond break in retrosynthesis that corresponds to a real bond-forming reaction (lessons/module-16/lesson-07.md)
- **synthon** — an idealised charged fragment from a disconnection, represented in practice by a real reagent (lessons/module-16/lesson-07.md)
- **functional-group interconversion (FGI)** — changing one functional group into another to make a synthesis possible (lessons/module-16/lesson-07.md)
- **chemoselectivity** — a reagent's preference for one functional group over another in the same molecule (lessons/module-16/lesson-07.md)
- **longest linear sequence** — the largest number of consecutive steps from any starting material to the target; it sets the overall yield (lessons/module-16/lesson-07.md)
