# Glossary inbox — Module 20

- **gross selection rule** — the condition a molecule must meet to show a kind of spectrum at all, such as a permanent dipole for pure rotation (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **specific selection rule** — the allowed changes of quantum number in a transition, such as ΔJ = ±1 or Δv = ±1 (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **Einstein coefficients** — rate constants for absorption (B₁₂), stimulated emission (B₂₁) and spontaneous emission (A₂₁); A/B grows as ν³ (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **hot band** — a transition that starts from an excited vibrational level, weak because few molecules are there (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **Doppler broadening** — line width caused by the spread of molecular velocities along the light beam; grows with frequency and temperature, falls with mass (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **natural line width** — the minimum width of a line set by the lifetime of the upper state, Δν̃ = 1/(2πcτ) (lessons/module-20/lesson-01.md)
- **centrifugal distortion constant** — D, the small correction −DJ²(J+1)² to rotational levels caused by the bond stretching as the molecule spins (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **B₀ and B_e** — the rotational constant of the vibrational ground state and of the hypothetical molecule at the potential minimum, B_v = B_e − α_e(v + ½) (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **symmetric top** — a molecule with two equal principal moments of inertia, such as NH₃ or CH₃Cl (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **spherical top** — a molecule with three equal principal moments of inertia, such as CH₄ or SF₆; it has no pure rotational spectrum (lessons/module-20/lesson-02.md)
- **P branch and R branch** — the rotational lines of a vibrational band with ΔJ = −1 (lower wavenumber) and ΔJ = +1 (higher wavenumber) (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **combination differences** — differences of two lines that share a level, used to get the rotational constant of the other level independently (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **polarisability** — how easily an electric field distorts a molecule's electron cloud, α in μ_ind = αE; its change makes a vibration Raman active (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **Stokes and anti-Stokes lines** — Raman lines at lower and higher energy than the laser, from molecules that gain or lose a vibrational quantum (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **Raman shift** — the difference in wavenumber between the laser and the scattered light, equal to the vibrational (or rotational) energy change (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **rule of mutual exclusion** — in a molecule with a centre of inversion, no vibration is both IR and Raman active (lessons/module-20/lesson-03.md)
- **Born–Oppenheimer approximation** — treating nuclear motion on a potential energy curve set by the much faster electrons (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Franck–Condon principle** — electronic transitions are vertical, and band intensities follow the squared overlap of the vibrational wavefunctions (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Franck–Condon factor** — the squared overlap |⟨v′|v″⟩|² that sets the relative intensity of a vibronic band (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Birge–Sponer extrapolation** — estimating a dissociation energy from the area under a plot of vibrational gaps ΔG(v + ½) against v (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Jablonski diagram** — a level diagram of singlet and triplet states showing absorption, relaxation, fluorescence, intersystem crossing and phosphorescence (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Kasha's rule** — emission comes from the lowest excited state of a given spin multiplicity (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **Stokes shift** — the difference between the absorption and emission maxima of a fluorescent molecule (lessons/module-20/lesson-04.md)
- **symmetry operation** — a movement (rotation, reflection, inversion, improper rotation) after which a molecule looks exactly the same (lessons/module-20/lesson-05.md)
- **improper axis** — S_n, an axis for rotation by 360°/n followed by reflection in the perpendicular plane; S₁ = σ and S₂ = i (lessons/module-20/lesson-05.md)
- **point group** — the group of all symmetry operations of a molecule, named in Schoenflies notation such as C₂v or T_d (lessons/module-20/lesson-05.md)
- **character table** — the table of characters of the irreducible representations of a point group, with the functions that transform like each (lessons/module-20/lesson-06.md)
- **irreducible representation** — a basic symmetry pattern of a point group (labelled A, B, E, T) into which any representation can be decomposed (lessons/module-20/lesson-06.md)
- **reduction formula** — n_i = (1/h) Σ g_k χ(R_k) χ_i(R_k), the number of times irrep i occurs in a reducible representation (lessons/module-20/lesson-06.md)
