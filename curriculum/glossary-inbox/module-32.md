- **overpotential (η)** — how far an electrode's potential is from its equilibrium (Nernst) potential; negative drives reduction, positive drives oxidation (lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md)
- **Butler–Volmer equation** — i = i0[exp(αa fη) − exp(−αc fη)], the current of an electrode reaction as a function of overpotential (lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md)
- **exchange current density (i0)** — the equal and opposite partial current densities of an electrode reaction at equilibrium; for a simple couple i0 = F k0 C (lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md)
- **transfer coefficient (α)** — the fraction of the electrical energy F·η that lowers the activation barrier of an electrode reaction, usually near 0.5 (lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md)
- **Tafel slope (b)** — the potential change per tenfold change of current far from equilibrium, b = 2.303RT/(αF); 118 mV/dec for α = 0.5 at 25 °C (lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md)
- **charge-transfer resistance (R_ct)** — the slope dη/di near equilibrium, R_ct = RT/(F i0) (lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md)
- **micro-polarisation** — measuring i0 from the linear current–potential slope within about ±10 mV of equilibrium (lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md)
- **theoretical specific capacity** — the charge an electrode material can store per gram, Q = nF/(3.6 M) in mAh/g (lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)
- **specific energy** — energy stored per unit mass, capacity times voltage; mAh/g × V = Wh/kg (lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)
- **corrosion potential (E_corr)** — the mixed potential at which the anodic metal dissolution and the cathodic reduction on a corroding metal run at equal rates (lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)
- **corrosion current density (i_corr)** — the rate of metal dissolution at the corrosion potential, expressed as a current density (lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)
- **mixed potential** — a potential set by two different reactions whose currents balance, not by one reaction at equilibrium (lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)
- **polarisation resistance (R_p)** — the slope dE/di of a corroding metal within about ±10 mV of E_corr; i_corr = B/R_p (Stern–Geary) (lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)
- **sacrificial anode** — a more active metal (zinc, magnesium) connected to a structure so that it corrodes instead of the structure (lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)
- **corrosion inhibitor** — a substance that adsorbs on a metal and slows the anodic or cathodic corrosion reaction; efficiency = 1 − i_corr,inh/i_corr,bare (lessons/module-32/lesson-05.md)
- **lattice and basis** — the lattice is the set of points where a crystal pattern repeats; the basis is the group of atoms attached to each point (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **Miller indices (hkl)** — three integers naming a family of lattice planes, from the reciprocals of the plane's axis intercepts (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **Bragg's law** — nλ = 2d sin θ, the condition for X-rays reflected from planes of spacing d to add up (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **systematic absences** — reflections that are missing because of the lattice centring or symmetry; fcc allows only all-even or all-odd hkl, bcc only an even sum (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **structure factor (F_hkl)** — the sum of the waves scattered by all atoms of the unit cell for one reflection; the intensity is proportional to |F|² (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **zero shift** — a constant angular offset of all peaks in a diffraction pattern, from the goniometer zero or sample height; fitted together with the lattice parameter (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **Kβ satellite** — a weak peak from the Cu Kβ line (1.3922 Å), at the same d as a strong Kα peak but at lower angle (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **reciprocal lattice** — the lattice of points at 1/d along the normal of each family of planes; the natural map of a diffraction pattern and of electron wave vectors (lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md)
- **Scherrer equation** — τ = Kλ/(β cos θ), an estimate of crystallite size from peak width; a lower limit if the instrument width is not removed (lessons/module-32/lesson-05.md)
- **band** — a near-continuous range of electron energy levels formed when many atomic orbitals combine in a solid; width about 4|β| for a chain (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **band gap (E_g)** — the energy range between the top of the highest filled band and the bottom of the next empty band (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **Fermi level (E_F)** — the energy at which the Fermi–Dirac occupation is one half; the top of the filled levels at 0 K (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **Bloch's theorem** — in a periodic lattice, electron wavefunctions are a plane wave e^{ikx} times a function with the lattice period (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **Brillouin zone** — the range of wave vectors from −π/a to π/a (in one dimension) that labels all distinct electron states of a lattice (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **hole** — an empty level in a nearly full valence band, which moves like a positive charge carrier (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **intrinsic carrier density (n_i)** — the electron (and hole) density of a pure semiconductor, n_i = √(N_c N_v) exp(−E_g/2kT) (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **doping (n-type, p-type)** — adding donor atoms that give electrons (n-type, P in Si) or acceptor atoms that create holes (p-type, B in Si); n·p = n_i² (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
- **direct and indirect band gap** — direct: valence-band top and conduction-band bottom at the same k, efficient light emission (GaAs); indirect: different k, a phonon is needed (Si) (lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)
