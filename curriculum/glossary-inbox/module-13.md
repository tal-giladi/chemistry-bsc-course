- **closed system** — a system that exchanges energy but not matter with its surroundings; an open system exchanges both, an isolated system neither (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **path function** — a quantity such as heat or work whose value depends on how a change is carried out, not only on the start and end states (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **exact differential** — a differential dz = M dx + N dy that belongs to a state function, recognised by equal mixed partial derivatives (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **internal energy (U)** — the total energy of the molecules in a system; it changes only by heat and work crossing the boundary (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **first law of thermodynamics** — ΔU = q + w, with q the heat into and w the work done on the system (IUPAC convention) (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **reversible process** — an idealised change carried out through equilibrium states, which could be reversed by an infinitesimal change; it needs the least work for compression and gives the most work in expansion (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **adiabatic** — with no heat exchanged (q = 0); for an ideal gas, a reversible adiabatic change keeps pV^γ constant (lessons/module-13/lesson-01.md)
- **heat capacity (Cp, CV)** — heat needed to raise the temperature by 1 K at constant pressure, Cp = (∂H/∂T)p, or at constant volume, CV = (∂U/∂T)V (lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)
- **Shomate equation** — the polynomial Cp = A + Bt + Ct² + Dt³ + E/t² (t = T/1000) that the NIST WebBook uses for gas heat capacities over stated temperature ranges (lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)
- **Kirchhoff's law** — ΔrH(T) = ΔrH(298 K) + ∫ΔrCp dT, the temperature dependence of a reaction enthalpy (lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)
- **bomb calorimeter** — a sealed steel vessel in which a sample burns in oxygen at constant volume, measuring ΔU after calibration with benzoic acid (lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)
- **calorimeter constant** — the heat capacity of a whole calorimeter in kJ/K, found by calibration with a standard (lessons/module-13/lesson-02.md)
- **second law of thermodynamics** — the total entropy of a system and its surroundings increases in every real process and stays constant only in the reversible limit (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Carnot efficiency** — the maximum fraction of heat any engine can turn into work, 1 − Tc/Th with kelvin temperatures (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **third law of thermodynamics** — the entropy of a perfect crystal is zero at 0 K, which makes absolute entropies measurable from heat capacities (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **entropy of mixing** — ΔmixS = −nR Σ xi ln xi for ideal gases or ideal solutions, always positive (lessons/module-13/lesson-03.md)
- **Helmholtz energy (A)** — A = U − TS; at constant T and V a process runs when A falls (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Maxwell relation** — an equality between two partial derivatives that follows from the equal mixed partials of a thermodynamic potential, e.g. (∂S/∂p)T = −(∂V/∂T)p (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **chemical potential (μ)** — the partial molar Gibbs energy of a component; matter moves from high to low μ (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **reaction quotient (Q)** — the product of activities (for ideal gases, pi/p°) raised to their stoichiometric numbers for the actual mixture; ΔrG = ΔrG° + RT ln Q (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **Gibbs–Helmholtz equation** — (∂(G/T)/∂T)p = −H/T², the basis of the van 't Hoff equation (lessons/module-13/lesson-04.md)
- **phase rule** — F = C − P + 2, the number of intensive variables that can be changed freely for C components in P phases (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **triple point** — the single temperature and pressure where solid, liquid and vapour of a pure substance coexist; for water 273.16 K and 611.657 Pa (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **critical point** — the end of the liquid–vapour line, above which liquid and gas are no longer distinct; for CO₂ 304.18 K and 73.80 bar (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **supercritical fluid** — a substance above its critical temperature and pressure, with liquid-like density and gas-like flow (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Clapeyron equation** — dp/dT = ΔtrsH/(T ΔtrsV), the slope of any phase-coexistence line (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Clausius–Clapeyron equation** — ln(p2/p1) = −(ΔvapH/R)(1/T2 − 1/T1), the vapour-pressure curve when one phase is an ideal gas (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **Antoine equation** — log10 p = A − B/(T + C), the empirical vapour-pressure fit used by the NIST WebBook (lessons/module-13/lesson-05.md)
- **partial molar volume** — the increase in a mixture's volume per mole of a component added, at constant T, p and other amounts (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
- **Raoult's law** — the vapour pressure of a component of an ideal solution is its mole fraction times its pure vapour pressure (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
- **activity (a)** — the effective concentration that makes μ = μ° + RT ln a exact; a = γ × concentration relative to the standard state, and 1 for a pure solid or liquid (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
- **activity coefficient (γ)** — the factor relating activity to concentration; 1 for ideal behaviour (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
- **ionic strength (I)** — I = ½ Σ mi zi², the measure of ionic concentration that controls activity coefficients (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
- **Debye–Hückel limiting law** — log10 γ± = −0.509 |z+z−| √I for dilute aqueous ions at 25 °C (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
- **osmotic coefficient (φ)** — the ratio of a measured colligative effect to its ideal value, a direct measure of solution non-ideality (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
- **cryoscopic constant (Kf)** — freezing-point depression per mol/kg of solute particles, R T*² M/ΔfusH; 1.86 K kg/mol for water (lessons/module-13/lesson-06.md)
