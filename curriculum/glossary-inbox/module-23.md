- **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** — the spread of molecular speeds in a gas at temperature T; its most probable, mean and rms speeds are √(2RT/M), √(8RT/πM) and √(3RT/M) (lessons/module-23/lesson-01.md)
- **root-mean-square (rms) speed** — the square root of the mean squared molecular speed, √(3RT/M); it sets the kinetic energy and the pressure of a gas (lessons/module-23/lesson-01.md)
- **heat-capacity ratio (γ)** — Cp/Cv of a gas; 5/3 for monatomic and 7/5 for rigid diatomic ideal gases (lessons/module-23/lesson-01.md)
- **degree of freedom** — an independent way a molecule can store energy (translation, rotation, vibration); each active one adds R/2 to Cv,m (lessons/module-23/lesson-01.md)
- **Clément–Desormes method** — a measurement of γ from manometer readings before and after a brief adiabatic expansion of a gas in a bottle: γ ≈ h1/(h1 − h2) (lessons/module-23/lesson-01.md)
- **enthalpy of neutralisation** — the enthalpy change when an acid and a base react to form one mole of water; −55.8 kJ/mol for any strong acid with any strong base (lessons/module-23/lesson-02.md)
- **spectator ion** — an ion present in a solution that takes no part in the reaction, such as Na⁺ and Cl⁻ in a strong acid–strong base neutralisation (lessons/module-23/lesson-02.md)
- **enthalpy of solution** — the enthalpy change when one mole of a substance dissolves in a solvent; the small difference between lattice and hydration energies, of either sign (lessons/module-23/lesson-02.md)
- **ionisation enthalpy** — the enthalpy change when a weak acid gives up a proton in solution (lessons/module-23/lesson-02.md)
- **ICE table** — a table of initial, change and equilibrium concentrations used to find every concentration in an equilibrium from one measured value (lessons/module-23/lesson-03.md)
- **Job plot (method of continuous variations)** — a plot of a signal against the mole fraction of one reactant at fixed total concentration; its maximum shows the stoichiometry of a complex (lessons/module-23/lesson-03.md)
- **formation constant** — the equilibrium constant for forming a complex from a metal ion and a ligand, such as K for Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻ ⇌ FeSCN²⁺ (lessons/module-23/lesson-03.md)
- **isolation method** — keeping all reactants but one in large excess so the rate depends only on that one, giving pseudo-first-order kinetics (lessons/module-23/lesson-04.md)
- **pseudo-absorbance** — −log10 of the linearised intensity of a photo colour channel relative to a blank, used like an absorbance (lessons/module-23/lesson-04.md)
- **electromotive force (EMF)** — the voltage a cell's chemistry drives, equal to the terminal voltage when no current flows (lessons/module-23/lesson-05.md)
- **internal resistance** — the resistance inside a cell, from the electrolyte and electrode surfaces, that lowers the terminal voltage as current flows: V = E − Ir (lessons/module-23/lesson-05.md)
- **load line** — a plot of a cell's terminal voltage against current for several loads; its intercept is the EMF and its slope is minus the internal resistance (lessons/module-23/lesson-05.md)
- **potentiometry** — measuring a cell potential at almost zero current and converting it to a concentration with the Nernst equation (lessons/module-23/lesson-05.md)
- **Nernst slope** — the change in electrode potential per tenfold change in ion concentration, 59.16/n mV at 25 °C (lessons/module-23/lesson-05.md)
- **surface tension** — the energy needed per unit area of liquid surface, or the force per unit length in the surface, in mN/m (lessons/module-23/lesson-06.md)
- **surfactant** — a molecule with a water-loving head and an oily tail that gathers at surfaces and lowers surface tension (lessons/module-23/lesson-06.md)
- **micelle** — a cluster of surfactant molecules in solution with the tails inside and the heads facing the water (lessons/module-23/lesson-06.md)
- **critical micelle concentration (CMC)** — the surfactant concentration above which micelles form and surface tension stops falling (lessons/module-23/lesson-06.md)
- **Tate's law** — the weight of a drop falling from a tip is proportional to the surface tension times the tip radius (lessons/module-23/lesson-06.md)
- **Gibbs adsorption isotherm** — Γ = −(1/nRT) dγ/d ln c, linking the fall of surface tension with concentration to the amount adsorbed at the surface (lessons/module-23/lesson-06.md)
- **surface excess (Γ)** — the extra amount of a solute per unit area of surface compared with the bulk, in mol/m² (lessons/module-23/lesson-06.md)
