- **system and surroundings** — the part of the world being studied (the reacting chemicals) and everything else (water, cup, air) (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **exothermic** — releasing heat to the surroundings, q < 0 for the system; endothermic is the opposite, q > 0 (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **specific heat** — heat needed to warm 1 g of a substance by 1 K; 4.184 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹ for water (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **calorimeter** — an insulated container in which heat from a process is measured through a temperature change (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **enthalpy change (ΔH)** — the heat exchanged at constant pressure, in kJ per mole of reaction as written (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **state function** — a property that depends only on the present state, not on the path taken to reach it, like enthalpy, entropy or altitude (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **Hess's law** — the enthalpy change of a reaction equals the sum of the enthalpy changes of any steps that add up to it (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **standard enthalpy of formation (ΔfH°)** — ΔH for making 1 mol of a compound from its elements in their standard states; zero for the elements themselves (lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md)
- **spontaneous process** — one that can proceed on its own once started, without outside work; says nothing about speed (lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md)
- **entropy (S)** — a measure of the number of microscopic arrangements consistent with a state; rises when gases form, solids melt or dissolve, or temperature rises (lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md)
- **second law of thermodynamics** — in a spontaneous process the total entropy of system plus surroundings increases (lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md)
- **Gibbs free energy (G)** — G = H − TS; at constant T and p a process is spontaneous when ΔG < 0 (lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md)
- **crossover temperature** — T* = ΔH/ΔS, the temperature at which ΔG changes sign when ΔH and ΔS have the same sign (lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md)
- **equilibrium constant (K)** — products over reactants at equilibrium, each concentration or pressure raised to its coefficient; related to ΔG° by ΔG° = −RT ln K (lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md)
- **reaction quotient (Q)** — the same expression as K but with the present amounts; Q < K runs forward, Q > K backward (lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md)
- **dynamic equilibrium** — a state in which forward and backward reactions run at equal rates so the amounts no longer change (lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md)
- **ICE table** — Initial, Change, Equilibrium rows used to solve for an equilibrium composition in terms of one unknown extent x (lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md)
- **Le Chatelier's principle** — a system at equilibrium shifts so as to partly undo a disturbance in concentration, pressure or temperature (lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md)
- **van 't Hoff equation** — ln(K₂/K₁) = −(ΔH°/R)(1/T₂ − 1/T₁), how K changes with temperature (lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md)
- **Brønsted acid and base** — a proton (H⁺) donor and a proton acceptor (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **conjugate pair** — an acid and the base formed when it loses one proton, such as CH₃COOH and CH₃COO⁻ (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **amphiprotic** — able to act as an acid or a base, like water or HCO₃⁻ (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **pH** — −log₁₀[H₃O⁺]; 7 is neutral at 25 °C, and one unit is a factor of ten (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **acid ionisation constant (Ka)** — the equilibrium constant for an acid giving its proton to water; pKa = −log₁₀ Ka (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **buffer** — a solution of a weak acid and its conjugate base that resists changes in pH (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **Henderson–Hasselbalch equation** — pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]) for a buffer (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **indicator** — a weak acid whose acid and base forms have different colours, changing over about pKa ± 1 (lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md)
- **oxidation and reduction** — loss and gain of electrons; oxidation raises the oxidation number, reduction lowers it (lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md)
- **oxidising agent** — the species that is reduced and so takes electrons from another; the reducing agent is oxidised (lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md)
- **oxidation number** — the charge an atom would carry if all its bonds were fully ionic (lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md)
- **half-reaction** — the oxidation or the reduction part of a redox reaction, written with electrons (lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md)
- **composition matrix** — a matrix with one row per element plus a charge row and one column per species; balanced coefficients lie in its null space (lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md)
- **end point** — the point in a titration where the indicator changes colour; ideally equal to the equivalence point (lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md)
- **galvanic cell** — an electrochemical cell in which a spontaneous redox reaction produces an electric current (lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md)
- **anode and cathode** — the electrode where oxidation happens and the electrode where reduction happens (lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md)
- **salt bridge** — an ion-conducting link between two half-cells that keeps each solution electrically neutral (lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md)
- **standard reduction potential (E°)** — the potential of a half-reaction written as a reduction, measured against the standard hydrogen electrode (lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md)
- **Faraday constant (F)** — the charge of one mole of electrons, 96 485.33212 C/mol (lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md)
- **Nernst equation** — E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q, the cell potential at non-standard concentrations (lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md)
- **internal resistance** — the resistance inside a cell, which lowers the voltage delivered when current flows (lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md)
- **heat capacity of a calorimeter** — the heat (J/K) the cup, lid and thermometer take up per kelvin, found by calibration (lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md)
- **titration** — adding a solution of known concentration to a sample until the reaction between them is just complete (lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md)
- **equivalence point** — the point in a titration where the reactants have been added in exactly stoichiometric amounts (lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md)
- **titrant** — the solution of known concentration added in a titration (lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md)
