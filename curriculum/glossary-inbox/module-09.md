- **measurand** — the quantity you intend to measure, for example chloride in a given water in mg/L (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **certified reference material (CRM)** — a material whose content is certified by a national lab or accredited producer, with a stated uncertainty, used to check bias (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **bias** — the mean of many results minus the reference value; a measure of systematic error (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **recovery** — the result found as a percentage of the known or certified amount (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **gross error** — a blunder, such as a copied number or a spill, that makes one result far from the rest (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **trueness** — closeness of the mean of many results to the reference value (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **repeatability** — the scatter of results under the same conditions: one analyst, one instrument, one day (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **reproducibility** — the scatter of results across analysts, instruments, days or labs (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **constant error** — a systematic error of fixed size whatever the sample size; found as the intercept of a sample-size series (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **proportional error** — a systematic error that is a fixed fraction of the result; found from the slope of a sample-size series (lessons/module-09/lesson-01.md)
- **degrees of freedom** — the number of independent pieces of information left for estimating scatter, n − 1 for a standard deviation (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **relative standard deviation (RSD)** — the standard deviation as a percentage of the mean (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **confidence interval** — a range x̄ ± t s/√n that, over many repeated experiments, contains the true mean at the stated rate (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **null hypothesis** — the statement a significance test tries to reject, usually "there is no difference" (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **p-value** — the probability of a test statistic at least as large as observed if the null hypothesis were true (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **type I error** — rejecting a true null hypothesis: a false alarm (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **type II error** — keeping a false null hypothesis: a missed difference (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **F-test** — a test comparing two variances by their ratio (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **Grubbs' test** — a test for whether the single most extreme result of a normal data set is an outlier (lessons/module-09/lesson-02.md)
- **measurement model** — the formula that gives the result from its input quantities (lessons/module-09/lesson-03.md)
- **share of variance** — one input's squared contribution as a fraction of the squared combined uncertainty (lessons/module-09/lesson-03.md)
- **correlation coefficient** — a number from −1 to 1 saying how the errors of two inputs move together (lessons/module-09/lesson-03.md)
- **Monte Carlo propagation** — finding a result's uncertainty by pushing many random draws of the inputs through the model (JCGM 101) (lessons/module-09/lesson-03.md)
- **coverage interval** — an interval containing a stated fraction (often 95 %) of the probable values of a result (lessons/module-09/lesson-03.md)
- **standardisation** — finding a titrant's exact concentration by titrating a weighed primary standard (lessons/module-09/lesson-03.md)
- **sensitivity** — the slope of the calibration function: signal change per unit concentration (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **external standards** — standards prepared and measured separately from the samples to build a calibration line (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **limit of detection (LOD)** — the smallest concentration whose signal can be told apart from the blank, usually 3 s_blank / slope (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **limit of quantification (LOQ)** — the smallest concentration measurable with acceptable precision, usually 10 s_blank / slope (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **linear range** — the concentration range over which the signal is proportional to concentration (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **matrix** — everything in a sample except the analyte (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **standard additions** — calibrating by adding known amounts of analyte to portions of the sample itself, to cancel a matrix effect (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **internal standard** — a different compound added in equal amount to every standard and sample to correct for losses and drift (lessons/module-09/lesson-04.md)
- **equivalence point** — the point in a titration where the titrant added matches the analyte by stoichiometry (lessons/module-09/lesson-05.md)
- **end point** — the observed signal of the equivalence point, such as an indicator's colour change (lessons/module-09/lesson-05.md)
- **titration error** — the difference between the end point and the equivalence point (lessons/module-09/lesson-05.md)
- **charge balance** — the condition that the total positive and negative charge in a solution are equal (lessons/module-09/lesson-05.md)
- **half-equivalence point** — the point where half the acid has been neutralised; there pH = pKa (lessons/module-09/lesson-05.md)
- **buffer capacity** — the amount of strong base (mol/L) needed to raise a buffer's pH by one unit (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
- **gravimetric factor** — the ratio of the analyte's molar mass to the weighed form's, times the stoichiometric ratio (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
- **digestion** — keeping a precipitate warm in its solution so that crystals grow larger and purer (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
- **co-precipitation** — impurities carried down with a precipitate, inside or on its crystals (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
- **peptisation** — a coagulated colloid breaking back into fine particles, for example when washed with pure water (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
- **common-ion effect** — the lower solubility of a salt in a solution that already contains one of its ions (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
- **sRGB** — the standard non-linear colour code used by phone cameras and screens (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
- **pseudo-absorbance** — −log10 of a linearised camera intensity relative to the blank, used like an absorbance (lessons/module-09/lesson-06.md)
