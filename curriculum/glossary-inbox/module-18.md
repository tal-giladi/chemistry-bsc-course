# Glossary inbox — Module 18

- **Slater's rules** — simple rules that estimate the shielding constant S, and so Z_eff = Z − S, from an atom's electron configuration (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **shielding constant** — the part of the nuclear charge cancelled for one electron by the other electrons, S in Z_eff = Z − S (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **d-block contraction** — the extra effective nuclear charge felt by the 4p electrons of Ga to Kr because the ten 3d electrons shield them poorly (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **electron affinity** — the energy released when a gas-phase atom gains one electron, X(g) + e⁻ → X⁻(g); positive when the anion is stable (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **first-row anomaly** — the distinct chemistry of Li to F (strong π bonds, few neighbours) caused by their small size and compact 2p orbitals (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **diagonal relationship** — the chemical similarity of Li and Mg, Be and Al, B and Si, because a step right and a step down have opposite effects on size and Z_eff (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **inert-pair effect** — the reluctance of the ns² electrons of heavy p-block elements (Tl, Pb, Bi) to bond, which makes the oxidation state two below the group maximum the stable one (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **amphoteric** — able to react both as an acid and as a base, as Al₂O₃ and ZnO do (lessons/module-18/lesson-01.md)
- **coordination number** — the number of nearest neighbours (atoms, ions or donor atoms) around a given atom or ion (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **cubic close packing (ccp)** — stacking of identical spheres in ABC layers, 74 % filled, coordination number 12; the same lattice as face-centred cubic (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **unit cell** — the smallest box that, repeated in three directions, builds a crystal; its edge is the lattice constant (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **radius ratio** — r₊/r₋ of an ionic solid, used as a rough guide to the cation's coordination number (4, 6 or 8) (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **lattice enthalpy** — the enthalpy needed to separate one mole of an ionic solid into its gas-phase ions (positive in this course) (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **Born–Haber cycle** — Hess's-law cycle from the elements to the gas-phase ions that gives a lattice enthalpy from measured formation, atomisation, ionisation and electron-affinity data (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **Kapustinskii equation** — estimate of a lattice energy from the number of ions, their charges and the sum of their radii, with no crystal structure needed (lessons/module-18/lesson-02.md)
- **ligand** — a molecule or ion that donates an electron pair to a central metal in a coordination compound (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **donor atom** — the atom of a ligand that bonds to the metal, such as N in NH₃ (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **denticity** — the number of donor atoms one ligand uses: monodentate, bidentate, ..., hexadentate (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **chelate** — a complex in which one ligand binds the metal through two or more donor atoms, forming a ring (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **coordination sphere** — the central metal and the ligands bound to it, written in square brackets (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **d-electron count** — the number of d electrons of a transition-metal ion: group number minus oxidation state (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **linkage isomer** — an isomer in which an ambidentate ligand (SCN⁻, NO₂⁻) binds through a different donor atom (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **fac/mer isomers** — the two arrangements of MA₃B₃: three A on one face of the octahedron (fac) or around a meridian (mer) (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **chelate effect** — the much larger formation constant of a chelate than of the comparable complex with monodentate ligands, mainly an entropy effect (lessons/module-18/lesson-03.md)
- **crystal field theory** — model in which ligands are point charges that raise the energy of the d orbitals pointing at them, splitting the d set (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **octahedral splitting (Δo)** — the energy gap between the e_g and t₂g sets of d orbitals in an octahedral complex (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **t₂g and e_g** — the lower set of three (dxy, dxz, dyz) and the upper set of two (dz², dx²−y²) d orbitals in an octahedral field (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **crystal-field stabilisation energy (CFSE)** — the energy gained by the d electrons from the splitting, relative to the unsplit average: (−0.4 n_t2g + 0.6 n_eg) Δo (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **pairing energy (P)** — the extra energy needed to put a second electron into an occupied orbital; low spin results when Δo > P (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **high spin / low spin** — the two ways of filling d⁴–d⁷ octahedral ions: most unpaired electrons (weak field) or fewest (strong field) (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **spectrochemical series** — ligands ordered by the splitting they cause, from I⁻ (weak) to CN⁻ and CO (strong) (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **nephelauxetic effect** — the reduced electron–electron repulsion of d electrons in a complex compared with the free ion, because they spread onto the ligands (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **ligand field theory** — molecular-orbital treatment of metal–ligand bonding that explains the splitting, including π donation and π back-bonding (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **π acceptor** — a ligand such as CO or CN⁻ with empty π* orbitals that take electron density from the metal's t₂g orbitals, increasing Δ (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **Jahn–Teller distortion** — the distortion of a complex with an unevenly filled degenerate set (high-spin d⁴, d⁹) that lowers its energy, as in Cu(II) (lessons/module-18/lesson-04.md)
- **complementary colour** — the colour seen when a band of visible light is absorbed from white light, opposite the absorbed colour on the colour wheel (lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md)
- **Laporte rule** — in a centrosymmetric molecule, electric-dipole transitions between states of the same parity (such as d–d) are forbidden; they appear only weakly (lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md)
- **charge-transfer band** — an intense absorption in which an electron moves between ligand and metal orbitals, as in permanganate (lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md)
- **molar absorption coefficient** — ε in the Beer–Lambert law, absorbance per unit concentration and path length (L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹), a measure of band intensity (lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md)
- **paramagnetic** — attracted into a magnetic field because of unpaired electrons; diamagnetic substances, with all electrons paired, are weakly repelled (lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md)
- **spin-only magnetic moment** — μ = √(n(n+2)) Bohr magnetons for n unpaired electrons, a good estimate for most first-row complexes (lessons/module-18/lesson-05.md)
