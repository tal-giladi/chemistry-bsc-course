- **diffusion coefficient** — D, how fast a substance spreads by random motion; the typical time to move a distance x is x²/2D (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **amphiphilic** — having a water-loving (polar) part and a water-fearing (nonpolar) part, like phospholipids and soap (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **lipid bilayer** — two sheets of phospholipids, tails inside and heads facing water, that form every cell membrane (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **hydrophobic effect** — the tendency of nonpolar groups to cluster in water, driven mainly by the entropy gained by the water released from around them (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **osmosis** — net movement of water through a membrane towards the side with more dissolved particles (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **osmolarity** — total concentration of dissolved particles, i·c, in osmol/L (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **osmotic pressure** — the pressure that would stop osmosis, Π = icRT (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **isotonic** — having the same osmolarity as the inside of a cell, so there is no net water flow; hypotonic is lower, hypertonic higher (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **Nernst potential** — the membrane voltage at which an ion is in equilibrium across the membrane, E = (RT/zF) ln(c_out/c_in) (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **organelle** — a membrane-bounded compartment inside a eukaryotic cell, such as the nucleus or a mitochondrion (lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md)
- **amino acid** — a molecule with an amino group, a carboxyl group and a side chain on one carbon; 20 kinds build proteins (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **peptide bond** — the planar amide bond joining two amino acids, formed by condensation (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **isoelectric point** — pI, the pH at which a molecule's net charge is zero (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **α-helix** — a protein secondary structure in which the C=O of residue i hydrogen-bonds to the N–H of residue i+4, 3.6 residues per turn (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **β-sheet** — a protein secondary structure of extended strands hydrogen-bonded side by side (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **PDB** — Protein Data Bank, the archive of 3D structures of proteins and nucleic acids, released as CC0 (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **UniProt** — the database of protein sequences and functions (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **resolution (crystallography)** — the smallest detail resolved by the diffraction data, in Å; lower is better (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **DSSP** — the standard method for assigning protein secondary structure from hydrogen-bond energies (lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md)
- **nucleotide** — a base, a five-carbon sugar and a phosphate; the monomer of DNA and RNA (lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)
- **phosphodiester bond** — the phosphate link between the 3' carbon of one sugar and the 5' carbon of the next in a nucleic acid (lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)
- **base pair** — A with T (two hydrogen bonds) or G with C (three) across the two strands of DNA (lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)
- **reverse complement** — the sequence of the partner DNA strand written 5' to 3': complement each base and reverse the order (lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)
- **melting temperature** — Tm, the temperature at which half of a DNA duplex has separated into single strands (lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)
- **codon** — three bases of mRNA that specify one amino acid or a stop (lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)
- **point mutation** — a change of one base; silent, missense or nonsense depending on the new codon (lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md)
- **ATP** — adenosine triphosphate, the cell's energy currency; hydrolysis to ADP and phosphate releases about −30.5 kJ/mol standard, about −50 to −57 kJ/mol in cells (lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md)
- **standard biochemical free energy** — ΔG°', the free energy change at pH 7 with all other species at 1 mol/L (lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md)
- **coupling** — linking an unfavourable process to a favourable one (usually ATP hydrolysis) so the total ΔG is negative (lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md)
- **fermentation** — ATP production by glycolysis alone, regenerating NAD⁺ by making ethanol and CO₂ (yeast) or lactate; 2 ATP per glucose (lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md)
- **respiration (cellular)** — complete oxidation of food to CO₂ and water with O₂, making about 30 ATP per glucose (lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md)
