- **force constant** — k in F = −kx, the force per metre of stretch of a spring or bond, in N/m; measured statically from the stretch or dynamically from the frequency (lessons/module-14/lesson-01.md)
- **effective spring mass** — the part of a spring's own mass that moves with the load, about one third of it for a uniform spring (lessons/module-14/lesson-01.md)
- **phyphox** — a free phone app from RWTH Aachen University that records the phone's sensors and exports the data (lessons/module-14/lesson-01.md)
- **autocorrelation** — how well a signal matches a time-shifted copy of itself; its first peak after zero shift gives the period (lessons/module-14/lesson-01.md)
- **ringdown** — the decaying oscillation of a system left to itself after a push (lessons/module-14/lesson-02.md)
- **damping rate** — γ, the rate at which the amplitude envelope e^(−γt) of a damped oscillation decays, in s⁻¹ (lessons/module-14/lesson-02.md)
- **logarithmic decrement** — δ = ln(A_n/A_(n+1)), the drop in the logarithm of the amplitude from one cycle to the next; Q ≈ π/δ (lessons/module-14/lesson-02.md)
- **quality factor (Q)** — ω₀/(2γ) = f₀/Δf, a measure of how many cycles an oscillator rings and how sharp its resonance is (lessons/module-14/lesson-02.md)
- **anharmonicity** — departure of a restoring force from F = −kx, which makes the frequency depend on amplitude and the vibrational levels of a bond crowd together (lessons/module-14/lesson-02.md)
- **normal mode** — a motion of a coupled system in which every part moves at the same frequency in a fixed pattern; any motion is a sum of normal modes (lessons/module-14/lesson-03.md)
- **force-constant matrix** — K, the matrix whose element K_ij is minus the force on mass i when mass j alone moves by unit distance (lessons/module-14/lesson-03.md)
- **beats** — the slow rise and fall of amplitude when two close frequencies add; in coupled oscillators it moves energy back and forth (lessons/module-14/lesson-03.md)
- **degenerate modes** — two or more normal modes with the same frequency, such as the two bends of CO2 (lessons/module-14/lesson-04.md)
- **valence force field** — a model of molecular vibrations built from bond-stretch and angle-bend force constants plus interaction terms (lessons/module-14/lesson-04.md)
- **IR activity** — a vibration absorbs infrared light only if it changes the molecule's dipole moment (lessons/module-14/lesson-04.md)
- **Fermi resonance** — mixing of two vibrational states of nearly equal energy and the same symmetry, such as ν₁ and 2ν₂ of CO2 (lessons/module-14/lesson-04.md)
- **sample rate** — f_s, the number of readings per second taken by a sensor or converter (lessons/module-14/lesson-05.md)
- **Nyquist frequency** — f_s/2, the highest frequency a sampled record can represent (lessons/module-14/lesson-05.md)
- **aliasing** — a frequency above the Nyquist limit appearing at a false lower frequency |f − m f_s| (lessons/module-14/lesson-05.md)
- **frequency resolution** — Δf = 1/T, the spacing of a discrete Fourier transform, set by the record length T (lessons/module-14/lesson-05.md)
- **spectral leakage** — side lobes around a peak caused by the abrupt start and end of a finite record (lessons/module-14/lesson-05.md)
- **window function** — a taper such as the Hann window applied to a record before the FFT to suppress leakage, at the cost of wider peaks (lessons/module-14/lesson-05.md)
- **impedance** — Z, the AC generalisation of resistance; R for a resistor, jωL for an inductor, 1/(jωC) for a capacitor (lessons/module-14/lesson-06.md)
- **resonance frequency (RLC)** — f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)), where the reactances of L and C cancel (lessons/module-14/lesson-06.md)
- **half-power bandwidth** — the frequency range over which the response stays above 1/√2 of its peak; it equals f₀/Q (lessons/module-14/lesson-06.md)
