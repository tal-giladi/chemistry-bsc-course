- **power law** — a relationship y = a x^n with a fixed exponent n; straight on log–log axes with slope n (lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md)
- **exponential decay** — a quantity that falls by the same factor in every equal time step, y = y0 e^(−kt) (lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md)
- **half-life** — the time for a decaying quantity to halve; for first-order decay t1/2 = ln 2 / k (lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md)
- **natural logarithm** — the logarithm to base e, written ln; ln x ≈ 2.303 log10 x (lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md)
- **Arrhenius equation** — k = A e^(−Ea/RT), the temperature dependence of a rate constant; ln k against 1/T is a straight line with slope −Ea/R (lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md)
- **activation energy** — the energy barrier Ea in the Arrhenius equation, in J/mol (lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md)
- **derivative** — the slope of the tangent to a curve at a point; the instantaneous rate of change (lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md)
- **rate of reaction** — minus the derivative of a reactant's concentration with respect to time, a positive number in mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹ (lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md)
- **chain rule** — the derivative of f(g(x)) is f′(g(x)) times g′(x) (lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md)
- **central difference** — the derivative estimate (y[i+1] − y[i−1]) / (2h); error proportional to h² (lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md)
- **integral** — accumulated change; the area under a curve between two limits (lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md)
- **net change theorem** — integrating a rate of change over an interval gives the total change of the quantity (lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md)
- **trapezoid rule** — numerical integration by joining neighbouring data points with straight lines and adding the trapezoid areas (lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md)
- **Simpson's rule** — numerical integration that fits parabolas through groups of three points; much more accurate than trapezoids on smooth data (lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md)
- **Shomate equation** — the polynomial NIST uses for heat capacity, Cp = A + Bt + Ct² + Dt³ + E/t² with t = T/1000 K (lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md)
- **Taylor series** — a function written near a point as a polynomial built from its derivatives at that point (lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md)
- **small-x approximation** — dropping x next to the initial concentration in an equilibrium expression; acceptable when x is under 5 % of it (lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md)
- **harmonic approximation** — treating a potential near its minimum as a parabola, so the motion is that of a spring (lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md)
- **partial derivative** — the derivative with respect to one variable while all others are held constant, written with ∂ and the fixed variables as subscripts (lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md)
- **total differential** — the sum of each partial derivative times the small change in its variable; the first-order change of a multi-variable function (lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md)
- **adding in quadrature** — combining independent uncertainties as the square root of the sum of their squares (lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md)
- **Newton's law of cooling** — dT/dt = −k (T − T_env): an object cools at a rate proportional to its temperature excess over the surroundings (lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md)
