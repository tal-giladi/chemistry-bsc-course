- **electron ionisation (EI)** — ionisation by a 70 eV electron beam that knocks one electron out of a gas-phase molecule and often breaks it into fragments (lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md)
- **molecular ion** — the radical cation M⁺· formed when a molecule loses one electron; its m/z gives the molecular mass (lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md)
- **base peak** — the tallest peak in a mass spectrum, set to 100 %; often a fragment, not the molecular ion (lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md)
- **nitrogen rule** — a molecule with an odd number of nitrogen atoms has an odd nominal mass (lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md)
- **degrees of unsaturation (DBE)** — the number of rings plus π bonds, (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2 (lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md)
- **alpha-cleavage** — breaking of the bond next to the carbon that carries O or N in a mass spectrometer, giving an ion stabilised by the heteroatom (lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md)
- **McLafferty rearrangement** — transfer of a γ-hydrogen to a carbonyl oxygen through a six-membered ring, with loss of an alkene, giving an even-mass ion (lessons/module-17/lesson-01.md)
- **wavenumber** — the number of waves per centimetre, in cm⁻¹; proportional to photon energy and used as the x-axis of IR spectra (lessons/module-17/lesson-02.md)
- **reduced mass** — m₁m₂/(m₁ + m₂), the effective mass of a two-atom vibration (lessons/module-17/lesson-02.md)
- **fingerprint region** — the part of an IR spectrum below about 1500 cm⁻¹, with many bands that together are unique to a compound (lessons/module-17/lesson-02.md)
- **chemical shift** — the position of an NMR signal relative to TMS, in parts per million of the spectrometer frequency (lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md)
- **shielding** — reduction of the magnetic field at a nucleus by its surrounding electrons, which moves its signal upfield (lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md)
- **chemically equivalent** — describes nuclei that symmetry or fast rotation can interchange; they give one NMR signal (lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md)
- **integration** — the area of an NMR signal, proportional to the number of nuclei that produce it (lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md)
- **spin–spin coupling** — splitting of an NMR signal by the magnetic orientations of nearby nuclei, transmitted through bonds (lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md)
- **coupling constant (J)** — the spacing in Hz between the lines of a multiplet; coupled partners share the same J (lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md)
- **second-order spectrum** — an NMR spectrum in which the shift difference in Hz is not much larger than J, so lines lean towards each other and the n+1 rule fails (lessons/module-17/lesson-03.md)
- **broadband decoupling** — irradiation of all protons while a ¹³C spectrum is recorded, which collapses each carbon signal to one line (lessons/module-17/lesson-04.md)
- **DEPT** — NMR experiments that show CH only (DEPT-90) or CH and CH₃ up and CH₂ down (DEPT-135), with quaternary carbons absent (lessons/module-17/lesson-04.md)
- **COSY** — a 2D NMR experiment that shows which protons are coupled to each other (lessons/module-17/lesson-04.md)
- **HSQC** — a 2D NMR experiment that shows which proton is bonded directly to which carbon (lessons/module-17/lesson-04.md)
- **HMBC** — a 2D NMR experiment that shows protons and carbons two or three bonds apart, across heteroatoms and quaternary carbons (lessons/module-17/lesson-04.md)
