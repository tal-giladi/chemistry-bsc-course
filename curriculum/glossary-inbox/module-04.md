- **force** — a push or pull that changes an object's momentum, F = ma = dp/dt, measured in newtons (lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md)
- **work** — energy transferred by a force acting over a distance, the integral of F dx, in joules (lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md)
- **kinetic energy** — energy of motion, ½mv² (lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md)
- **potential energy** — energy stored by position in a conservative force field; the force is minus its slope, F = −dU/dx (lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md)
- **momentum** — mass times velocity, p = mv; conserved in every collision (lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md)
- **Morse potential** — a model bond-energy curve U(r) = De(1 − e^(−a(r − re)))², with well depth De and bond length re (lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md)
- **electronvolt** — the energy an electron gains moving through 1 V, 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, equal to 96.485 kJ/mol (lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md)
- **simple harmonic motion** — oscillation under a restoring force proportional to displacement, at angular frequency ω = √(k/m) (lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md)
- **reduced mass** — μ = m1m2/(m1 + m2), the effective mass of a vibrating two-body system (lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md)
- **force constant** — the stiffness k of a spring or bond, in N/m; for a bond k = μ(2πcν̃)² (lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md)
- **wavenumber** — the number of waves per unit length, ν̃ = 1/λ, usually in cm⁻¹; proportional to photon energy (lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md)
- **quality factor** — Q, a measure of how slowly an oscillator loses energy; large Q means weak damping and a sharp resonance (lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md)
- **Coulomb's law** — the force between point charges, F = q1q2/(4πε0 r²) (lessons/module-04/lesson-03.md)
- **electric field** — force per unit positive test charge, in V/m (lessons/module-04/lesson-03.md)
- **electric potential** — potential energy per unit charge, in volts; a voltage is a difference of potential (lessons/module-04/lesson-03.md)
- **relative permittivity** — εr, the factor by which a medium weakens electrostatic forces and energies; about 78 for water at 25 °C (lessons/module-04/lesson-03.md)
- **dipole moment** — μ = qd for charges ±q a distance d apart, in debye (1 D = 3.336 × 10⁻³⁰ C m) (lessons/module-04/lesson-03.md)
- **Madelung constant** — the sum of all Coulomb interactions of one ion in a crystal, in units of the nearest-neighbour pair energy; 1.7476 for rock salt (lessons/module-04/lesson-03.md)
- **Ohm's law** — V = IR for a component of constant resistance (lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md)
- **conductivity** — κ = 1/ρ, the inverse of resistivity, in S/m; what a conductivity meter reports for a solution (lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md)
- **Kirchhoff's rules** — current in equals current out at every node; voltages around any closed loop sum to zero (lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md)
- **internal resistance** — the resistance inside a battery that lowers its terminal voltage under load, V = EMF − Ir (lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md)
- **Faraday constant** — the charge of one mole of electrons, F = N_A e = 96 485.332 C/mol (lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md)
- **photon** — a quantum of light with energy E = hν = hc/λ (lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md)
- **Planck constant** — h = 6.626 070 15 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, exact; links a photon's energy to its frequency (lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md)
- **photoelectric effect** — emission of electrons from a surface by light above a threshold frequency, independent of brightness (lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md)
- **turn-on voltage** — the voltage at which an LED starts to conduct, found by extrapolating the linear part of its I–V curve to zero current; about hc/(eλ) (lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md)
