- **research paper (primary article)** — the first published report of new work, normally peer reviewed (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **peer review** — the check of a manuscript by independent experts before a journal publishes it; reviewers rarely see raw data or repeat the work (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **IMRaD** — the standard structure of a paper: Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **three-pass reading** — reading a paper three times with increasing depth: 5–10 minutes to decide, up to an hour to understand, longer to check (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **five Cs** — the questions after a first pass: category, context, correctness, contributions, clarity (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **Supporting Information (SI)** — material published with a paper outside the main text, such as raw data and extra spectra (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **fitness for purpose** — the property of a method that answers the question asked with adequate sensitivity, precision and cost (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **molecularly imprinted polymer (MIP)** — a polymer formed around a template molecule so that it later binds that molecule selectively (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **hedging** — words that set the strength of a claim, such as "shows", "suggests", "may" (lessons/module-11/lesson-01.md)
- **preprint** — a manuscript posted publicly before peer review, for example on ChemRxiv (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **review article** — a paper that summarises and evaluates many primary papers on a topic (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **evaluated data** — values recommended by experts after comparing all available measurements, with uncertainty and conditions (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **DOI (Digital Object Identifier)** — a permanent identifier for a paper or data set, resolved at doi.org (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **Crossref** — the main registration agency for journal DOIs; its open API gives metadata, licences and update notices (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **retraction** — the formal withdrawal of a published paper because its results cannot be relied on (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **correction (erratum)** — a published notice that fixes an error in a paper without withdrawing it (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **expression of concern** — a notice from a journal that a paper's reliability is under question (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **predatory journal** — a publication that charges fees but does not carry out real peer review (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **citation chasing** — finding related work backward through a paper's references and forward through the papers that cite it (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **FAIR data** — data that are Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable (lessons/module-11/lesson-02.md)
- **reverse outline** — a list of the first sentence of every paragraph, read alone to test whether a text tells its story (lessons/module-11/lesson-03.md)
- **sentence title** — a figure or slide title that states the figure's message as a sentence (lessons/module-11/lesson-03.md)
- **chartjunk** — figure elements that are neither data nor needed to read the data, such as 3D effects or heavy grids (lessons/module-11/lesson-03.md)
- **perceptually uniform colour map** — a colour map in which equal steps in data look like equal steps in colour, such as viridis (lessons/module-11/lesson-03.md)
- **dot plot (strip plot)** — a plot that shows every individual value as a point, often with the group mean and interval (lessons/module-11/lesson-03.md)
