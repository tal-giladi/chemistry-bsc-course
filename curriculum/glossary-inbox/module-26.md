# Glossary inbox — Module 26

- **bulk metals** — the metal ions present in cells at millimolar levels (Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺), used for charge, signalling and phosphate binding (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **trace metals** — metals present at micromolar levels or below, almost all bound to proteins (Fe, Zn, Cu, Mn, Co, Mo, Ni), used for chemistry (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **metalloprotein** — a protein that needs a bound metal ion to fold or to work (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **siderophore** — a small molecule made by bacteria or fungi that binds Fe³⁺ very strongly and carries it into the cell; enterobactin is the strongest known (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **ferritin** — the iron-storage protein, a protein shell around a core of hydrated iron(III) oxide (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **transferrin** — the blood protein that carries two Fe³⁺ ions, one per lobe (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **hard–soft acid–base (HSAB) principle** — hard (small, highly charged) ions prefer hard donors such as O; soft (large, polarisable) ions prefer soft donors such as S (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **Irving–Williams series** — the stability order of high-spin divalent first-row complexes, Mn²⁺ < Fe²⁺ < Co²⁺ < Ni²⁺ < Cu²⁺ > Zn²⁺ (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **donor set** — the list of atoms bound to a metal ion in a site, for example N₃O (lessons/module-26/lesson-01.md)
- **haem (heme)** — protoporphyrin IX with a central iron ion, the O₂-binding and electron-carrying cofactor of globins and cytochromes (lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- **proximal histidine** — the histidine that binds the haem iron from below in haemoglobin and myoglobin (His F8) (lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- **distal histidine** — the histidine on the O₂ side of the haem that does not bind the iron but hydrogen-bonds to bound O₂ (His E7) (lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- **met-haemoglobin** — haemoglobin with Fe(III) haem, which cannot carry O₂ (lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- **superoxo** — bound O₂ that has received about one electron (O₂•⁻ character, O–O bond order about 1.5, stretch near 1100 cm⁻¹) (lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- **Soret band** — the very intense porphyrin π→π* absorption near 400–430 nm; the weaker Q bands lie near 540–580 nm (lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- **entatic state** — a metal site held by the protein in a strained geometry poised for its function, as in blue-copper proteins (lessons/module-26/lesson-02.md)
- **fractional saturation** — the fraction of binding sites that hold a ligand, S, between 0 and 1 (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **P50** — the O₂ partial pressure at which a carrier is half saturated; a measure of affinity (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **cooperativity** — binding at one site changes the affinity of the other sites; positive when it raises it (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **Hill equation** — S = pⁿ/(P50ⁿ + pⁿ), an empirical fit of cooperative binding (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **Hill coefficient** — the exponent n of the Hill equation and the slope of the Hill plot; a measure of cooperativity, not the number of sites (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **Hill plot** — log[S/(1−S)] against log p; slope 1 for independent sites (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **T state and R state** — the low-affinity (tense) and high-affinity (relaxed) quaternary structures of haemoglobin in the two-state model (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **MWC (two-state) model** — the Monod–Wyman–Changeux model of cooperativity: the whole molecule switches between T and R (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **allosteric effector** — a molecule that binds away from the active site and changes its affinity or activity (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **Bohr effect** — the fall in haemoglobin's O₂ affinity when pH falls or CO₂ rises (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG)** — a red-cell metabolite that binds T-state haemoglobin and lowers its O₂ affinity (lessons/module-26/lesson-03.md)
- **Lewis-acid catalysis** — catalysis by a metal ion that accepts electron density, for example by lowering the pKa of bound water (lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)
- **carbonic anhydrase** — the zinc enzyme that hydrates CO₂ to bicarbonate, with a turnover near 10⁶ s⁻¹ (lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)
- **reorganisation energy** — λ, the energy needed to distort reactants and surroundings to the product geometry without transferring the electron (lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)
- **Marcus inverted region** — the regime where electron transfer slows as the driving force exceeds λ (lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)
- **tunnelling decay constant** — β, the exponential fall of electron-transfer rate with distance, about 1.1 Å⁻¹ in proteins (lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)
- **respiratory chain** — the series of membrane complexes that pass electrons from NADH to O₂ and pump protons (lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)
- **cytochrome c oxidase** — complex IV, the haem–copper enzyme that reduces O₂ to water with four electrons and four protons (lessons/module-26/lesson-04.md)
