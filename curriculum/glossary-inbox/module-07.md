- **nuclide** — an atom with a given number of protons and neutrons, written like ³⁷Cl (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **atomic number** — Z, the number of protons in the nucleus; it fixes the element (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **mass number** — A, the number of protons plus neutrons in a nucleus (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **isotopes** — atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **abundance** — the mole fraction of an element's atoms that belong to one isotope (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **atomic weight** — the abundance-weighted mean relative atomic mass of an element's isotopes, as tabulated by IUPAC (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **dalton** — Da, the atomic mass unit, one twelfth of the mass of a ¹²C atom (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **mole** — the SI unit of amount of substance; one mole contains exactly 6.022 140 76 × 10²³ specified entities (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **molar mass** — M, the mass of one mole of a substance, in g/mol (lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md)
- **orbital** — a region where an electron with a given set of quantum numbers is likely to be found; it holds at most two electrons (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **aufbau principle** — electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy in the ground state (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **Hund's rule** — electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly, with parallel spins, before they pair (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **Pauli exclusion principle** — no two electrons in an atom can have the same four quantum numbers (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **valence electrons** — the electrons in the outermost shell, which take part in bonding (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **shielding** — the reduction of the nuclear attraction felt by an electron because of the electrons between it and the nucleus (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **effective nuclear charge** — Zeff, the net positive charge an electron feels after shielding (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **ionisation energy** — the energy needed to remove an electron from a gaseous atom or ion (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **electronegativity** — the tendency of an atom in a molecule to attract the shared electrons of a bond (lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md)
- **ionic bond** — the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions formed by electron transfer (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **covalent bond** — a bond formed by a shared pair of electrons between two atoms (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **Lewis structure** — a drawing of a molecule's valence electrons as bonding pairs and lone pairs (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **octet rule** — main-group atoms tend to bond until they have eight valence electrons (two for hydrogen) (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **formal charge** — the charge an atom would have if every bond's electrons were shared equally: valence electrons minus lone-pair electrons minus bonds (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **resonance hybrid** — the real structure of a molecule described by two or more Lewis structures, an average of them, not a flip between them (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **bond order** — the number of shared electron pairs between two atoms, possibly fractional in a resonance hybrid (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **lattice energy** — the energy released when gaseous ions come together to form one mole of an ionic solid (lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md)
- **VSEPR** — valence shell electron pair repulsion: electron domains around a central atom arrange themselves as far apart as possible (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **electron domain** — a region of electron density around a central atom: a single, double or triple bond or a lone pair, each counted once (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **molecular shape** — the arrangement of the atoms (not the lone pairs) around a central atom, such as bent or trigonal pyramidal (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **dipole moment** — μ, the vector sum of a molecule's bond dipoles, in debye (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **intermolecular forces** — attractions between molecules: dispersion, dipole–dipole, hydrogen bonds and ion–dipole (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **dispersion forces** — attractions between temporary, induced dipoles; present in all molecules and growing with polarisability (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **hydrogen bond** — a strong attraction between H bonded to N, O or F and a lone pair on another N, O or F (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **polarisability** — how easily an electron cloud is distorted by an electric field (lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md)
- **stoichiometric coefficient** — the number in front of a formula in a balanced equation; it gives mole ratios (lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md)
- **limiting reagent** — the reactant that runs out first and fixes the amount of product; it has the smallest amount divided by coefficient (lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md)
- **theoretical yield** — the amount of product the limiting reagent allows if the reaction goes exactly as written (lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md)
- **percent yield** — actual yield divided by theoretical yield, times 100 % (lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md)
- **ideal gas** — a model gas of point molecules with no attractions, obeying PV = nRT (lessons/module-07/lesson-06.md)
- **partial pressure** — the pressure a gas in a mixture would have alone in the same volume; p_i = x_i P (lessons/module-07/lesson-06.md)
- **mole fraction** — x_i, the amount of one component divided by the total amount (lessons/module-07/lesson-06.md)
- **compressibility factor** — Z = PVm/RT; 1 for an ideal gas, below 1 when attractions dominate, above 1 when molecular size dominates (lessons/module-07/lesson-06.md)
- **van der Waals equation** — (P + a/Vm²)(Vm − b) = RT, the ideal gas law corrected for attractions (a) and excluded volume (b) (lessons/module-07/lesson-06.md)
- **weighing by difference** — weighing a container before and after delivering a sample, so the mass delivered is the difference and the scale offset cancels (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
- **readability** — the smallest step a balance or scale displays (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
- **Class A glassware** — volumetric glassware made to the tightest tolerance; Class B is allowed twice the tolerance (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
- **meniscus** — the curved surface of a liquid in a narrow tube; for clear liquids the bottom of the curve is read (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
- **parallax error** — a reading error caused by viewing a scale or mark from above or below instead of at eye level (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
- **standard solution** — a solution whose concentration is known accurately enough to calibrate other measurements (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
- **serial dilution** — a series of repeated dilution steps, each made from the previous solution (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
- **uncertainty budget** — a table of every input's contribution to the uncertainty of a result, showing which one dominates (lessons/module-07/lesson-07.md)
