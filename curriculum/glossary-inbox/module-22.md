# Glossary inbox — Module 22

- **rate of reaction** — the rate of change of a concentration divided by its stoichiometric coefficient, v = (1/ν) d[i]/dt, the same for every species (lessons/module-22/lesson-01.md)
- **rate law** — the measured relation between rate and concentrations, often v = k[A]^α[B]^β (lessons/module-22/lesson-01.md)
- **order of reaction** — the exponent of a concentration in a rate law; found by experiment and not necessarily a whole number (lessons/module-22/lesson-01.md)
- **molecularity** — the number of particles taking part in one elementary step: uni-, bi- or termolecular (lessons/module-22/lesson-01.md)
- **elementary reaction** — a single step that happens as written; its orders equal its coefficients (lessons/module-22/lesson-01.md)
- **pseudo-first-order conditions** — one reactant in large excess, so a second-order reaction decays as first order with k' = k[B]0 (lessons/module-22/lesson-01.md)
- **lifetime** — the time for a first-order decay to fall to 1/e, τ = 1/k; the half-life is τ ln 2 (lessons/module-22/lesson-01.md)
- **relaxation time** — the time constant of the return to equilibrium after a disturbance; τ = 1/(kf + kr) for A ⇌ B (lessons/module-22/lesson-02.md)
- **stopped flow** — a method that mixes two solutions in about a millisecond and follows the reaction optically (lessons/module-22/lesson-02.md)
- **flash photolysis** — a method that creates a reactive species with a short light pulse and follows its decay (lessons/module-22/lesson-02.md)
- **initial-rate method** — finding an order from the slope of ln v0 against ln [A]0 for several starting concentrations (lessons/module-22/lesson-02.md)
- **mechanism** — the sequence of elementary steps by which a reaction proceeds (lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md)
- **rate-determining step** — a slow step that sets the overall rate when the steps after it are fast (lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md)
- **pre-equilibrium** — a fast reversible step before the slow step, treated with its equilibrium constant (lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md)
- **steady-state approximation** — setting d[I]/dt ≈ 0 for a reactive intermediate that is destroyed much faster than it forms (lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md)
- **Lindemann mechanism** — collisional activation of a molecule followed by its decay; explains first order at high pressure and second order at low pressure (lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md)
- **falloff** — the pressure range in which a unimolecular rate constant changes from its high-pressure to its low-pressure limit (lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md)
- **chain reaction** — a mechanism in which reactive carriers are regenerated by propagation steps, with initiation and termination steps (lessons/module-22/lesson-03.md)
- **activation energy** — Ea = RT² d ln k/dT; the slope of an Arrhenius plot times −R (lessons/module-22/lesson-04.md)
- **pre-exponential factor** — A in k = A e^(−Ea/RT), with the units of k (lessons/module-22/lesson-04.md)
- **steric factor** — the fraction of energetic collisions with a suitable orientation, P in collision theory (lessons/module-22/lesson-04.md)
- **transition state** — the configuration at the top of the lowest pass between reactants and products, also called the activated complex (lessons/module-22/lesson-04.md)
- **Eyring equation** — k = (kBT/h) exp(Δ‡S/R) exp(−Δ‡H/RT), from transition-state theory (lessons/module-22/lesson-04.md)
- **entropy of activation** — Δ‡S, positive for loose transition states (bond fission), negative for tight ones (association, ring closure) (lessons/module-22/lesson-04.md)
- **catalyst** — a substance that speeds a reaction by a new mechanism and is regenerated, leaving the equilibrium constant unchanged (lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md)
- **turnover frequency** — catalytic cycles per second per active site (lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md)
- **Langmuir–Hinshelwood mechanism** — a surface reaction between two adsorbed reactants; its rate passes through a maximum as one pressure rises (lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md)
- **Michaelis–Menten equation** — v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S]) for an enzyme with a single substrate (lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md)
- **Michaelis constant** — Km = (k−1 + kcat)/k1, the substrate concentration at which v = Vmax/2 (lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md)
- **specificity constant** — kcat/Km, the second-order rate constant of free enzyme with substrate at low substrate concentration (lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md)
- **product inhibition** — slowing of an enzyme by products that bind to it, often competitively (lessons/module-22/lesson-05.md)
