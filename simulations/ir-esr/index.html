How to use this, and why

The procedure
  1. Evacuate the 10 cm cell and record the background: the spectrometer's own response, the windows and water vapour are divided out.
  2. Fill with 50–100 mbar of HCl in a fume hood. Low pressure keeps the lines narrow (pressure broadening) and the strong lines below saturation.
  3. Choose the resolution: 0.5 cm⁻¹ separates the 2.1 cm⁻¹ isotope doublets; 0.1 cm⁻¹ gives positions to better than 0.01 cm⁻¹. Average 16–64 scans.
  4. Ratio to the background and convert to absorbance A = −log₁₀(I/I₀).
  5. For the overtone, about 50 times weaker, use a longer path (multi-pass cell) or a higher pressure; then pump out through a base trap.
Why each step is done
  • Each line is ν̃(m) = ν̃₀ + (B′ + B″)m + (B′ − B″)m² − 2(D′ + D″)m³ with m = J + 1 (R) or −J (P). The cubic term is the centrifugal distortion: about 3 cm⁻¹ at R(10), many times the line width, so a high-resolution fit needs it.
  • Heights follow |m| ν̃ e−hcF₀(J″)/kBT; ln[A/(|m|ν̃)] against F₀(J″) is a line of slope −hc/kBT. If the numbering were wrong by one J, the plot would split by branch.
  • H³⁷Cl has ωe scaled by ρ = 0.999243 and B by ρ²: a free check of the whole analysis.
  • Fundamental and overtone centres give ωexe = (2ν̃₀₁ − ν̃₀₂)/2 and ωe = ν̃₀₁ + 2ωexe.
Safety reasoning

HCl gas is H280 (gas under pressure), H314 (severe burns and eye damage) and H331 (toxic if inhaled) (PubChem CID 313). It is handled from a small cylinder on a vacuum line in a fume hood, with goggles and gloves, and pumped out through a base trap, never vented. With a trace of moisture it etches the water-soluble KBr or NaCl windows, so the cell is dried first. This is a lab-only experiment.

What can go wrong
  • Saturation: at high pressure or a long path the strongest lines absorb everything at their centre; the tops go flat and noisy, the lines look broader and the doublets merge even at high resolution. Keep peak absorbance below about 1.
  • Coarse resolution blurs each doublet into one peak and pulls every position towards the ³⁷Cl partner: constants come out about 1 % off, as in lesson 20.3.
  • Numbering from the wrong gap: the fit residuals jump between the branches and the band centre moves by about 2B. Try the check box.
  • Too few scans or too short a path for the overtone: the lines vanish into the noise.
  • A leaky cell lets in air and water; water lines appear between the HCl lines (not modelled here).

Find the lines and fit g and a

Each line centre is the zero crossing between a maximum and the next minimum. A straight line of centre against line index gives a (slope) and B₀ (intercept), so g = hν/(μBB₀).

How to use this, and why

The procedure
  1. Make the radical where it lives long enough: CH₃• by photolysing CH₃I frozen at 77 K or irradiating cold hydrocarbons; H atoms trapped in irradiated frozen acids or quartz.
  2. Tune the cavity to the microwave frequency and set the power low, below saturation.
  3. Set the modulation amplitude below about a third of ΔBpp.
  4. Sweep the field through resonance and record the lock-in signal: the first derivative of the absorption.
  5. Calibrate the field with an NMR gaussmeter and the frequency with a counter, or with a standard such as DPPH (g = 2.0036).
Why each step is done
  • The two spin states differ by only 0.32 cm⁻¹ at 0.34 T, so their populations are almost equal (Boltzmann factor 0.9985 at room temperature): the signal is small and needs field modulation and a lock-in amplifier, which give the derivative.
  • Hyperfine splittings are fixed in field; g differences scale with the frequency. Recording at two bands tells them apart.
  • a measures spin density at the nucleus: the Fermi contact term for s orbitals, spin polarisation and McConnell's aH = Qρ (|Q| ≈ 2.3 mT) for π radicals.
  • When a is not small compared with B₀ (the H atom), use the exact Breit–Rabi levels; the second-order shift is −a²[I(I+1) − mI²]/2B₀.
Safety reasoning

The bridge emits microwaves: never look into an open waveguide. The electromagnet's field attracts steel tools and affects pacemakers. Liquid nitrogen causes frostbite and can displace oxygen in a small room. Radicals are often made with UV lamps or radiation sources, each under its own rules. ESR is a lab-only technique.

What can go wrong
  • Overmodulation (modulation above about ΔBpp/3): more signal at first, then broad, distorted lines whose heights no longer follow 1 : 3 : 3 : 1.
  • Saturation (too much power): the line broadens and the signal falls instead of rising.
  • Reading a centre at a maximum instead of at the zero crossing.
  • Using n + 1 for nuclei with I > ½: deuterium and ¹⁴N need 2nI + 1.
  • Taking the midpoint of the hydrogen doublet as B₀: g comes out 2.0137 instead of 2.0023.
