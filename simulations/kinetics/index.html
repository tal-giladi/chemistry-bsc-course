How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Change one thing at a time: a starting concentration, a temperature or the excess reagent, and keep everything else fixed.
  2. Follow the reaction past at least two half-lives, with readings spread evenly in time.
  3. Find the order: plot c, ln c and 1/c against t and look for the straight one and for random residuals; better, change c₀ and see how the rate or half-life responds.
  4. Use a large excess of one reagent (pseudo-first-order), and measure k′ at two or more excess concentrations to get the order in that reagent.
  5. Measure k at four or more temperatures and fit ln k against 1/T for Ea; fit enzyme rates with the hyperbola directly.
Why each step is done
  • Over a small conversion every order gives a nearly straight line; only a long run or a change of c₀ decides the order.
  • Only first-order half-lives are independent of c₀; second-order half-lives double as c halves.
  • With B in large excess, [B] hardly changes, so k[A][B] behaves like k′[A]; a tenfold excess makes k′ a few per cent off, fiftyfold negligible.
  • The intercept of an Arrhenius plot is far outside the data, so A is always much less certain than Ea.
  • Km is defined by the bend of the curve; substrate concentrations must straddle it.
Safety reasoning
  • Bleach (sodium hypochlorite) burns eyes and skin (GHS H314, H318): glasses and gloves, small amounts, always added to plenty of water.
  • Never mix bleach with acids (chlorine gas) or ammonia (chloramines); work in moving air.
  • Warm water for a temperature run: no hotter than about 50 °C.
What can go wrong
  • Deciding the order from a short run, or from R² alone; a curved residual pattern is the stronger sign.
  • Too little excess reagent: the decay is no longer a single exponential.
  • Ignoring the end offset A∞: the ln plot bends at late times and k′ comes out low.
  • Auto-exposure on the phone: the camera brightens a fading cup and flattens the curve.
  • A warm cup cools during the run, so its temperature, and Ea, are uncertain.
  • Taking Km and Vmax from a Lineweaver–Burk line: the slowest, least certain rates dominate it.
  • Euler's method with a large step: its error is proportional to h, and for first order it oscillates when hk > 2.
Data analysis

Export the data of each tab (CSV) and analyse them in Python in the Module 3, 22, 23 and 29 lab folders: integrated rate laws by linear fits, A → B → C with solve_ivp or the matrix exponential, Arrhenius and Eyring fits with uncertainties from the covariance matrix, A = A₀e−k′t + A∞ with curve_fit, and Michaelis–Menten by nonlinear least squares.
