The spring

Analysis: two ways to measure k

Your readings

#Methodm / kgReadingDerived

How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Weigh the spring alone and write down ms. Weigh every load, including the bag and string.
  2. Static method: hang 50–300 g in steps, read the spring length each time between the same two points, and fit length against mass. k = g / slope.
  3. Dynamic method: hang the phone, pull down 2 cm, release, wait two or three bounces, then measure the period. Repeat three times at each mass.
  4. Fit T² against m with a free intercept. k = 4π² / slope, and intercept / slope = meff ≈ ms/3.
  5. Compare the two values of k using their combined uncertainty uc = √(u₁² + u₂²).
Why each step is done
  • The two methods are independent: one uses gravity and a ruler, the other a clock and no g at all. Agreement tests the oscillator model itself.
  • Timing 10 periods divides your reaction-time error by 10; phyphox finds the period from the autocorrelation of the accelerometer signal and is better still.
  • The spring's own coils move too. A free intercept in the T² fit measures that extra mass instead of hiding it inside a wrong slope.
  • The period does not depend on the amplitude while Hooke's law holds, so the size of the pull does not matter, as long as it is small.
Safety reasoning
  • The real risk at home is dropping the phone: a hook that cannot slip, the phone sealed in a zip bag and tied on, cushions underneath, pulls of 2–3 cm only.
  • Keep faces away from a stretched spring; it can slip off and snap back.
  • Nothing in this experiment uses mains power.
What can go wrong
  • Leaving out the spring's mass: the dynamic k comes out a few percent low and seems to disagree with the static one. Set ms = 30 g to see it.
  • Forcing the T² line through the origin hides meff inside the slope.
  • A spring wound with built-in tension does not stretch at all until a minimum load: the lowest static points sit on a flat line. Drop them.
  • Overstretching past the elastic limit: the spring stays longer and k changes. Try a 2 kg load on a soft spring.
  • Stopwatch timing of a single period is dominated by your reaction time; time ten.
Data analysis

Export the static readings (mass_kg,length_m) and the dynamic ones (mass_kg,trial,period_s), the formats of the Module 14 lab folder, and run lesson01_spring.py on them. The uncertainty in k from a period is twice the relative uncertainty in T, because k ∝ 1/T².

Oscillator

Seed: . Sampling at 100 Hz, like a phone accelerometer.

Recording

Analysis

How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Hang the phone on the spring, pull down about 3 cm, release gently and start recording "acceleration without g".
  2. Let it ring for 20–60 s, stop, export the data as CSV.
  3. Find the peak of each cycle and plot ln(peak) against time. The slope is −γ.
  4. Fit the full damped cosine starting from the peak-method γ and the measured frequency.
  5. Q = ω₀/2γ ≈ π/δ. Repeat with a cardboard disc (more drag) and with elastic bands (anharmonic).
Why each step is done
  • The envelope e−γt is first-order decay, so a semi-log plot of the peaks is a straight line if the damping is linear in velocity. A curved plot tells you the model is wrong.
  • The phone measures acceleration, but for light damping a ≈ −ω₀²x, so the acceleration decays with the same envelope.
  • Small peaks are pushed up by noise (the highest of several noisy samples is biased high), so they flatten the decay and make Q too high. The threshold drops them.
  • Windowed frequencies show anharmonicity: a softening oscillator (β < 0) speeds up as it calms down.
Safety reasoning
  • As for the spring: a hook that cannot slip, the phone in a zip bag tied on, cushions underneath, small pulls.
  • Elastic hair bands can snap: check them for cracks, never stretch past about twice their length, and wear safety glasses.
What can go wrong
  • Using γ where 2γ is needed: the amplitude decays at γ, the energy at 2γ, and Q = ω₀/2γ.
  • Fitting peaks lost in the noise: set the threshold to 0 and watch Q rise.
  • Pulling too far with a card attached: quadratic air drag makes the start of the semi-log plot curve downwards.
  • Calling every frequency drift anharmonicity: check first that the phone is not twisting or swinging sideways.
  • Raising b past 2√(mk): the motion becomes critically damped, then overdamped, and there are no peaks to fit.
Data analysis

The recording exports as Time (s) plus three acceleration columns, like phyphox; the spring axis is column 3, so py lesson02_damping.py my_spring.csv 3 reads it. The peak table gives ln(peak) against t for your own straight-line fit; the slope's standard uncertainty gives u(γ) and so u(Q) = Q u(γ)/γ.

Not trained here: releasing a phone without a jerk or a sideways swing, and spotting a twisting motion by eye.

#ModeTrialt₁₀ / sω² / s⁻²

How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Hang three equal pendulums (lengths equal within 2 mm) and join neighbours with rubber bands, slightly stretched.
  2. Excite each mode by releasing all three bobs in its shape: (1, 1, 1), (1, 0, −1), (1, −2, 1).
  3. Time 10 swings of each mode, three times.
  4. Convert to ω² and fit ω² = g/L + cλ with λ = 0, 1, 3.
  5. Finally release bob 1 alone and note when its swing has moved to the others.
Why each step is done
  • In a normal mode every mass moves at one frequency, so timing it is like timing a single pendulum. Any other start mixes modes and the timing has no single meaning.
  • Eigenvalues are ω², not ω; the shapes are the eigenvectors. With unequal masses you must solve K v = ω² M v, not K v = ω² v.
  • The middle mass is still in mode 2 by symmetry, so changing m₂ cannot change that mode's frequency.
  • Pulling one mass starts all modes at once; their frequencies differ, so they drift out of step and the swing wanders along the chain at the beat frequency.
Safety reasoning
  • Light bobs (bags of rice, small plastic bottles), never glass; a broom handle taped down so it cannot roll; small swings, faces out of the path.
  • Rubber bands under tension can snap: wear safety glasses.
What can go wrong
  • Releasing mode 3 slightly off its shape mixes in modes 1 and 2: the amplitude beats and the timing scatters. Untick "release by hand" to see a perfect release.
  • Forgetting M in the eigenproblem: with a heavy middle mass the frequencies come out wrong.
  • Treating the zero mode of the free chain as an error: it is the whole chain sliding, a translation.
  • Bands too slack or too high on the strings: the coupling c becomes tiny and modes 1 and 2 have almost the same period.
Data analysis

Export the timings as mode,trial,t10_s (the format of coupled_pendulums_example.csv) and run lesson03_modes.py. The fit here uses the same straight line ω² = ω₀² + cλ; g/L gives L to compare with your tape measure.

#Moleculekk₁₂kδν₁ν₂ν₃ν₃/ν₁

How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Start with one force constant (k₁₂ = 0) fitted to ν₃ and see how badly ν₁ comes out.
  2. Add the interaction constant k₁₂ and fit both stretches; fit kδ to the bend.
  3. Keep the force constants and change only the masses (¹³C, ¹⁸O) to predict isotope shifts.
  4. Decide from each mode shape whether the dipole moment changes, and so whether the band appears in the IR spectrum.
Why each step is done
  • With one constant, ν₃/ν₁ = √(1 + 2mO/mC) = 1.915 whatever k is; the measured ratio is 1.762. Only an extra constant can fix that.
  • The potential comes from the electrons and does not depend on the nuclear masses (Born–Oppenheimer), so the same k, k₁₂, kδ must describe every isotopologue.
  • The carbon stands still in ν₁, so ¹³C cannot move ν₁; ¹⁸O moves every mode.
  • With partial charges −q, +2q, −q the dipole change is the charge-weighted sum of the mode shape. Zero means IR inactive.
Safety reasoning
  • Nothing here is hazardous; the real spectrum comes from the NIST WebBook. In a laboratory, CO₂ cylinders are high-pressure gas: chained, with a regulator, and CO₂ displaces air in closed rooms.
What can go wrong
  • Using the reduced mass of one C=O bond for CO₂; the modes involve all three atoms.
  • Expecting three IR bands for three atoms: there are four vibrations, ν₁ is IR inactive and the two bends coincide.
  • Forgetting the bend's degeneracy in the zero-point energy.
  • Shifting ν₁ for ¹³C.
  • Comparing harmonic model values with fundamentals: real fundamentals sit a few percent below the harmonic values (CCCBDB harmonic 1351, 672, 2396 cm⁻¹).
Data analysis

Record each model you try and export the table. In lesson04_co2.py you solve the same two equations: k + k₁₂ = mOω₁² and k − k₁₂ = ω₃²/(1/mO + 2/mC).
