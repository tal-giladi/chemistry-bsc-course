How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Boyle's law: fix n and T, set five volumes, record p each time, plot p against 1/V. The slope is nRT, so R = slope/(nT).
  2. Real gas: choose the gas and T, plot Z against p; below Tc plot isotherms and read the flat two-phase pressure.
  3. Kinetic box: let the histogram settle, then read p, the rms speed and T = M⟨v²⟩/3R; export speeds for the Python analysis.
  4. Clément–Desormes: pump a little air in, wait until the manometer stops falling, read h₁; open the tap about 1 s; close it, wait until the manometer stops rising, read h₂; γ ≈ h₁/(h₁ − h₂).
Why each step is done
  • Plotting p against 1/V turns Boyle's hyperbola into a straight line through the origin, so a slope and its uncertainty come from a linear fit.
  • Z shows the departure from ideal: Z < 1 when attractions dominate, Z > 1 when the molecules' own volume does.
  • Waiting after pumping lets the compression heat leave; the short opening is an adiabatic expansion; waiting after closing lets the gas return to room temperature at constant volume.
Safety reasoning
  • Gas under pressure stores energy. Real Clément–Desormes setups use 10–20 cm of water (1–2 kPa), a plastic or taped vessel and eye protection. In this model the stopper pops above about 35 cm.
  • Mercury manometers are no longer used: mercury vapour is toxic. Water or oil is used instead.
  • Compressing gas fast heats it (a bicycle pump, a diesel engine); expanding it fast cools it. High-pressure CO₂ near its critical point (31 °C, 73.8 bar) needs rated equipment.
What can go wrong
  • Tap open too long: heat leaks into the cold gas, h₂ is small and γ comes out low. Too short: the pressure has not reached room pressure and the gas surges; γ scatters both ways.
  • Reading h₁ or h₂ before the manometer settles; a leaky stopper (h falls while you wait).
  • In the box: reading T or p before the speed distribution has relaxed; a few thousand atoms fluctuate by 1–2 %.
  • Celsius instead of kelvin, g/mol instead of kg/mol in √(3RT/M).
Data analysis

Export the readings and fit p = b·(1/V) in Python to get R with its uncertainty. Export 3000 speeds (column speed_m_s) and the trials (trial,h1_cm,h2_cm) for lesson01_gases.py (Module 23). Each γ trial has a reading uncertainty of about 0.05 cm per height, which becomes about 1 % in γ because h₁ − h₂ is a difference.

Not trained here: handling a real pump, tap and manometer, or the feel of a seal that leaks.

Constants: R = 8.314462618 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and kB = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K (CODATA 2022, exact). Van der Waals a and b from the CRC Handbook table quoted by Atkins. Hard-sphere diameters are typical kinetic (collision) diameters. The ideal-gas gauge has 0.2 % random noise. The Clément–Desormes model: damped flow through the tap, adiabatic change of the gas left in the bottle, and Newtonian heat exchange with the room (time constants tuned so that a 1 s opening loses about 1 % of the cooling and the closed bottle settles in 2–3 minutes).
