How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Weighing by difference: close the doors, weigh the bottle with the solid (m1), lift it off, tap the solid into the beaker, weigh the bottle again (m2). The mass delivered is m1 − m2.
  2. Transfer pipette: rinse it twice with a little of the solution, fill above the mark, wipe the outside, let the meniscus fall to the mark with your eye level with it, deliver with the tip touching the wall, wait a few seconds, and do not blow out the last drop (TD/"Ex" glassware is calibrated to leave it).
  3. Volumetric flask: add the aliquot, fill to about 1 cm below the mark, then dropwise until the bottom of the meniscus sits on the mark, stopper and invert 10–15 times.
  4. Reading a meniscus: eye level with the bottom of the curve; a dark card held behind the tube makes the edge sharp.
Why each step is done
  • The balance offset drifts. Both readings of a difference contain it, so it cancels, and anything left on paper is no longer counted as delivered.
  • Open doors let air currents push the pan; a warm object sets up a convection current that makes it seem lighter.
  • Rinsing the pipette with the solution stops the water film inside from diluting the aliquot.
  • Class A tolerances are limits; divide by √3 for a standard uncertainty. The smallest volume you measure usually dominates the budget.
  • Parallax is systematic for a habit: always reading from above gives the same sign of error every time, so it does not average out.
Safety reasoning
  • Never pipette by mouth; use a pipette filler. Even dilute salt solutions are a bad habit, and real labs pipette toxic and corrosive liquids.
  • Chipped glassware cuts: check tips and rims before use and discard damaged pieces in the glass bin, not the general waste.
  • Hot glassware looks like cold glassware. Let it cool in a desiccator before weighing (which also fixes the warm-object error).
  • Label every flask with its contents, concentration, date and your initials.
What can go wrong
  • Solid left on the weighing paper is counted but not delivered: the result is high by up to a few mg.
  • Blowing out a TD pipette, or not rinsing it, gives a percent-level error that no budget formula will show.
  • Overshooting the mark cannot be undone: start again.
  • Measuring a 2 mL aliquot in a graduated cylinder gives an uncertainty of several percent.
Data analysis

Export the runs and attempts as CSV and recompute the budget in Python (Lesson 07.7, Part D): relative uncertainties add in quadrature, and the share of each term is (ui/xi)² divided by the total.

Not trained here: the feel of a pipette filler, controlling a drop at the mark, or a balance's real settling behaviour.

Tolerances: Class A per ASTM E288 (flasks), E969 (transfer pipettes), E287 (burettes) and E1272 (graduated cylinders), as quoted by Harvey (Analytical Chemistry 2.1, 2.4) and supplier tables; Class B taken as twice Class A. Each piece of glassware gets a fixed hidden error inside its tolerance. Balance: offset with a slow linear drift, Gaussian noise (larger with the doors open) and a warm-object buoyancy error that decays with time.
