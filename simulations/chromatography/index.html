How to use this, and why

Procedure
  1. Pick the technique from the analytes: GC for volatile, heat-stable compounds; HPLC for the rest.
  2. GC: inject a solvent blank first, then the sample with a split that keeps the column from overloading; start cool and ramp; with an MS keep the filament off during the solvent (solvent delay).
  3. Run the n-alkane ladder under exactly the same conditions and convert retention times into retention indices; compare with database indices on two phases of different polarity.
  4. HPLC: start from pKa, log P and UV; buffer at least 1.5–2 pH units from every pKa; run two scouting compositions, fit log k = log kw − Sφ and choose the composition from the worst pair.
  5. Before a sequence, run the system-suitability test: critical-pair Rs, tailing, area RSD of replicate injections and plates.
  6. For a column, measure H at many velocities, fit H = A + B/u + Cu, and run at or a little above uopt, within the pressure limit.
Why each step is done
  • Resolution Rs = (√N/4)·((α − 1)/α)·(k/(1 + k)): selectivity α is the cheapest lever, plates the most expensive (Rs grows only as √N), and k above about 10 buys little.
  • A temperature programme solves the general elution problem: early peaks stay apart at low temperature, late ones come out sharp as the oven heats.
  • Retention times move with flow, length and temperature; retention indices relative to the alkanes do not, so they can be compared with a database.
  • A weak acid is retained as the neutral molecule; near its pKa a 0.1 pH error moves k by about 11 %, two units away by 0.2 %.
  • Too much sample on a capillary column overloads the film: the peak broadens and fronts, and a small neighbour disappears under it.
  • The van Deemter terms: multiple paths (A, zero in an open tube), longitudinal diffusion (B/u) and mass transfer (Cu); uopt = √(B/C), Hmin = A + 2√(BC).
Safety reasoning
  • Hexane is highly flammable and harms nerves with long exposure: dilute samples in a fume hood and cap the vials.
  • Hydrogen carrier gas is flammable: the GC needs a hydrogen sensor, leak checks and a revalidated method. The injector, oven and transfer line run at 250–300 °C.
  • Acetonitrile is flammable and toxic by inhalation and skin contact; prepare mobile phases in a hood.
  • HPLC pumps run at 100–400 bar: tighten fittings with the pump stopped and never look into a leaking fitting under pressure. Flush phosphate buffer out with water before going to high organic, or it crystallises.
What can go wrong
  • Isothermal GC: too cold and the late peaks take hours, too hot and the early peaks crowd together at the void.
  • Identifying a peak from one retention time, or comparing an index from a ramped run with an isothermal or other-phase database value.
  • A small peak hidden under a big one (p-cymene under limonene): look for it with an extracted-ion chromatogram or on a polar column.
  • Solvent delay set shorter than the solvent peak: the filament burns in the hexane.
  • HPLC buffer near a pKa: retention drifts and peaks swap order; an aged column tails and loses plates and fails the SST.
  • Chasing the van Deemter optimum of a small-particle column past the pump's pressure limit.
Data analysis

Export the data of a tab (CSV) and analyse it in Python as in the Module 19 and 25 lab folders: k = (tR − tM)/tM, N = 5.545(tR/w½)², H = L/N, Rs and the Purnell equation; the linear retention index I = 100[n + (tx − tn)/(tn+1 − tn)]; log k against φ for kw and S, and a resolution map; a van Deemter fit with curve_fit and the uncertainties of uopt and Hmin from the covariance matrix.
