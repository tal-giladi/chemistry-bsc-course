# Instructor notes — 06.1 Cells, membranes and compartments

- Objective: diffusion time t = x²/2D and cell size; bilayer self-assembly by the hydrophobic effect and the permeability ranking; osmolarity and Π = icRT; Nernst potential; home isotonic point of potato.
- Likely confusion: molarity versus osmolarity (NaCl counts twice); which side is "out" in the Nernst equation.
- Misconception: "Lipids attract each other, which is why bilayers form." The driving force is mainly the entropy gained by water released from around the tails, not tail–tail attraction.
- Analogy: diffusion is a drunk walk; to get ten times further you need a hundred times as many steps.
- Expected hands-on result (example data): isotonic point 0.145 ± 0.005 M NaCl (all-points fit 0.151 ± 0.010), 0.29 osmol/L, 7.1 bar at 22 °C; mass change +8.7 % in water, −14.3 % in 0.5 M. Nernst at 37 °C: K⁺ −96.9 mV, Na⁺ +60.6 mV, Cl⁻ −64.1 mV. Diffusion: 0.5 ms (1 µm), 0.2 s (20 µm), 2 s for a protein, 5e4 s (1 cm). Real potatoes vary with variety and storage; any crossing inside the data range with a sensible uncertainty is a success.
- Safety: kitchen materials, cutting hazard (corer or chip cutter preferred), do not eat soaked potato; WARNING box.
- Extension: repeat with sucrose solutions (i = 1) and check that the isotonic sucrose concentration is about twice the NaCl one; or time how long a drop of food colouring takes to spread 1 cm and 2 cm in still water and test the x² law.
