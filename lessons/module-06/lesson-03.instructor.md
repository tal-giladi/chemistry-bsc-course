# Instructor notes — 06.3 Nucleic acids and the flow of information

- Objective: nucleotide structure and 5'→3' direction; base pairing (2 and 3 H-bonds), helix dimensions; reverse complement, Chargaff, GC content, Wallace Tm; translation and point mutations; DNA extraction chemistry.
- Likely confusion: complement versus reverse complement; which end is 5'.
- Misconception: "Hydrogen bonds alone hold DNA together." Stacking is a major contributor; salt (screening of backbone charges) matters as much as sequence.
- Analogy: a zipper whose teeth only fit their partner (specificity from H-bonds), but whose strength comes from the stack of teeth pressed together (stacking).
- Expected hands-on result: strawberry extract gives cloudy white strands within 1–2 minutes at the alcohol boundary (DNA with RNA and pectin). Python: 231 bases, 64.1 % GC, protein identical to P0CG48 1–76; forward primer Tm 58 °C, reverse 72 °C (80 % GC, poor pair); K48R missense, codon 48 TAG gives 47 residues; genome copy 1.05 m, 3.3 pg, 775 MB.
- Safety: pharmacy alcohol is flammable and an eye irritant; 50 mL only, no flames, window open, safety glasses; nothing eaten; WARNING box.
- Extension: redesign the reverse primer to within 3 °C of the forward one (shift or shorten it) and recheck with the script; or compare with an online nearest-neighbour Tm calculator and note how much the Wallace rule differs.
