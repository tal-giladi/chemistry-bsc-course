# Instructor notes — 20.1 Light-matter interaction and selection rules

- Objective: the learner places a transition in its spectral region, applies the gross rules (permanent dipole, changing dipole, changing polarisability) and specific rules, computes Boltzmann ratios with degeneracy, and compares natural, Doppler and instrument widths.
- Likely confusion: the two separate reasons for a weak line (small transition dipole versus small population); $A/B \propto \nu^3$ read as "visible transitions are stronger" (it is about spontaneous emission, not absorption strength); units of $k_BT$ against cm⁻¹.
- Misconception: "no dipole, no spectrum". N₂ has no microwave or IR spectrum but a clear Raman line; CO₂ has no dipole but strong IR bands. Second misconception: "the Boltzmann factor makes higher J more populated" — it is the $2J + 1$ degeneracy.
- Analogy: a selection rule is a lock and the photon a key; population is how many people stand at the door; line width is how blurred your photo of the door is.
- Expected hands-on result: $k_BT/hc$ = 207.2 cm⁻¹; HCl J=1/J=0 2.71; HCl v=1/v=0 8.9e-7; I₂ v=1/v=0 0.357; I₂ B/X 1.1e-33; A/B relative factors 57, 2.4e10, 8e12; Doppler 269 kHz (CO), 0.0060 cm⁻¹ = 180 MHz (HCl), 0.014 cm⁻¹ (I₂). Part A table as in the lesson.
- Extension: compute the temperature at which the HCl v = 1 population reaches 1 % (about 900 K) and discuss why hot bands appear in flame and stellar spectra; or estimate the pressure at which collision broadening (0.1 cm⁻¹/atm) equals the HCl Doppler width (about 0.06 atm).
