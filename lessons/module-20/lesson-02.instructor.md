# Instructor notes — 20.2 Rotational spectra

- Objective: the learner assigns a rotational ladder, fits $B$ and $D$ with the $\tilde\nu/(J+1)$ against $(J+1)^2$ line, gets $r_0$, distinguishes $B_0/r_0$ from $B_e/r_e$, predicts isotope shifts and classifies polyatomic rotors.
- Likely confusion: level spacing versus line spacing ($2B$); $B$ in MHz versus cm⁻¹; why $B_0$ is smaller than $B_e$ (vibrational averaging of $1/r^2$ in an anharmonic well).
- Misconception: "a microwave oven heats food by exciting the rotational line of water". The 2.45 GHz oven frequency is not a water line; heating comes from dielectric loss in the liquid, where free rotation is quenched. Second: "isotopes change the bond length" — only the averaged $r_0$ changes slightly; $r_e$ is the same.
- Analogy: a figure skater who spins faster with arms out — at high speed the arms (atoms) fly out, the moment of inertia grows and the levels sag (centrifugal distortion).
- Expected hands-on result: $B_0$ = 1.922526 cm⁻¹ = 57 635.9 MHz, $D$ = 6.11e-6 cm⁻¹, $r_0$ = 1.13089 Å, rigid misfit −1.341 cm⁻¹ at $J+1 = 38$, residuals with $D$ below 0.001 cm⁻¹; ¹³CO $B_0$ = 1.837971 cm⁻¹ vs 1.837769 predicted; $J_\text{max}$ 0.84 (10 K) and 6.84 (298 K). Pencil: 110 190 MHz predicted vs 110 201 MHz measured.
- Extension: fit the ¹³CO lines alone (only 9, sparse in $J$) and compare the uncertainty of $D$ with the ¹²CO fit; or use $D = 4B^3/\omega_e^2$ backwards to estimate $\omega_e$ of CO from the microwave data alone (about 2160 cm⁻¹, against 2170 from the IR).
