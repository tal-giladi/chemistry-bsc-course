# Instructor notes — 20.5 Symmetry elements and point groups

- Objective: the learner identifies E, Cn, σ (h, v, d), i and Sn on 3D molecules, assigns the point group with the flowchart, and reads polarity and chirality off the group.
- Likely confusion: elements versus operations (one C3 axis = two operations); σv versus σd; finding S4 in CH₄ and S6 in staggered ethane; choosing the principal axis in D2h/D2d.
- Misconception: "no mirror plane means chiral" (i and other Sn also forbid chirality); "four ligands means tetrahedral symmetry" (CH₂Cl₂ is C2v); "a flat drawing shows the symmetry" (CH₄ drawn flat looks D4h).
- Analogy: a symmetry operation is a trick played while you close your eyes — if you cannot tell, when you open them, that anything happened, it was a symmetry operation.
- Expected hands-on result: models — CH₄ 4C3, 3C2, 3S4, 6σd (Td); NH₃ C3v; BF₃ D3h. Python 8/8: H₂O C2v, NH₃ C3v, CH₄ Td, BF₃ D3h, C₂H₄ D2h, CHFClBr C1, HCN C∞v, CH₂Cl₂ C2v. Pencil: PCl₅ D3h, [PtCl₄]²⁻ D4h, cis-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ C2 (chiral), cis-ClCH=CHCl C2v.
- Extension: build a ferrocene model, staggered and eclipsed (D5d and D5h), and add its coordinates to `molecules20.py` to test the finder; or find the 120 operations of C₆₀ (Ih) by counting axes.
