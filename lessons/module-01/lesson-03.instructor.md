# Instructor notes — 01.3 The lab notebook and the lab report

- **Objective:** a dated, repeatable notebook entry with raw data first and honest corrections; a short eight-section report with labelled tables and a result with spread.
- **Likely confusion:** raw versus processed data. Learners write "water 50 g" and think it is raw. Ask: "what did the display show?"
- **Misconception:** a neat notebook is a good notebook. Neatness by erasing or recopying is worse than a messy but honest page. Also: "bad repeats should be deleted" — they are flagged and kept, and excluded only with a stated reason.
- **Analogy:** the notebook is a flight recorder; the report is the accident investigation written afterwards. You cannot write the investigation if the recorder was edited.
- **Expected hands-on result:** about 5 mL per level teaspoon (real spoons give roughly 4–7 mL), a spread of a few tenths of a millilitre, a table with "mass / g" headers, and a discussion naming spoon levelling as the main error. The 1 g scale resolution matters little because 10 spoons are weighed at once.
- **Extension:** repeat with a tablespoon and compare the ratio with the cooking rule of 3 teaspoons per tablespoon; or swap notebooks with someone and try to repeat their measurement from the page alone.
