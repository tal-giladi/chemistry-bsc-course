# Instructor notes — 01.4 Chemical safety: hazards, SDS and Israeli law

- **Objective:** read GHS labels and SDS sections, use PubChem's GHS section, reason about concentration, and know the legal and course limits for home work in Israel.
- **Likely confusion:** PubChem shows several classifications with percentages. Explain these are company notifications; read the most common one. Also hazard (property) versus risk (hazard plus exposure).
- **Misconception:** "not classified means harmless" (baking soda in large amounts is not), and "the pure substance's label applies to my product" (vinegar versus glacial acetic acid). Also "small private amounts of reagents are fine" — the law and the lack of a fume hood both say no.
- **Analogy:** hazard is a dog's bite; risk is whether the dog is on a lead. The label tells you how hard it bites; your controls are the lead.
- **Expected hands-on result:** the four-row table as in the lesson (acetic acid Danger flame + corrosion H226/H314; sodium hypochlorite Danger corrosion + environment H314/H318/H400/H410; sodium bicarbonate not classified; ethanol Danger flame H225), at least one "do not mix" label warning, and a four-heading risk assessment rating Lesson 01.5 as low risk.
- **Extension:** download a real SDS for a household bleach sold in Israel from the manufacturer's website and compare its section 2 with PubChem; or look up the EU's concentration limits for classifying acetic acid mixtures.
- **Check before reuse:** GHS revision (Rev. 11, 2025; Rev. 12 expected 2027), PubChem classifications, the poison centre number 04-777-1900, and the status of Israeli standard SI 2302 — all checked 2026-10-16.
