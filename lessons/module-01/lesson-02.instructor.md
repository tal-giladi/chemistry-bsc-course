# Instructor notes — 01.2 Units, significant figures and dimensional analysis

- **Objective:** units on every line, conversions by factors equal to 1, correct rounding, and using units to catch errors.
- **Likely confusion:** the two rounding rules (sig figs for ×/÷, decimal places for +/−). Learners apply the ×/÷ rule to sums. Mixed problems (a sum, then a division) need each rule at its step, with extra digits carried.
- **Misconception:** "exact numbers limit sig figs" (5 repeats, 1000 mL/L, 273.15). Also "the mole is 12 g of carbon-12", taught before 2019.
- **Analogy:** a conversion factor is like changing money at an exchange counter: the value does not change, only the currency it is written in. If you end up holding dollars per shekel squared, you used the rate upside down.
- **Expected hands-on result:** 7–8/8. The most common miss is problem 7 (37.5 mol from forgetting mL to L) and problem 2 (310 K instead of 310.2 K).
- **Extension:** have the learner look up R on the NIST CODATA site, then derive 0.08206 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ from 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ using 1 atm = 101 325 Pa and 1 L = 10⁻³ m³.
