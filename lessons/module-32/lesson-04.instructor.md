# Instructor notes — 32.4 Band theory: metals, semiconductors and insulators

- Objective: the learner builds a band from a Hückel chain (width 4|β|, DOS), explains metals by partial filling and gaps by a two-atom repeat, computes n_i(T) for Si, Ge, GaAs from Ioffe parameters, uses np = n_i², and links Eg to LED colour and direct/indirect gaps.
- Likely confusion: why the band width does not grow with N; k as a label versus momentum; why the exponent has 2kT; Fermi level versus "highest filled level" at T > 0.
- Misconception: "insulators have no electrons that can move"; "doping adds both carriers"; "silicon cannot make light at all" (it does, weakly).
- Analogy: a car park (band). A full car park with the next level closed off (gap) has no traffic even with many cars; a half-full one lets cars move. Heat opens a few spaces upstairs.
- Expected hands-on result: chain widths 5.000 (N 2), 8.090 (4), 9.595 (10), 9.995 (100), 10.000 eV (1000); centre DOS 0.064/eV; gaps 1, 3, 6 eV for Δα 1, 3, 6 (0.016 eV finite-size gap at Δα 0); n_i(300 K) Si 8.60e9, Ge 1.97e13, GaAs 2.32e6; ratios 400/300 K 528, 67, 2941; Arrhenius slope for Si 1.207 eV. Home (optional): cold red LED needs a few tens of mV more, drifting back. Pencil: ~160 cm⁻³; p 1e17, n ~740; semimetal.
- Safety: optional home part uses the 04.5 circuit only; WARNING box (resistor always in, no lasers, batteries never opened, only the bare LED in the freezer).
- Extension: add periodic boundary conditions to the chain and plot E against k = 2πj/(Na) to see the cosine band; or compute the intrinsic resistivity of Si with μn 1400 and μp 450 cm²/(V s) and compare with Ioffe's 3.2 × 10⁵ Ω cm.
