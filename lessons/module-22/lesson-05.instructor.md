# Instructor notes — 22.5 Catalysis and enzyme kinetics

- Objective: the learner explains why catalysts leave $K$ unchanged, contrasts homogeneous, surface and enzyme catalysis, derives Michaelis–Menten with the SSA, interprets $V_\text{max}$, $K_m$, $k_\text{cat}/K_m$, and completes the module practical: an end-to-end fit of the 1913 invertase data with and without product inhibition.
- Likely confusion: $K_m$ versus $K_S$ (Briggs–Haldane versus rapid equilibrium); why Michaelis and Menten could not report $k_\text{cat}$ (no enzyme concentration); arbitrary units of the initial rates versus mM/min from the time courses.
- Misconception: "a catalyst shifts equilibrium toward products". Second: "the enzyme with the larger $k_\text{cat}$ is always faster" — at low [S] the specificity constant decides (quiz q3).
- Analogy: an enzyme is a checkout till — $k_\text{cat}$ is how fast the cashier scans when there is always a queue, $K_m$ is how long the queue must be before the cashier is busy half the time, and products left on the belt (product inhibition) slow the next customer.
- Expected hands-on result: Part A foam roughly proportional to yeast, restart on fresh peroxide. Part B: $V_\text{max}$ = 3.91 ± 0.06 (arb.), $K_m$ = 17.9 ± 1.0 mM (nonlinear); 3.95 and 18.4 mM (Lineweaver–Burk); global fit 0.775 ± 0.009 mM/min, RMS 0.0159 with inhibition; 0.648 ± 0.013 mM/min, RMS 0.0322 without; $V_\text{max}/K_m$ = 0.0464 min⁻¹ vs 0.0454 ± 0.0032 (1913). Worked example: predicted apparent first-order constant at 20.8 mM = 0.0207 min⁻¹, matching 22.1.
- Extension: let $K_S$ float together with $V_\text{max}$ in the global fit and compare with 16.7 mM; or drop the 333 mM course and see how much the fitted $V_\text{max}$ changes (it tests the slight substrate inhibition). Link forward to Module 29 for inhibition types.
