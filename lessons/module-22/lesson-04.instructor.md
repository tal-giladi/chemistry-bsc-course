# Instructor notes — 22.4 Temperature dependence: Arrhenius and transition-state theory

- Objective: the learner fits Arrhenius with uncertainties, estimates a collision-theory rate and steric factor, extracts $\Delta^\ddagger H$ and $\Delta^\ddagger S$ from an Eyring plot, and explains curved Arrhenius plots with real OH + CH₄ data.
- Likely confusion: $E_a$ versus $\Delta^\ddagger H$ (differ by $RT$ for unimolecular reactions); why $A$ is so uncertain (extrapolation to $1/T = 0$); the meaning of $k_BT/h$ (a universal crossing frequency, not a vibration of the molecule).
- Misconception: "activation energy is the barrier height and never changes". It is defined as a slope and can vary with $T$, and can even be negative for composite reactions (22.3). Second: "a straight Arrhenius plot over 100 K proves it is straight everywhere".
- Analogy: the transition state is the top of a mountain pass; $\Delta^\ddagger H$ is how high the pass is, $\Delta^\ddagger S$ how wide — a wide pass lets many walkers through at once.
- Expected hands-on result: OH + CH₄ $E_a$ 13.8 (196–300 K), 16.5 (300–420 K), 34.4 kJ/mol (840–2000 K), local $E_a$ 12.9 → ~34 kJ/mol; N₂O₅ $E_a$ = 92.5 kJ/mol, $A = 1.05\times10^{15}$ s⁻¹, $\Delta^\ddagger H$ = 90.5 kJ/mol, $\Delta^\ddagger S$ = +36 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Worked: +34 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ at 298 K; $P \approx 0.005$. Pencil: 56.8 kJ/mol and factor 0.19; ratio 1.5e4; 99.8 and 102.3 kJ/mol. Tablet: typically a few tens of kJ/mol (a test file 10/20/40 °C with 90/55/25 s gave 31 ± 1 kJ/mol); learners' scatter 5–10 % per point.
- Extension: fit the modified Arrhenius form $A(T/298)^n e^{-E/RT}$ to all 20 OH + CH₄ points with `curve_fit` and compare $n$ with the review value 2.23 (NIST 2021 entry); or compute the methane lifetime at 220 K and 300 K from the low-T fit to show why altitude matters.
