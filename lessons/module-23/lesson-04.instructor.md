# Instructor notes — 23.4 Kinetics: dye fading tracked with a phone camera

- Objective: the learner sets up isolation conditions, turns phone red values into pseudo-absorbance, fits k′ with an offset, finds the order in hypochlorite from a doubling, estimates Ea from two temperatures, and runs the experiment within the bleach safety rules.
- Likely confusion: why a bimolecular reaction gives a single exponential; why no calibration is needed for k′; what the offset A∞ is; why the fit error understates the run-to-run scatter.
- Misconception: "acid would speed it up, so a little vinegar helps". It releases chlorine; the lesson and quiz say so explicitly. Watch for this in discussion.
- Analogy: isolation is a single customer in a shop with a thousand assistants; how fast they are served depends only on how many customers there are.
- Expected hands-on result: example data k′ = 2.99, 6.04, 5.89 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ (t½ 3.9, 1.9, 2.0 min), n = 1.01, Ea = 50 kJ/mol. Home: half-lives of a few minutes with 2 mL of 4 % bleach in 200 mL are typical, but brand and bottle age vary; n within 0.8–1.2 and Ea within roughly 30–70 kJ/mol are good results.
- Extension: three or four bleach volumes (1, 2, 3, 4 mL, total still under 10 mL) and a log–log plot of k′ against volume; or compare with the allura red results of Madriz et al. (2021) using red colouring and the green channel.
