# Instructor notes — 23.2 Thermochemistry: enthalpy of neutralisation and solution

- Objective: the learner calibrates a cup, extrapolates ΔT, computes ΔH per mole of the limiting reagent with uncertainty, predicts citric acid + NaHCO₃ by Hess's law with ionisation enthalpies, and corrects for retained CO₂ by mass loss.
- Likely confusion: why all strong acid/base pairs give the same ΔH; why an endothermic reaction is spontaneous (entropy of the gas); which reagent is limiting; the sign convention when the cup cools.
- Misconception: "endothermic reactions need heating to run". The fizzing tablet runs by itself and chills the water.
- Analogy: spectator ions are the audience at a wedding; the heat belongs to the two people who actually marry (H⁺ and OH⁻).
- Expected hands-on result: example data C_cal = 47 ± 10 J/K; reaction ΔT −2.54 K, +34.6 ± 1.1 kJ/mol, CO₂ escaped 92 %, corrected +36.1 ± 1.1 vs Hess +34.7; NaCl +4.3 ± 0.3 (table +3.9); Epsom +13.9 ± 0.7 (tables +12 to +16). Sim neutralisation within 1–2 % of −55.8 kJ/mol. Home results: reaction +30 to +38 kJ/mol is a good measurement.
- Extension: measure citric acid's own enthalpy of solution from the cooling in step 2 (needs a separate before/after record), or repeat the reaction with citric acid limiting and compare the per-mole value (third proton, ionisation −3.38 kJ/mol).
