# Instructor notes — 23.1 Gas laws and kinetic theory in the lab

- Objective: the learner turns a speed sample into a temperature with its statistical uncertainty, explains each step of Clément–Desormes, computes γ and Cv,m from manometer heights, and reads degrees of freedom from γ.
- Likely confusion: why the log formula reduces to h1/(h1 − h2); why helium and argon give the same pressure at the same T and N; which of the three speeds to use where.
- Misconception: "hotter gas means every molecule moves faster by the same amount". The whole distribution broadens and shifts; some molecules are always slow.
- Analogy: the tap opening is like taking a cake out of the oven for one second to check it: too long and the kitchen's heat (here, the room's) gets in and spoils the measurement.
- Expected hands-on result: example data T = 300.5 ± 4.5 K, rms 433.2 m/s (sample and theory), χ² 16.1 for 23 dof; γ = 1.411, SD 0.022, 95 % CI ± 0.019; Cv,m = 2.43 R. Sim: 5 s opening gives γ near 1.30. Pencil: 140 K; CO₂ γ = 1.311, Cv = 3.2R.
- Extension: add a "valve open time" model (Newton cooling during the opening) and fit the opening time that explains a γ of 1.32; or measure the speed of sound in a cardboard tube with phyphox and get γ from c = √(γRT/M).
