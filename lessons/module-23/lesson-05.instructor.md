# Instructor notes — 23.5 Electrochemistry: cells, potentials and a lemon battery

- Objective: the learner separates EMF from terminal voltage, fits a load line for E and r with uncertainties, predicts series and parallel combinations and maximum power, and extracts n from a Nernst slope.
- Likely confusion: why the voltmeter reads the EMF (near-zero current); why salt water has lower r but lower E (different cathode reaction); ln versus log10 constants.
- Misconception: "a bigger voltage means more power". A lemon stack with 3 V cannot run a motor that a 1.5 V AA cell runs easily; internal resistance decides.
- Analogy: internal resistance is a narrow door out of a full stadium; the crowd (EMF) is the same, but how many people get out per minute depends on the door.
- Expected hands-on result: example data lemon E = 0.936 ± 0.018 V, r = 1815 ± 52 Ω, Pmax 121 µW; salt5 0.785 V, 431 Ω; vinegar 0.955 V, 976 Ω; Nernst slope 29.64 ± 0.27 mV/decade, n = 2.00, E at 0.100 M 1.103 V. Home: E 0.7–1.0 V, r from a few hundred ohms (salt) to a few kilohms (lemon).
- Extension: measure r against electrode spacing (1, 2, 4, 8 cm) and against salt concentration, and plot r against 1/conductivity; or record V under a 1 kΩ load every 10 s for 5 minutes to see polarisation.
