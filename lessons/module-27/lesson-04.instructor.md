# Instructor notes — 27.4 Intermolecular forces from electrostatics (module practical)

- Objective: the learner computes Keesom, Debye and London coefficients from measured μ, α′ and I, explains boiling-point trends with them, computes point-charge dimer energies, and writes the module report linking 27.2–27.4.
- Likely confusion: "dipole–dipole" (fixed, r⁻³) versus Keesom (averaged, r⁻⁶, 1/T); factor-of-2 conventions in the induction term; why a hydrogen bond needs charges, not dipoles.
- Misconception: "polar molecules are held together mainly by dipole–dipole forces". For HCl, HBr, HI the numbers say dispersion; only small, very polar molecules (H₂O, NH₃) are dominated by orientation.
- Analogy: dispersion is like two people on a wobbly bridge falling into step — neither has a fixed rhythm (dipole), but their fluctuations become correlated and pull them together.
- Expected hands-on result: Part 1 (298 K, 10⁻⁷⁹ J m⁶): HCl 23.1/6.0/96.9 (18/5/77 %), HBr 7.6/4.9/183.5, HI 0.7/2.2/371.1, NH₃ 76.9/9.2/53.5 (55 % K), H₂O 192.6/10.4/34.2 (81 % K); non-polar bp vs ln C₆ r = 0.988; HCl pair at 0.4 nm −1.85 kJ/mol. Part 2: TIP3P dimer minimum −25.5 kJ/mol at 2.75 Å, tilt 0° in this constrained scan (Coulomb −32.8, LJ +7.3); point dipole −12.7; tilt 30/60/90° → −24.2/−19.6/−10.8. Experimental D0 13.2 kJ/mol; De ≈ 21–23 kJ/mol (QMC). Part 3 CO₂: end-to-end +1.59, parallel +1.73, T-shaped −3.29 kJ/mol. Pencil: −0.74 kJ/mol (fixed −3.3), ratio 4.46, edge-to-face from quadrupoles.
- Note: the scan holds the H-bond linear and the acceptor plane perpendicular to the donor plane; TIP3P has no lone-pair sites, so it prefers the acceptor dipole along the bond. Models with lone-pair sites (TIP4P/TIP5P-type) tilt it. Good discussion point; do not present the 0° tilt as the real dimer geometry.
- Extension: fit a Lennard-Jones C₆ to the second virial coefficient of argon from NIST WebBook data and compare with London's estimate; or add a dispersion term (London C₆) to the CO₂ dimer energies and see whether the parallel slipped geometry becomes competitive.
