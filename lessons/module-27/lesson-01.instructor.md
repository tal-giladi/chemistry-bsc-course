# Instructor notes — 27.1 Maxwell's equations for chemists

- Objective: the learner reads the four equations as flux and circulation statements, derives Coulomb's law from Gauss's law, computes c from ε0 and μ0, measures c with a microwave oven, and explains static εr versus n².
- Likely confusion: why charges outside a closed surface give no net flux even though they change the field there; the role of D versus E; why "dielectric constant" depends on frequency.
- Misconception: "the dielectric constant is a fixed property of the liquid". It is a function of frequency (and temperature); the static value and n² are its two ends for a polar liquid.
- Analogy: flux is like counting people crossing a closed fence line: passers-by who walk in and out again add nothing to the net count; only people who were born inside (the charges) do.
- Expected hands-on result: Part 1 c = 299 792 458 m/s (difference ~6e-13). Part 2 flux 3.619e-9 V m both ways, unchanged after moving outside charges. Part 3 example data: spacing 6.27 cm, c = (3.07 ± 0.05) × 10^8 m/s (true 2.998). Real kitchen runs give 5.5–6.5 cm spacings and c within 5–10 %. Part 4 ratios 0.99, 1.05, 1.01, 2.46, 19, 15, 45. Pencil: 4.4e10 V/m, factor 3.1; n = 1.375; λ in ice 6.8 cm.
- Extension: solve Poisson's equation numerically in 1D for a slab of charge, or compute the Born solvation energy of Na⁺ in water and compare with the experimental hydration enthalpy (overestimate shows the first-shell problem).
