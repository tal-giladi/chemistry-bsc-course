# Instructor notes — 27.2 Multipoles and molecular charge distributions

- Objective: the learner computes Q, μ and the traceless Θ from point charges, applies the origin rule and symmetry rules, and compares model moments with CCCBDB values.
- Likely confusion: the Buckingham factor ½ and the trace term; sign conventions (chemistry μ from − to +, physics the same for μ = Σqr but drawings differ); why the quadrupole of water depends on origin but that of CO₂ does not.
- Misconception: "partial charges are physical observables". They are model parameters; only moments (and the potential) are observables.
- Analogy: describing a crowd from a helicopter: first how many people (charge), then whether they are lopsided to one side (dipole), then whether they bunch at the ends or the middle (quadrupole). The higher you fly, the less the fine detail matters.
- Expected hands-on result: water TIP3P |μ| = 2.347 D, Θ diagonal (+1.76, −1.68, −0.08) D Å about the centre of mass (exp. +2.63, −2.50, −0.13); CO₂ Θzz = −14.09 × 10⁻⁴⁰ C m² (exp. −14.27 ± 0.61); OH⁻ shift changes μ by 4.80 D; water Θ changes by up to 3.52 D Å on a 1 Å shift; expansion error 14 % at 2.5 Å, ~1.5 % at 7.5 Å. HCl custom model 1.093 D. Pencil: BF₃, trans-DCE, SF₆ non-polar; SF₆ only zero Θ; N₂ model −4.65 × 10⁻⁴⁰; 0.050 V and 0.025 V.
- Extension: compute the electrostatic potential map of water on a plane with the charges and compare with a published ab initio ESP map; or fit two charges to reproduce both μ and Θ of HCl and discuss why two charges cannot do both.
