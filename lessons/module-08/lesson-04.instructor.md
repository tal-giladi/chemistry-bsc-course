# Instructor notes — 08.4 Acids, bases and pH

- Objective: Brønsted acids/bases and conjugate pairs; Kw, pH, pOH; strong-acid and weak-acid pH with the 5 % check; Henderson–Hasselbalch buffers; indicator colour ranges and red cabbage.
- Likely confusion: strong vs concentrated, and pH = pKa (true only for an equal-ratio buffer, not for a weak acid alone).
- Misconception: "pH 7 is always neutral" and "dilution raises the pH by one unit for any acid". Show the 37 °C value and the vinegar dilution (only +0.5).
- Analogy: a buffer as a shock absorber, or a bank account with both a savings and a credit line: H+ in or out is taken up until one side runs out.
- Expected hands-on result: vinegar and lemon red-pink; tap water purple (tap water in Israel is typically pH 7–8.5, so it may lean blue); sparkling water pinker; baking soda blue to blue-green; soap blue-green/green (pH-balanced liquid soaps can stay purple). Script: 2.00, 2.41, 2.92, 5.65, 8.35, 4.75; small-x 0.46 %, ionised 0.46 % and 1.46 %. Part C pH 10.30; formic about 0.5 lower.
- Safety emphasis: no bleach, oven or drain cleaner (H314 corrosives); the "never mix bleach" rule is repeated because vinegar is on the bench.
- Extension: buffer capacity (add 1 mL of vinegar to buffered vs unbuffered cup and watch the colour), forward to Module 9 (titration curves) and Module 10 (the 5-repeat vinegar project).
