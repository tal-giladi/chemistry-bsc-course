# Instructor notes — 08.3 Chemical equilibrium and Le Chatelier

- Objective: write K and Q; direction from Q vs K; ICE tables solved exactly and with small-x (5 % check); Le Chatelier for concentration, pressure, temperature; van 't Hoff.
- Likely confusion: K vs Q. Students treat "K < 1" as "goes backward". Always ask "compared with what?".
- Misconception: adding a reactant or a catalyst "changes K", and pressure always shifts gas equilibria. Show the water-gas shift: equal gas moles, no pressure effect; and inert gas at constant volume changes no partial pressure.
- Analogy: equilibrium as two shops trading customers through one door at equal rates; Le Chatelier as a queue that rebalances when you add people to one side.
- Expected hands-on result: Part A Q = 2 > K (backward); α(10 bar) = 0.088 both ways; NH3 less/more/unchanged; K(50 °C) = 1.75. Script: K(1000 K) = 0.905, conversion 48.8 % and 73.6 %; small-x errors 33.4/12.3/3.8/1.3/0.4/0.1 % from 0.1 to 30 bar; van 't Hoff K 0.0405, 0.312, 1.75, 27.7; 700 K extension K ≈ 7.6, conversion 73 %. Soda: warm bottle loses 2–4× more in 30 min (large spread; depends on the brand and how full).
- Data note: the Appendix G based Kp(N2O4, 298 K) = 0.31 is about twice the value from JANAF-based tables (about 0.15). A 2 kJ/mol difference in ΔG° explains it; a good discussion of data quality. The constant-ΔH, ΔS water-gas K at 1000 K (0.9) is a little below the measured value (about 1.4).
- Extension: oxygen loading of haemoglobin as Le Chatelier (high pO2 in lungs, low in tissues), preview of the Hill equation in Module 26.
