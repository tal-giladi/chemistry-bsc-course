# Instructor notes — 08.2 Entropy, free energy and spontaneity

- Objective: ΔrS° from absolute S°; ΔG = ΔH − TΔS with correct units; crossover T = ΔH/ΔS; ΔG° = −RT ln K both ways.
- Likely confusion: J vs kJ in TΔS and kelvin vs Celsius. Nearly every wrong numeric answer is one of these. Insist that students write units on every term.
- Misconception: "spontaneous means it happens quickly" and "endothermic processes cannot be spontaneous". Use ice melting and the cold pack against the second, diamond against the first.
- Analogy: ΔG as the budget. ΔH is the cash paid or received; TΔS is the value of the freedom gained. The deal goes through when the total is favourable. Temperature sets the exchange rate for freedom.
- Expected hands-on result: ice–salt cup −10 to −20 °C within 3–5 min (finely crushed ice and good stirring go colder; eutectic about −21 °C); control 0 ± 1 °C; sugar only a few degrees below zero. Part B: ΔS° +175.8 J/(mol K), ΔG° +2.9 kJ/mol, K 0.31, T* 315 K; ΔG° for K = 10⁻³ is +17.1 kJ/mol. Script: ΔG(298) −4.35, −32.74, +2.89, +8.59 kJ/mol; T* 258, 463, 315, 370 K; K(298) 5.79, 5.4e5, 0.312, 0.0313.
- Data note: the NH4NO3 standard-state ΔG° (−4.1 to −4.4 kJ/mol) refers to 1 mol/L ideal solution; real solubility is far higher because activity coefficients differ. Do not over-read the 258 K crossover.
- Extension: the K for water evaporation is the vapour pressure in bar. Ask students to compute it at 50 °C with constant ΔH, ΔS (0.12 bar; measured 0.123) — a preview of Clausius–Clapeyron in Module 13.
