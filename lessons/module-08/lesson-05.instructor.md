# Instructor notes — 08.5 Redox reactions and balancing

- Objective: oxidation numbers; identify oxidised/reduced species and agents; half-reaction balancing in acid and base; balancing as a null-space problem; vitamin C by iodine titration with standardisation.
- Likely confusion: "the oxidising agent is oxidised". Drill the sentence "X is reduced, so X is the oxidising agent".
- Misconception: a balanced equation only needs balanced atoms. Use the Fe/MnO4- trap (atoms fine, charge +9 vs +5).
- Analogy: electrons as money; the reducing agent pays, the oxidising agent is paid. Oxidation numbers are the bookkeeping of a fictional all-ionic world.
- Expected hands-on result: standardisation 6.9 mL per 1.00 mL povidone-iodine at label strength (5.5–8 mL typical; older bottles lower); juice 30–60 mg per 100 mL fresh, often 20–40 for carton juice open several days. Pencil: +6, +6, −4/+4, −1; 3Cu + 2NO3- + 8H+ → 3Cu2+ + 2NO + 4H2O; Cr gains 3 e-. Script: 5,1,8→5,1,4; 3,2,8→3,2,4; 1,1→1,2,2; 2,3,16→4,3,11; 2,6,4→2,3,8; 1,5→3,4; oxalate 2,5,16→2,10,8; H2O/H2O2 gives a 2-D null-space message.
- Data note: the povidone-iodine complex releases iodine slowly; titrate steadily and wait 30 s at the end point. Effervescent tablets contain bicarbonate and citric acid that do not interfere much, but use plain tablets for the standard.
- Extension: half-reaction potentials from Appendix L predict which way the redox goes; this is lesson 08.6. Forward to Module 10 (iodometric and permanganate titrations).
