# Instructor notes — 29.1 Protein structure and stability

- Objective: the learner treats folding as the equilibrium N ⇌ U, converts $\Delta G_U$ into $K_U$ and fraction unfolded, uses the Gibbs–Helmholtz stability curve with $\Delta C_p$, fits a thermal melt and a urea melt with sloping baselines, and converts $\Delta C_m$ into $\Delta\Delta G$.
- Likely confusion: the sign convention ($\Delta G_U$ of unfolding is positive for a stable protein; some texts use $\Delta G$ of folding); $T_m$ as onset rather than midpoint; why a single thermal melt cannot fix $\Delta C_p$ (it shows up only as curvature of $\Delta H$ with temperature, which one scan barely sees).
- Misconception: "a stable protein is never unfolded" — it is unfolded 1 in 10⁴ of the time even at 24 kJ/mol. Second: "cold makes proteins more stable without limit" — the stability curve has a maximum. Third: "egg white has a melting point" — its unfolding is irreversible and time dependent.
- Analogy: the stability is the small profit of a business with huge income (enthalpy of contacts, water entropy) and huge costs (chain entropy); a small change in either line wipes out the profit.
