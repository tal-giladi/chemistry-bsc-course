# Instructor notes — 19.4 Gas and liquid chromatography

- Objective: the learner chooses GC or HPLC and the main settings (phase, carrier or mobile phase, injection, detector), calculates a retention index and identifies peaks with NIST indices on two phases, calibrates with an internal standard, and reasons about particle size and pressure.
- Likely confusion: Kováts (isothermal, log form) versus linear van den Dool–Kratz indices; why alkanes define the scale on every column; "reversed phase" naming (non-polar column, polar solvent).
- Misconception: "the retention time identifies the compound". It only rules candidates out; independent evidence (second phase, MS) is needed.
- Analogy: the alkane ladder is like milestones along a road; you report a peak as "a third of the way from milestone 10 to 11", which stays true even if the whole road is driven faster.
- Expected hands-on result: X 1031/1196, Y 1098/1552 → limonene and linalool; raw areas s_r 11.4 % of mean, ratios 0.7 %; sanitizer 68.3 ± 0.3 % v/v. NIST: limonene DB-5 ≈ 1031, DB-Wax ≈ 1189–1200; linalool DB-5 ≈ 1098–1101, wax ≈ 1552. Sim: a ramp of about 5 K/min resolves the mixture; hydrogen shortens the run.
- Extension: compute the isothermal Kováts index with logarithms of adjusted times for an isothermal ladder and compare; discuss deuterated internal standards in GC-MS; preview of 25.4 (GC-MS against NIST spectra) and 25.5 (HPLC method thinking).
