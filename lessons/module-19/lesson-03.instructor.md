# Instructor notes — 19.3 Extraction and the theory of chromatography

- Objective: the learner calculates extraction efficiency for single and repeated extractions, uses D(pH) for a weak acid, calculates k, α, N, H and Rs from a chromatogram, uses the Purnell equation to choose a lever, and fits the van Deemter equation.
- Likely confusion: K_D versus D; base width (16) versus half-height width (5.545) in N; that α is defined with the later peak on top so it is always ≥ 1.
- Misconception: "a longer column fixes every overlap". Resolution scales with √N, so doubling the length gains only 41 % and doubles the time; selectivity is cheaper.
- Analogy: chromatography is a crowd walking through a market; each person stops at stalls for a while. People who like the stalls (high k) arrive late; people who like them about equally (α ≈ 1) arrive together however long the street (N) is.
- Expected hands-on result: 45.5 % left after 1 × 30 mL, 36.4 % after 3 × 10 mL, 33.5 % after 6 × 5 mL; benzoic acid 98.0 / 96.2 / 43.8 / 0.8 % extracted at pH 2 / 4.2 / 6 / 8; van Deemter A 0.33 mm, B 5.98, C 0.040, u_opt 12.2 cm/s, H_min 1.31 mm, N ≈ 1530; at 2 u_opt 16 % fewer plates. Pencil: N 222 000, H 0.14 mm; N ≈ 135 000 for α = 1.02.
- Extension: show why the A term vanishes for an open tube and why the C term falls with smaller particles (preview of 19.4 and the module practical); let learners derive u_opt by differentiating.
