# Instructor notes — 19.1 UV-Vis spectrophotometry and Beer-Lambert

- Objective: the learner converts between T, A, ε (or a) and c; chooses wavelength, cell and dilution; explains chemical, bandwidth and stray-light deviations; finds the linear range from residuals; plans readings near A ≈ 0.4.
- Likely confusion: why the log appears (each layer removes the same fraction); that "absorbance 2" means 1 % of the light, not "twice as dark"; absorptivity in mg units versus molar absorptivity.
- Misconception: "a higher absorbance is always more precise because the signal is bigger". The error curve shows the opposite above about 1.
- Analogy: stray light is like a window left open in a dark room; however thick the curtains, the room never gets darker than the light through the window.
- Expected hands-on result: stray-light fit a = 0.160 L mg⁻¹ cm⁻¹, s = 0.40 %; ceiling 2.40; working range 0–6 mg/L (slope 0.1578, s_r 0.0020); diluted drink 64.4 ± 0.2 mg/L; error minimum 0.54 % at A = 0.4343. Sim: s = 1 % gives a ceiling of 2.00.
- Extension: add a 1 nm-wide polychromatic model (two wavelengths with different ε) to the script and show the negative deviation; or connect to 09.6, where the phone photos showed the same flattening.
