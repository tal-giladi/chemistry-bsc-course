# Instructor notes — 02.3 Integrals as accumulated change

- Objective: integral = accumulated change; isothermal work, ΔH from Cp(T); trapezoid and Simpson on data.
- Likely confusion: work sign conventions (by the gas vs on the system, IUPAC). Make the learner name the convention every time.
- Misconception: "Constant temperature means constant pressure." For an isothermal expansion p falls as 1/V.
- Analogy: a water meter integrates flow rate over time; the bill is the integral, not the flow.
- Expected hands-on result: exact work 2271.4 J; trapezoid 2295.5/2275.3/2271.5 J at 4/10/100 intervals; Simpson 2272.7 J at 4; ΔH(N2, 298→500 K) = 5911 J/mol vs 5879 with constant Cp; amount used ≈ 0.0971 mol/L both ways.
- Extension: compute ΔS = ∫Cp/T dT for N2 over the same range (≈ 15.1 J/(mol K)) and compare with Cp ln(T2/T1).
