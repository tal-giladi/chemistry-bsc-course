# Instructor notes — 02.4 Series and approximations

- Objective: Taylor series as the origin of every chemistry shortcut; error estimate from the first dropped term; small-x for weak acids.
- Likely confusion: why the 5 % rule (x/C < 0.05) corresponds to C/Ka > 400. Show x/C ≈ sqrt(Ka/C) and square it.
- Misconception: "Weak acid means the shortcut always works." It fails for dilute solutions and for moderately weak acids (formic acid at 0.01 M).
- Analogy: zooming in on a smooth curve on a map until it looks like a straight road (one term), then a gentle bend (two terms).
- Expected hands-on result: shortcut error +0.7 % at 0.1 M, +2.2 % at 0.01 M, +6.9 % at 1e-3 M, +23 % at 1e-4 M; x/C < 5 % above about 6.9e-3 M (C/Ka ≈ 384, close to the 400 rule).
- Extension: add water autoionisation (charge balance x = Ka C/(Ka + x) + Kw/x) and find where it matters (below about 1e-6 M for acetic acid). Preview of Module 8/9.
