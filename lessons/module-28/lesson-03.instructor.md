# Instructor notes — 28.3 Linear free-energy relationships: Hammett

- Objective: the learner builds σ from benzoic acid pKa values, fits ρ with uncertainty for phenols and anilinium ions from the IUPAC dataset, predicts pKa values and relative rates (also for two substituents), and knows when σ+ / σ− are needed and when plots break.
- Likely confusion: σ belongs to the substituent, ρ to the reaction; log₁₀ versus ln; why σm(OCH₃) is positive while σp is negative (field versus resonance).
- Misconception: "a Hammett plot must be straight for every substituent". Curvature or a break often carries the mechanistic information (σ+ / σ− or a change of rate-determining step). Second: "ρ is a fixed property of a reaction type" — it depends on solvent, temperature and the substituent set (the anilinium fit gives 3.07 here against 2.77 in the Reusch table).
- Analogy: σ is a substituent's "pull" measured on a standard spring (benzoic acid); ρ is how stiff the spring of a new reaction is compared with the standard.
- Expected hands-on result: 55 compounds extracted from IUPAC v2.4; σ from benzoic acids agree with Hansch to 0.011 (max 0.03); benzoic slope 1.016 ± 0.013; phenol ρ = 2.00 ± 0.14 (n = 15, R² 0.94); anilinium ρ = 3.07 ± 0.14 (n = 17, R² 0.97); effective σ⁻ p-NO₂ 1.42 (phenol) / 1.17 (anilinium), p-CN 1.01 / 0.93, p-COCH₃ 0.99. Worked: 8.43, 8.73, 2.94 against 8.18, 8.63, 2.97; ester factors 96, 0.21, 460. Pencil: ρ ≈ −1.9, σ+ for p-OCH₃ (30×); phenylacetic ρ < 1; σ = 0.39 → m-Br/m-Cl about 9–10×, m-CF₃ 12×.
- Extension: fit the phenol series with a Yukawa–Tsuno-type term for the para acceptors, or extract substituted pyridinium ions from the IUPAC file and test whether σ works for a heteroaromatic ring. If the download fails (no internet), the learner can still do Part B and lesson 28.5; mention that the extraction rules are written at the top of `fetch_iupac_pka.py`.
