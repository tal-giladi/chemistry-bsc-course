# Instructor notes — 17.2 Infrared spectroscopy: functional groups

- Objective: the learner estimates wavenumbers with Hooke's law, assigns the diagnostic bands (O–H, N–H, C–H either side of 3000, C≡N, C≡C, C=O, C=C, C–O, NO₂), tells the carbonyl classes apart, and checks the phase of a spectrum before comparing.
- Likely confusion: the drawing convention (wavenumber decreasing to the right, transmittance pointing down); alcohol vs acid O–H shapes; why a polar C=O is strong and an internal C≡C nearly invisible.
- Misconception: "one band, one group". Decisions need partners (C=O + broad O–H = acid; C=O + C–O = ester). Second misconception: NIST and SDBS spectra of the same compound "should look the same"; NIST IR is mostly gas phase.
- Analogy: a molecule is a set of springs with masses; heavy masses and soft springs hum low, light masses and stiff springs hum high.
- Expected hands-on result: C–H ≈ 3020, C=O ≈ 1720, C≡N ≈ 2200 cm⁻¹, ratio 1.36; classifier A ketone, B acid, C alcohol, D nitrile, E terminal alkyne, F ester. NIST gas: 2-hexanone C=O ≈ 1730; butanoic acid free O–H ≈ 3580 and C=O ≈ 1780; propanenitrile ≈ 2260; 1-octyne ≈ 3330 and ≈ 2120. SDBS liquid: butanoic acid broad 2500–3300 and ≈ 1710; 1-butanol broad ≈ 3330 and C–O ≈ 1060.
- Extension: predict the C=O shift of acetophenone relative to 2-butanone (conjugation, ≈ 1685 vs ≈ 1715) and check both in SDBS by name; or compute the CO₂ asymmetric stretch from its normal-mode formula (14.4) and compare with 2349 cm⁻¹.
