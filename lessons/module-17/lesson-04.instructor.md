# Instructor notes — 17.4 Carbon NMR and 2D NMR basics

- Objective: the learner counts ¹³C lines from symmetry, places them in shift ranges, sorts carbons with DEPT-90/135, and knows which question COSY, HSQC and HMBC answer.
- Likely confusion: why ¹³C lines are singlets (decoupling) and why their heights mean little; the DEPT-135 sign convention; HSQC (one bond) vs HMBC (two to three bonds).
- Misconception: "a small line is less than one carbon" or "the carbonyl must be an impurity because it is small". Quaternary carbons are always weak.
- Analogy: the ¹³C spectrum is a guest list (who is there), DEPT tells you how many hydrogens each guest brought, and 2D spectra are the seating plan (who sits next to whom).
- Expected hands-on result: counts 4, 4, 3, 2, 2, 4, 3 for the C₄H₁₀O isomers and 3, 4, 2 for the dichlorobenzenes; P–T = 2-butanol, 2-methyl-2-propanol, 2-methyl-1-propanol, 2-methoxypropane, 1,2-dichlorobenzene. SDBS: 2-butanol ≈ 10, 23, 32, 69; MTBE ≈ 27, 49, 73 (quaternary weakest); 1,2-DCB three lines, 1,4-DCB two.
- Extension: predict the HMBC correlations that separate butyl acetate from propyl propanoate, or ask which 2D experiment would show the connection between the two halves of 4'-methoxyacetophenone (HMBC from OCH₃ to the ring C–O carbon).
