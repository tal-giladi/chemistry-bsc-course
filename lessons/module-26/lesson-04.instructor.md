# Instructor notes — 26.4 Metalloenzymes, electron transfer and respiration (module practical)

- Objective: the learner explains Lewis-acid catalysis by Zn²⁺ in carbonic anhydrase, uses the Marcus equation with a tunnelling distance term, computes respiratory-chain free energies from $E^{\circ\prime}$, and completes the module practical: Hill fits of the standard O₂ curve, cooperativity energy from the Hill-plot ends, O₂ delivery with and without cooperativity and Bohr shift, and the electron current and power of respiration.
- Likely confusion: $\lambda$ versus $\Delta G^\ddagger$; edge-to-edge versus centre-to-centre distance; $E^\circ$ at pH 0 versus $E^{\circ\prime}$ at pH 7; mL STPD and the 22.414 L/mol conversion; the meaning of tiny fit errors on smoothed data.
- Misconception: "more driving force is always faster" (inverted region, quiz q2). Second: "zinc enzymes are redox enzymes". Third: "the Hill fit is good because R² is high" — the residual wave says otherwise.
- Analogy: electron transfer is a relay race with the runners standing at most about 20 Å apart; each handover is fastest when the runners are at the same height of a gentle ramp (small λ, driving force matched), and a runner who must jump down a cliff (too much driving force) stumbles.
- Expected hands-on result: Part A — E(O₂/H₂O, pH 7) 0.815 V; steps −73.3, −36.7, −109.2 kJ/mol, total −219.2 (per NADH), −438 per O₂; optimum rates 1.1e12/4.5e9/5.6e7/7.6e4/3.1e2 s⁻¹ at 5/10/14/20/25 Å; cyt c → a 6.5e4 s⁻¹. Part B — Hill all points P50 26.38 ± 0.11, n 2.647 ± 0.026, RMS 0.95 %; 20–80 % P50 26.62 ± 0.04, n 2.629 ± 0.015; Severinghaus eq RMS 0.30 %; worst misfit at 8 mmHg (6.7 vs 4.1 %); K_T 166, K_R 1.40 mmHg, ratio 118, 12.3 kJ/mol; Ca 19.9, Cv 15.1 mL/dL, VO₂ 238 mL/min; release 22.8/18.9/3.3 % (100→40) and 65.4/36.0/8.3 % (100→20); pH 7.20 P50 32.9, S(20) 32 → 21 %; current 68 A, power 78 W.
- Extension: fit an MWC model (L, K_R, c) to the standard curve and compare K_T = K_R/c with the end-constant estimate; or change the Hb concentration to 10 g/dL (anaemia) and find the cardiac output needed for the same VO₂.
