# Instructor notes — 26.1 Metals in biology: why these elements

- Objective: the learner sorts bulk from trace metals and gives each a job, calculates free Fe³⁺ at a given pH from Ksp, explains siderophores, transferrin and ferritin, uses HSAB and the Irving–Williams series to match metals to donor sets, and reads donor sets from PDB structures.
- Likely confusion: availability versus abundance; $K_f$ of enterobactin quoted for the fully deprotonated ligand versus the effective constant at pH 7; why the distance cutoff is longer for Ca²⁺ and for sulfur donors.
- Misconception: "copper binds best, so it must be the best metal". The Irving–Williams peak is a hazard that cells manage with chaperones. Second: "zinc enzymes do redox" — Zn²⁺ is d¹⁰ and redox-inert.
- Analogy: metal sites are locks and metal ions keys; the cell does not leave keys lying about (free Cu, free Fe), it hands each key to its lock with a chaperone.
- Expected hands-on result: Part A — Mb Fe CN 6 (N₅O, Fe–O 1.81 Å); CA II Zn CN 4 (N₃O, Zn–O 1.90 Å); calmodulin four Ca²⁺ each CN 7 all O (2.13–2.63 Å); plastocyanin Cu N₂S₂ (Cu–S(Met) 2.82 Å). Free Fe³⁺: 1e-3 M (pH 2), 1e-9 (pH 4), 1e-18 (pH 7), 5e-22 (pH 8.1); 6e-10 ions per 1 fL cell. Part B — a small grey patch under the magnet if the cereal uses reduced iron; nothing if it uses ferrous salts. Part C — pH 3; Zn²⁺ for Cys₂His₂.
- Extension: download 4HHB with `fetch_pdb.py` and compare the five-coordinate deoxy iron with 1A6M; or look up transferrin (PDB 1A8E) and find the Fe³⁺ donor set (two Tyr, Asp, His and carbonate) — check the entry first.
