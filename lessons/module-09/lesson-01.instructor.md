# Instructor notes — 09.1 Types of error, accuracy and precision

- **Objective:** classify errors; compute bias, relative error and recovery against a CRM and judge them with a z-score that includes the certificate's uncertainty; split constant from proportional error with a sample-size series.
- **Likely confusion:** expanded uncertainty $U$ (k = 2) versus standard uncertainty, and $s$ versus $s/\sqrt{n}$ in the z-score. Quiz q2 targets both.
- **Misconception:** "precise means accurate". Analyst B (s = 0.085 mg/L, bias −4.6 %) is the counter-example; let the learner find it before naming it.
- **Analogy:** darts. A tight group off the bullseye is precise but biased; a loose group centred on it is true but imprecise. A CRM is the bullseye you can actually see.
- **Expected hands-on result:** analyst A mean 25.21, s 0.64, z ≈ 0.7; analyst B mean 23.84, s 0.085, z ≈ 7.5. Sample-size series intercept +0.62 ± 0.29 mg, slope 598.5 ± 1.6 mg/g (−1.33 %; true −1.5 % and +0.80 mg). Example coins: intercept +0.26 ± 0.04 g, slope 3.502 ± 0.006 g/coin. Learners' own scales: intercept usually within ±0.1 g; slope within about 1 % of 3.5 g.
- **Extension:** weigh the coins on a second scale and compare slopes — the coin mass is now common to both, so the ratio of slopes is a pure ratio of scale gains. Or ask a pharmacy to weigh one sealed item on its calibrated scale and use it as a home "CRM".
- **Scope:** the coin experiment trains the reasoning, not real balance calibration with OIML class weights.
