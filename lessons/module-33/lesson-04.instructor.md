# Instructor notes — 33.4 Getting feedback and what comes next

- Objective: the learner finishes the project package (package check, cold read, peer review, reader request, archive) and chooses a realistic next step. This lesson is the module practical.
- Likely confusion: what a reader's comment does and does not certify; which data may be re-shared (licence follows the source); Zenodo versus a preprint server.
- Misconception: "no reply means the work is bad"; "an expert will want the whole report"; "archiving is only for published papers".
- Analogy: asking an expert for feedback is like asking a stranger for directions. "Where is everything?" gets a shrug; "Is this the bus to Haifa?" gets an answer.
- Expected hands-on result: exercise on `runs/demo-project` 10 PASS, 2 FAIL (the TODO checks); solution 12 passed, 0 failed; fresh empty project 4 failures (raw data, poster, feedback note, 0 h logged). The learner's own package ends at 0 failed.
- Facts checked 2026-10-17: ORCID free for researchers; Zenodo run by CERN and OpenAIRE, DataCite DOIs; ChemRxiv FAQ (preprints before peer review, curation by PhD chemists; the FAQ page blocked automated reading, so eligibility of unaffiliated authors was not confirmed — the lesson does not claim either way); ICS 89th annual meeting 10–11 Feb 2026, Tel Aviv University, undergraduate membership 50 NIS; Open University of Israel open undergraduate admission per its English FAQ (seen via search; its current English site did not state it, so the lesson says "check its current rules").
- The learner sends e-mails from their own account; the course never sends anything on their behalf.
- Extension: the learner reviews a second peer's project with the five-part form and compares their verdict with another reviewer's.
