# Instructor notes — 33.3 Writing the report and the poster

- Objective: the learner writes a report built around one result (title, five-part abstract, claim-first results, limitations of data, method and setting) and an A0 poster that passes the ten-second test and the A4 print test.
- Likely confusion: writing order versus reading order; how strong a word ("shows", "suggests") a result supports; why a parity plot hides a percentage error.
- Misconception: "a longer report is a better report"; "limitations weaken the work"; "a poster is the report in large print".
- Analogy: the title and abstract are a shop window; the reader decides from the window whether to go in.
- Expected hands-on result: `lesson03_poster_figure_solution.py` on the generated demo data: 90 modes, scale factor 0.9621, RMS 91.9 → 12.5 cm-1, title "Scaling by 0.962 cuts the RMS error from 92 to 12 cm-1", `runs/poster_residuals.png` 22 × 18 cm at 300 dpi with 24 pt labels; unscaled residuals rise from about +30 to +150 cm-1.
- The Noa numbers in the worked example are illustrative, not measured; say so if a learner asks to cite them.
- Sizes: A0 841 × 1189 mm (ISO 216:2007, current on 2026-10-17); font sizes and the 300–800 word target are rules of thumb.
- Extension: have the learner present the poster in three minutes to someone outside chemistry and record which question they asked first; that question usually shows what the poster fails to say.
