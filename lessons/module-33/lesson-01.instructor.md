# Instructor notes — 33.1 Choosing a question you can answer at home

- Objective: the learner turns a topic into an answerable question (number or yes/no, population, reference, possible surprise), tests it against data/tools/safety/hours, budgets 180 h by phase and writes the one-page proposal.
- Likely confusion: attended time versus machine time; why a rerun factor is needed; that a licence is part of the data check.
- Misconception: "a bigger question is a better project"; "research means nobody has ever done anything like it" (a well-designed benchmark of a known method on a new population is real research); "no supervisor means no review".
- Analogy: the question is the address you type into a navigation app. "Somewhere in the north" gives no route; a street and a number do.
- Expected hands-on result: `lesson01_budget_solution.py` defaults print 6.5 h attended, 16.2 h machine (2.0 days), VERDICT feasible; 200 items × 15 min × 1.3 = 65 h > 50 h planned. The proposal fills every template line, names a licence and date, and has a plan B.
- Technion facts (SAP mirror, courses_2025_200, read 2026-10-17): 01240353, 4 points, 180 h in a research lab at an academic institution or approved company, prior approval, final report approved by the researcher, handed in the semester after; 75 points accumulated. 01240355: 6 points, about 240 h in a faculty group, proposal abstract approved by the supervisor, poster at a faculty forum, report.
- Extension: ask the learner to write the opposite of their expected result and say what decision it would change; if it changes nothing, the question is weak.
