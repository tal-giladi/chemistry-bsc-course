# Instructor notes — 13.3 The second law and entropy

- Objective: ΔS from reversible paths (heating, phase change, isothermal gas, mixing); ΔS_total as the criterion for irreversibility; Carnot limit; third-law entropies; state-function check with NIST data.
- Likely confusion: using the real heat of an irreversible process in q/T (free expansion is the classic test), and forgetting ΔS_surr.
- Misconception: "entropy is disorder" taken literally (ice cubes in a glass look "disordered" but have lower entropy than the water). Prefer "number of accessible arrangements of energy and matter"; Module 21 makes it quantitative.
- Analogy: entropy as the "price tag" of heat — the same 1 kJ of heat buys more entropy in a cold place than in a hot one, which is why heat flows hot → cold and why a Carnot engine must dump some heat.
- Expected hands-on result: rubber band clearly warm on fast stretch, cool on fast release (a few kelvin). Mixing: measured final T 1–3 °C below the mean in an ordinary mug; ΔS_total(ideal) +1.71 J/K for 60/20 °C. Script: 40.00 °C, −25.90/+27.61/+1.71 J/K; ΔS heating water 298→373 K 16.931 (constant Cp 16.913); ΔvapS° route A 118.89, route B 147.59 − 28.71 = 118.88 J/(mol K). Heat pump COP limit 18.
- Data note: route B uses ΔvapH(298) from ΔfH(g) − ΔfH(l) (ideal-gas standard state), and the Antoine fit valid 273–303 K. The vapour is treated as ideal at 0.03 bar, which is excellent.
- Extension: compute the Trouton ratio ΔvapH/Tb for water (109 J/(mol K)) and compare with the ~88 of non-hydrogen-bonded liquids; discuss why hydrogen bonding raises it.
