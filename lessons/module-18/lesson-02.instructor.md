# Instructor notes — 18.2 Solid structures and lattice energy

- Objective: packing (ccp/hcp/bcc), the four ionic structures, counting ions per cell and density; lattice energy from Born–Landé, Born–Mayer and Kapustinskii; the Born–Haber cycle with NIST/CODATA data; using estimates to decide whether a compound exists.
- Likely confusion: the sign convention (positive here, "energy to break the lattice"); atomisation enthalpy vs bond enthalpy; r0 vs a; whether the cycle gives an energy or an enthalpy (it is an enthalpy: ΔH = ΔU + Δn RT, 2RT = 4.96 kJ/mol for MX).
- Misconception: "ionic compounds form because electron transfer is downhill". Na + Cl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ costs +147 kJ/mol in the gas phase; the lattice pays. Second EA of O is the extreme case.
- Analogy: Born–Haber is a hiking trip with an altimeter: you cannot measure the height of the cliff (lattice) directly, but you can walk around it on trails whose ups and downs you have measured.
- Expected hands-on result: Born–Haber NaCl 787.2, KCl 717.2, LiF 1047.7, CaF₂ 2641.4 kJ/mol; Born–Mayer + νRT 760.6, 692.3, 1003.6, 2539.9 (ratios 0.966, 0.965, 0.958, 0.962; mean 0.963); Kapustinskii + νRT 751.2, 677.3, 965.7, 2645.5. Oxides: cycle without EA₂ 3045.3 (MgO), 2656.2 (CaO); EA₂ cost +816 (Born–Mayer, MgO) and +781 (Kapustinskii, CaO). Part A: NaCl₂ ΔfH ≈ +2240; MgCl −94; MgCl₂ −590; disproportionation −402 kJ/mol.
- Data note: the CaO lattice constant is deliberately left blank, so the script falls back to Kapustinskii; the learner sees that the structure-free estimate is good enough for a qualitative conclusion.
- Extension: add CsCl (M = 1.7627, a = 4.123 Å, ΔfH from the WebBook) and test the radius-ratio rule; or add the dispersion term C/r⁶ with a literature C and see how much of the 4 % gap it closes.
