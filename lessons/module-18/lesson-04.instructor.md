# Instructor notes — 18.4 Crystal field and ligand field theory

- Objective: octahedral, tetrahedral and square-planar splitting from orbital shapes; CFSE in Δ units and kJ/mol; the high/low-spin decision Δo vs P (with P reduced in complexes); the spectrochemical series and its LFT (π donor/acceptor) explanation; Jahn–Teller distortion.
- Likely confusion: barycentre bookkeeping (+0.6/−0.4 and the tetrahedral −0.6/+0.4); "CFSE" vs the total LS–HS energy difference (pairing terms); which d counts have a spin choice.
- Misconception: "strong-field ligands are the most negatively charged". CN⁻ and CO are strong because of π back-bonding; F⁻ is weak because of π donation.
- Analogy: the barycentre is a see-saw: three children (t₂g) slightly down balance two (e_g) higher up.
- Expected hands-on result: with P = 0.8 P_free all 8 spin choices match (CoF₆³⁻ HS, Co(H₂O)₆³⁺ LS, Co(NH₃)₆³⁺ LS, Rh and Ir ammines LS, Co(NH₃)₆²⁺ HS, Fe(CN)₆⁴⁻ and ³⁻ LS); with 1.0, 6 of 8 (both Co(III) aqua and ammine wrongly HS). CFSE: Ti(H₂O)₆³⁺ −96, CrF₆³⁻ −216, Cr(H₂O)₆³⁺ −250, Cr(CN)₆³⁻ −382, Fe(CN)₆⁴⁻ −924 kJ/mol. Worked example: LS–HS +139 kJ/mol (CoF₆³⁻), −95 kJ/mol (Co(NH₃)₆³⁺).
- Data note: Δ values come from one consistent lecture table (agreeing with OpenStax where they overlap); pairing energies are free-ion values from LibreTexts. The 0.8 factor is a teaching rule of thumb and is labelled as such in the lesson.
- Extension: find a spin-crossover compound (for example an Fe(II) tris(pyrazolyl)borate or [Fe(phen)₂(NCS)₂]) in the literature and estimate how close Δo and P must be for a switch near room temperature (ΔG = ΔH − TΔS with the spin entropy R ln 5 for S = 2).
