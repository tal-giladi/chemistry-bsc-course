# Instructor notes — 18.1 Periodic trends in depth and the main-group elements

- Objective: Slater Z_eff as a number (including the 4s-before-3d removal for Fe and the Ga d-block contraction); electron affinity and its three irregularities; first-row anomaly, diagonal relationship, inert-pair effect; acid–base character of oxides; a kitchen test of a basic hydroxide and an acidic oxide.
- Likely confusion: comparing Z_eff across shells (Cl 6.10 > F 5.20 but F has the higher IE); the EA sign convention (this course: positive = energy released, as on the NIST/primary-data tables).
- Misconception: "transition metals lose electrons in reverse filling order" and "PCl₅ uses 3d orbitals". Both are worth one minute of explicit correction.
- Analogy: shielding as people standing between you and a campfire: those right beside you (same group) block a little, those closer to the fire (inner shells) block almost all of it.
- Expected hands-on result: Part B Na 2.20, Fe 3d 6.25, Fe 4s 3.75, Ga 5.00 vs Al 3.50; r = 0.965 (period 2) and 0.949 (period 3). Part C: milk of magnesia blue-green, breath slightly redder purple (takes 1–2 min; dilute indicator works better), bicarbonate blue and nearly unchanged.
- Extension: compute Slater Z_eff for 3d electrons from Sc to Zn and relate the rise to the shrinking radii of M²⁺ ions; or look up the Pauling electronegativities of group 14 and show the dip at Ge (d-block contraction again).
