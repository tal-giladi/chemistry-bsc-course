# Instructor notes — 18.5 Colour and magnetism of transition-metal complexes (module practical)

- Objective: colour as the complement of the absorbed band; Δo from the lowest band of octahedral d¹, d³, d⁸, d⁹; band intensity from the Laporte and spin rules; charge transfer for d⁰/d¹⁰ and very intense bands; spin-only moments and spin-state assignment. Module practical: nine complex cards, prediction table first, then code and a one-page report.
- Likely confusion: absorbed vs seen colour; reading Δo off the first band of d² or d⁷ ions (needs Tanabe–Sugano, Module 20); counting "BM = number of unpaired electrons".
- Misconception: "intense colour means a big Δ" (intensity is about selection rules, position is about Δ); "d⁰ compounds are colourless" (permanganate, chromate).
- Analogy: a coloured solution is a white-light sieve: the band is the hole size, ε is how many holes there are.
- Expected hands-on result: Part A: Δo 9 090 cm⁻¹ = 109 kJ/mol; 600 nm absorbed → blue; Co(II) 4.6 BM → 3 unpaired, high spin with orbital contribution. Part B script: d = 1, 3, 3, 6, 6, 5, 5, 0, 9; n = 1, 3, 3, 4, 0, 1, 5, 0, 1; spin: Fe(H₂O)₆²⁺ HS, Fe(CN)₆⁴⁻ LS, Fe(CN)₆³⁻ LS, Mn(H₂O)₆²⁺ HS, others "only one"; Δo 20 040, 17 391, 15 060 cm⁻¹; CT for cards 6 and 8; colour agreement 8 of 9 (card 7 flagged, a deliberate discussion point).
- Assessing the report: look for one explicit link per card to an earlier lesson (18.1 for 4s-first counting, 18.3 for oxidation state, 18.4 for spin), and an honest "what this does not train" paragraph.
- Extension: take the measured spectrum of a complex from an open-access paper (or Module 25's phone spectrometer later), convert the bands to cm⁻¹ and decide whether they are d–d or CT from their ε.
