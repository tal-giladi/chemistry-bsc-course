# Instructor notes — 18.3 Coordination compounds: geometry and isomers

- Objective: read a complex (sphere charge, oxidation state, d count, CN, geometry), name it by IUPAC 2005, classify and count isomers by symmetry, and quantify the chelate effect with ΔG° = −RT ln K.
- Likely confusion: ligands vs donor atoms (CN of [Co(en)₃]³⁺ is 6); "-ido" (IUPAC 2005) vs "-o" (older books, OpenStax) ligand names; why d⁸ Pt(II) is square planar (deferred to 18.4).
- Misconception: "cis and trans are the same molecule drawn differently" and "chelates bind better because the bonds are stronger". Both are tested in the quiz.
- Analogy: a chelating ligand is a person holding a railing with two hands: if one hand slips, the other keeps them in place long enough to grab again.
- Expected hands-on result: Part B counts MA₆ 1, MA₅B 1, MA₄B₂ 2, MA₃B₃ 2, MA₂B₂C₂ 6/5 (1 pair), M(AA)₃ 2/1, M(AA)₂B₂ 3/2, M(AA)B₄ 1, MABCDEF 30/15, MA₂B₂CD 8/6. Part C: ferrous sulfate or fumarate turns strong black tea blue-grey/black within 1–2 min; one teaspoon of lemon juice lightens it noticeably within 2 min. Bisglycinate reacts slowly. Milky tea or very hard water masks the effect.
- Safety: the iron-tablet warning must be read aloud in a group setting; one tablet only.
- Extension: extend the isomer counter to square-planar complexes (the 8 operations of a square, plus the perpendicular flip) and confirm MA₂B₂ has 2 and MABCD has 3 isomers.
