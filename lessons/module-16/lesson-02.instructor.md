# Instructor notes — 16.2 Aldehydes, ketones and nucleophilic addition

- Objective: carbonyl electrophilicity; aldehyde > ketone reactivity from hydration constants; hydride, Grignard, amine (imine/enamine), alcohol (hemiacetal/acetal) and Wittig additions; Grignard disconnections; acetal protection.
- Likely confusion: "base-promoted" (strong nucleophile, irreversible, acid work-up afterwards) vs "acid-catalysed" (weak nucleophile, reversible). Have learners label every arrow-pushing scheme with one or the other before drawing.
- Misconception: hydrate or acetal formation as a dead end. Stress reversibility: formalin still behaves as formaldehyde, acetals come off with aqueous acid. This sets up glucose (16.6).
- Analogy: the carbonyl carbon as a parking spot under a roof (the oxygen pulls electron density away): small cars (H) park easily, two big cars (alkyl groups) crowd it, CF₃ groups make it extra inviting.
- Expected hands-on result: hydrate 99.9999 % (hexafluoroacetone), 99.90 %, 50.0 %, 0.0999 %. 2-phenyl-2-butanol: PhBr + butanone, CH₃Br + propiophenone, EtBr + acetophenone, all "checks". 4-phenyl-2-butanol: two pairs. Drill 5/5. Part C: protect, 2 CH₃MgBr, deprotect.
- Extension: ask why the Wittig reaction is preferred over dehydration of the Grignard alcohol to make methylenecyclohexane (dehydration of 1-methylcyclohexanol gives mainly the more substituted 1-methylcyclohexene, Zaitsev, lesson 15.6).
