# Instructor notes — 16.5 Aromaticity and electrophilic aromatic substitution

- Objective: aromatic stabilisation from hydrogenation data; Hückel 4n+2 for rings, ions and heterocycles; EAS mechanism and electrophiles; directing effects; order of steps.
- Likely confusion: pyridine vs pyrrole lone pairs. Make learners draw the p orbital on each N: in pyridine the N already uses its p orbital for the C=N π bond, so the lone pair sits in an sp² orbital in the plane.
- Misconception: "deactivating means meta-directing". Halogens are the counterexample; walk through the σ-complex resonance forms for para attack on chlorobenzene.
- Analogy: the σ-complex as a bridge with a weak span: groups that can lean in (lone pairs) hold up the span under the ortho/para positions; electron-pulling groups make it sag there, so traffic goes meta.
- Expected hands-on result: π counts 6 (benzene, C₅H₅⁻, C₇H₇⁺, pyridine, pyrrole, furan, thiophene), 2 (C₃H₃⁺), 4 and 8 (antiaromatic if planar), None for cyclopentadiene; all agree with RDKit flags. Nitration predictions: toluene o/p, nitrobenzene m, chlorobenzene o/p, 4-methylanisole ortho to OMe, benzoic acid m. Drill 5/5. Part C orders: NO₂ then Br; Br then NO₂ (separate para); acyl then Br.
- Safety framing: nitration is taught on paper only and only as single nitration of simple arenes; do not extend drills to polynitration.
- Extension: ask learners to predict and then check with the script where bromination of 4-nitrotoluene goes (ortho to CH₃ = meta to NO₂: the two groups agree).
