# Instructor notes — 16.7 Retrosynthesis and multi-step planning

- Objective: disconnections, synthons, reagents, FGI and protecting groups; ordering by directing effects, incompatibilities and chemoselectivity; judging routes by longest linear sequence; the module practical (two verified routes with written justification).
- Likely confusion: synthon vs reagent. Insist on writing both for every disconnection (R⁻ = RMgBr; ⁺CH(OH)R = RCHO).
- Misconception: "the checker passed, so the route works". It is a teaching model of reaction types; it knows no yields, conditions or purification. Ask learners to name one practical problem per route anyway (hazards, separations such as ortho/para isomers).
- Analogy: retrosynthesis as route-finding on a map from the destination back to the nearest station (the catalogue); the order of steps is the timetable: right trains, wrong order, wrong city.
- Expected hands-on result: `py lesson07_practical_solution.py` shows 6 passing routes (W1, W2, W3, P1, P2, P2 alternative) and 2 failing ones with the Friedel–Crafts and wrong-position messages. Learner routes may differ; accept any route the checker passes plus a correct justification. P1 is essentially unique within the catalogue (acylation, meta nitration, NaBH₄); P2 has at least two (aldol/H₂/Grignard; Grignard/PBr₃/Grignard).
- Marking the practical: trees with ≥2 first-level options (30 %), route files pass (30 %), order/chemoselectivity justification names the actual effect (25 %), failure analysis and scoring (15 %).
- Safety framing: all targets are fragrance or teaching compounds; paracetamol is a classic teaching acylation. Do not set targets outside the classic teaching set.
- Extension: add 4-bromobenzoic acid's isomer, 3-bromobenzoic acid, as a target and compare; or ask for a convergent plan to 2-methyl-4-phenylbutan-2-ol and its longest linear sequence.
