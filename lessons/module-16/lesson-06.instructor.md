# Instructor notes — 16.6 Amines, sugars and heterocycles

- Objective: amine basicity (pKaH) and protonation at pH 7.4; amine synthesis; glucose as a cyclic hemiacetal, anomers, mutarotation, glycosides, reducing sugars; basicity and reactivity of pyridine, pyrrole, imidazole; the Maillard reaction.
- Likely confusion: pKa of the amine vs pKa of its conjugate acid. Insist on writing "pKaH" and "% BH⁺" every time.
- Misconception: "β is more stable, so it should be 100 %". ΔG° = −1.4 kJ/mol gives only 64 : 36. Link back to K = exp(−ΔG°/RT) (08.3) and to methylcyclohexane (15.2).
- Analogy: the anomeric carbon as a revolving door: the open-chain aldehyde is the moment the door is open, rare but enough to let molecules switch sides.
- Expected hands-on result: kitchen: milk-brushed and baking-soda slices browner than the water control; qualitative only. Python: imidazole 26.2 %, ethylamine 99.96 %, aniline 0.17 %, pyridine 0.70 %, ammonia 98.6 %; 5/5/4 stereocentres; fit a_eq 52.6, a0 112.1, k 0.0119 min⁻¹, t½ 58 min, 64 % β. Drill 5/5.
- Data note: the mutarotation file is generated (make_data.py, k = 0.0120 min⁻¹ is illustrative). Learners with access to a polarimeter (some school labs) can use their own CSV with columns t_min, alpha_deg.
- Safety: Part A is the only home activity in the module; check the toaster rule is read (no metal in the toaster).
- Extension: ask why fructose, a ketose, also mutarotates and forms a five-membered furanose in sucrose; then why sucrose is non-reducing.
