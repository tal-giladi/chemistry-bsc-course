# Instructor notes — 16.4 Enolates and the aldol reaction

- Objective: keto–enol tautomerism; α-H acidity and the choice of base; kinetic vs thermodynamic enolates; alkylation, malonic and acetoacetic ester syntheses; aldol, crossed aldol, Claisen, Michael, Robinson.
- Likely confusion: "if NaOH makes only 0.05 % enolate, how can the aldol work?" The enolate is consumed and regenerated continuously; an aldol needs enolate and carbonyl together, so a small steady fraction is ideal. Alkylation needs the opposite (no free carbonyl), hence LDA.
- Misconception: drawing the enolate as attacking through oxygen. Enolates are ambident, but with carbon electrophiles (alkyl halides, carbonyls) they react at carbon in the cases taught here.
- Analogy: donor and acceptor roles as dance partners: every enolisable aldehyde can lead or follow, so a mixed room makes four pairings; a partner who can only follow (benzaldehyde) gives one.
- Expected hands-on result: K(NaOEt, acetone) = 5.0e-4, K(LDA) = 5.0e16. Enolisable positions: acetone two CH₃; benzaldehyde and pivalaldehyde none; 2-methylcyclohexanone CH (1 H) and CH₂ (2 H); ethyl acetate one CH₃. Acetaldehyde + propanal 4 products; benzaldehyde + acetone 1; acetaldehyde + pivalaldehyde 2. Drill 5/5.
- Extension: aldolase uses an enamine (lysine + carbonyl, lesson 16.2) instead of an enolate. Ask learners to draw the retro-aldol of fructose 1,6-bisphosphate with an enamine and find the C3–C4 bond that breaks.
