# Instructor notes — 16.1 Alcohols, ethers and oxidation

- Objective: oxidation levels of carbon; oxidation of 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols with dry mild vs aqueous strong oxidants; OH activation (PBr₃, SOCl₂, acid); Williamson ether synthesis; epoxide opening under base and acid.
- Likely confusion: "why does PCC stop at the aldehyde?" The answer (no water, no hydrate) only makes sense after hydrates in 16.2; tell learners it will come back, and return to it there.
- Misconception: oxidation level as a property of a molecule ("ethanol is at −1"). It belongs to each carbon; ethanol has a −3 and a −1 carbon. The lab prints per-atom lists on purpose.
- Analogy: the oxidation ladder as floors of a building. Substitutions (alcohol ↔ bromide ↔ ether) are walking along a corridor; oxidants and reductants are the lift.
- Expected hands-on result: ladder −4, −2, 0, +2, +4; ethanol [−3, −1]; acetaldehyde [−3, +1]; acetic acid [−3, +3]; acetone [−3, +2, −3]; tert-butanol [−3, +1, −3, −3]. Alcohol classes: 1-butanol primary, 2-butanol secondary, tert-butanol tertiary (no reaction), cyclohexanol secondary, benzyl alcohol primary, methanol "methyl". Drill 5/5 with the worked answers. Epoxide: base gives COCC(C)(C)O, acid gives COC(C)(C)CO.
- Safety framing: Cr(VI) reagents are taught as the classic case only; mention that modern labs avoid them. Nothing in this lesson is done at home.
- Extension: ask for the balanced equation for methanol → formaldehyde with dichromate (2 e per CH₃OH: 3 CH₃OH + Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 8 H⁺ → 3 CH₂O + 2 Cr³⁺ + 7 H₂O), then compare with the industrial silver-catalysed dehydrogenation that needs no oxidant at all.
