# Instructor notes — 10.3 Precipitation and complexometric titrations

- Objective: exact pAg curve from Ksp; Mohr/Volhard/Fajans and their pH and solubility traps; α(Y⁴⁻) and K′f; total, Ca and Mg hardness. Covers the S3 experiments "precipitation titrations" and the complexometry half of "gravimetry and complexometry".
- Likely confusion: why the jump size depends on Ksp; why only Y⁴⁻ counts; why Mg sets the pH of a hardness titration and not Ca.
- Misconception: "back titrations are always more accurate" (Volhard has the AgCl/AgSCN trap); "hardness is calcium" (it is Ca + Mg as CaCO3).
- Analogy: EDTA as a claw that only opens fully at high pH; protons hold it shut.
- Expected hands-on result: pAg 8.14, 7.60, 5.87, 4.87, 3.87, 2.20; ideal chromate 6.67e-3 M; with 2.5 mM chromate 0.010 mL past V_eq; α(Y⁴⁻) at pH 10 = 0.365 (Harvey table 0.367); minimum pH 7.6 (Ca), 9.5 (Mg); worked hardness 257 mg/L. Pencil: 41.1 mg/L Cl⁻.
- Safety: no home version — AgNO3 (H272, H314, H410), K2CrO4 (H350) and ammonia buffers are lab reagents; say so plainly.
- Extension: compute the full EDTA titration curve of Ca²⁺ at pH 10 (pCa vs V) with K′f, and show how the jump shrinks at pH 8 and 7.
