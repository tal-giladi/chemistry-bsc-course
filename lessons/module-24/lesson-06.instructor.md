# Instructor notes — 24.6 Multi-step synthesis with spectra at every step

- Objective: module practical. The learner plans and runs benzaldehyde → benzoin → benzil → benzilic acid on the virtual bench, identifies every intermediate from mp, IR and ¹H NMR, quantifies a contaminant from integrals, computes step and overall yields and the starting amount for a target, and writes it up on the lab-report template.
- Likely confusion: the 2:1 stoichiometry of the benzoin condensation; why the oxidation can stall; how a phenyl group migrates in the benzilic acid rearrangement; scaling NMR integrals to a shared group.
- Misconception: "a high yield means a good step" (S2a, 91 %, is 20 mol% starting material). Second: "overall yield is the lowest step yield".
- Analogy: a multi-step synthesis is a relay race — a dropped baton (an impure intermediate) is not fixed by the next runner going faster.
- Expected hands-on result: Python S1 58.3 % benzoin PASS; S2a FAIL (benzil with about 20 mol% benzoin); S2b 83.5 % PASS; S3 74.6 % PASS; overall 36.3 %; 2.6 g benzaldehyde per 1.00 g benzilic acid. Sim: overall 30–45 %.
- Extension: replace step 3 by NaBH₄ reduction of benzil to hydrobenzoin (meso, mp 136–137 °C per the LibreTexts experiment) and predict the spectral change; or design a fourth step (benzil + dibenzyl ketone to tetraphenylcyclopentadienone) and list the check you would use.
