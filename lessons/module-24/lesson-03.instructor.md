# Instructor notes — 24.3 Checking purity: TLC and melting point

- Objective: the learner computes Rf, uses the co-spot to judge completion and identity, tunes eluent polarity, predicts melting-point depression from the ideal-solution equation, and interprets a mixed melting point. Home: paper chromatography of food colourings and markers with replicate Rf values.
- Likely confusion: polarity of stationary versus mobile phase (normal phase on silica: polar stays down); "melting range" versus "melting point"; why the first liquid appears at the eutectic, below the end of melting.
- Misconception: "Rf is a constant of the compound" — it depends on plate, eluent, chamber and temperature. Second: "an impurity raises the melting point" (it lowers it, except in solid solutions, which are rare in this course).
- Analogy: TLC is a race on a sticky track; polar runners keep stopping to shake hands with the silica.
- Expected hands-on result: Python depressions 0.7/1.5/3.7/7.5 K; example Rf 0.15, 0.47, 0.88. Home: composite colourings and black markers split into two or three spots; permanent marker barely moves in salt water; replicate Rf spread about ±0.03.
- Extension: put the same food colouring on paper and run it in plain water and in 0.4 % and 2 % salt water; explain the change in Rf with ionic strength (salt shields charged dye–cellulose interactions).
