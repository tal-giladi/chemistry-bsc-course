# Instructor notes — 24.2 Purification: recrystallisation and distillation

- Objective: the learner chooses a recrystallisation solvent from data, calculates minimum volume and maximum recovery, explains each procedural step, and uses Raoult + Antoine + Fenske to judge a distillation. Home: alum (or sugar) recrystallisation with recovery compared to the solubility limit.
- Likely confusion: "minimum solvent" versus the small practical excess needed for hot filtration; theoretical plates versus physical trays; why the head temperature stays near the low boiler's bp then rises.
- Misconception: "more solvent dissolves impurities better, so it gives a purer product" — it mainly costs yield. Second: "simple distillation separates any two liquids with different boiling points".
- Analogy: recrystallisation is a bouncer at a club door that only recognises one face: slow queuing (slow cooling) lets it check everyone; a rush (crash cooling) lets impurities in.
- Expected hands-on result: Python 40 mL; 89/88/78 %; 92.8 °C, 71 %; 87.2 °C, 86 %; α = 2.42; 3.3 plates. Home alum 50–75 % recovery, octahedral crystals, larger when cooled slowly; sugar gives almost no crystals on cooling.
- Extension: build the T–x–y diagram for ethanol–water from NIST Antoine constants and show that Raoult's law misses the azeotrope; discuss activity coefficients (13.6).
