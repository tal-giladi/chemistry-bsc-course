# Instructor notes — 24.4 Virtual synthesis: aspirin

- Objective: the learner explains the acid-catalysed acetylation and the reason for each step, computes limiting reagent, theoretical and percent yield and atom economy, judges purity from yield, mp and FeCl₃, and confirms the product with real IR (NIST) and ¹H NMR (SDBS, by name).
- Likely confusion: why the phenol, not the carboxylic acid, is acetylated; why the quench water is added slowly; why a negative FeCl₃ test does not prove identity.
- Misconception: "a higher crude yield is a better run" — the honest yield is of pure, dry product. Second: "the acid catalyst is used up".
- Analogy: the FeCl₃ test is a smoke detector — very sensitive to the one thing (free phenol) you want gone, blind to everything else.
- Expected hands-on result: 14.48 mmol salicylic acid, 53.0 mmol anhydride (3.7 eq), 2.61 g theoretical, atom economy 75.0 %; example verdicts 88.6 % impure, 73.6 % pure, 113 % wet. Sim: 70–80 % after recrystallisation, mp 134–136 °C. Spectra: aspirin C=O about 1750 and 1690 cm⁻¹; 3H singlet near 2.3 ppm.
- Extension: estimate the salicylic acid content of a crude sample from its NMR integrals (acetyl CH₃ versus aromatic H), then compare with the melting-point estimate from 24.3. Discuss why commercial aspirin is made from salicylic acid made by the Kolbe–Schmitt carboxylation of phenol.
