# Instructor notes — 24.5 Virtual synthesis: substitution, Grignard and aldol

- Objective: the learner explains mechanism, conditions and side reactions of 1-bromobutane (SN2), triphenylmethanol (Grignard) and dibenzalacetone (Claisen–Schmidt aldol), plans each work-up, computes amounts and the Grignard water budget, and diagnoses failed runs from yield, mp/bp and IR.
- Likely confusion: why concentrated H₂SO₄ removes butanol and ether (protonation makes them ionic); why the product layer switches; why the Grignard needs initiation; why benzaldehyde cannot self-condense.
- Misconception: "the organic layer stays on the same side through a work-up". Second: "a little water just slows a Grignard down" — it destroys reagent stoichiometrically.
- Analogy: a Grignard reagent is a very hungry base; give it any proton (water, alcohol, acid) and it eats that instead of the ketone.
- Expected hands-on result: Python 14.97 g, 5.20 g, 2.31 g; 70 % SN2; 19/37/185 mg water for 5/10/50 % loss. Sims: SN2 60–75 %, bp about 101 °C; dry Grignard 60–80 %, mp about 160 °C; wet/fast run below 40 % with more biphenyl; aldol 2:1 yellow solid mp about 110 °C, 1:1 mainly benzalacetone.
- Extension: replace 1-butanol with 2-methyl-2-propanol in the bench and predict the mechanism change (SN1, more elimination); compare the Grignard route to triphenylmethanol from methyl benzoate (two equivalents of PhMgBr).
