# Instructor notes — 24.1 Lab techniques: reflux, extraction and drying

- Objective: the learner gives the reason for every reflux and work-up step, identifies layers by density, plans an acid–base extraction with $D(\mathrm{pH})$, and knows what a dry organic solution looks like. Opens the module with the plain statement that this lab cannot be done at home.
- Likely confusion: partition coefficient versus distribution ratio (19.3); why a base moves an acid into the water (the anion is held by water); why NaHCO₃ rather than NaOH.
- Misconception: "the organic layer is always on top". Second: "boiling chips can be added when bumping starts" (never into a hot liquid).
- Analogy: the separatory funnel is a bouncer with a pH dial: turn the dial up and acids lose their right to stay in the ether.
- Expected hands-on result: Python prints 45.5 % vs 36.4 %; 98.7 % benzoic acid kept after three water washes, about 7e-4 % after three bicarbonate washes, 99.91 % recovered after acidifying. Kitchen jar: yellow oil on top, blue water below, slow separation with detergent.
- Extension: plan the work-up of a mixture of an amine, an acid and a neutral compound (three bottles: acid wash, base wash, neutral left). Link forward to 24.4 (aspirin is an ester that NaOH would hydrolyse) and 24.5 (1-bromobutane is the lower layer).
