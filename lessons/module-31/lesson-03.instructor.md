# Instructor notes — 31.3 Hartree-Fock and DFT: geometry optimisation

- Objective: the learner explains HF (mean field, no correlation) versus Kohn–Sham DFT (approximate exchange–correlation functional), places functionals on Jacob's ladder, separates method error from basis error, optimises water at four levels in ORCA and proves a minimum with frequencies, including repairing a false minimum.
- Likely confusion: why a bigger basis lowers the energy but can move a geometry away from experiment (HF); why DFT still uses orbitals; what "3c" adds (basis, D4, gCP).
- Misconception: "converged optimisation = minimum." Acetone from the MMFF94 start converges with xtb defaults onto a methyl-rotation saddle (−62 cm⁻¹). Second: "the lowest total energy identifies the best method" (energies are only variationally comparable within one method).
- Analogy: method error is the quality of the lens, basis error the number of pixels. More pixels never fix a blurred lens (HF limit), and a sharp lens on a 10-pixel sensor (good functional, minimal basis) also fails.
- Expected hands-on result: reference (PySCF 2.14.0) water r/angle: HF/SVP 0.9450/105.13, HF/TZVP 0.9420/106.51, B3LYP/SVP 0.9667/103.10, B3LYP/TZVP 0.9627/105.24; energies −75.961338, −76.059579, −76.358316, −76.462996 Eh. xtb tight 0.9593 Å, 107.22°. Acetone: first −13.534140 Eh, −62.4 cm⁻¹; restart from xtbhess.xyz with vtight −13.534178 Eh, lowest 71.8 cm⁻¹; 0.10 kJ/mol. ORCA runs were not made by the author; expect agreement with PySCF to about 0.002 Å, 0.3°, and energies to the third decimal.
- Extension: add r2SCAN-3c and PBE0-D4/def2-TZVP rows for water and formaldehyde and rank methods by mean absolute error against CCCBDB geometries; or time HF, B3LYP and r2SCAN-3c on pentane to see the cost differences.
