# Instructor notes — 31.1 Setting up Avogadro, xtb and ORCA

- Objective: the learner installs Avogadro 2.0.0, xtb 6.7.1 and ORCA 6.1.1 from official sources, knows the ORCA EULA limits (academic or private non-work use, no redistribution, citation), runs and reads a first xtb job, and applies the checking routine (termination, convergence, charge/multiplicity, imaginary frequencies, same model chemistry).
- Likely confusion: why total energies from xtb (−5.07 Eh for water) and DFT (about −76 Eh) differ so much; what the six near-zero "frequencies" are; that "normal termination" is not "converged".
- Misconception: "ORCA is free, so anyone can use it for anything." The EULA excludes work-related use, including by people whose work could benefit from it. Second: "a lower total energy means a better method" (only true for variational methods in the same family and basis, and never across xtb/DFT).
- Analogy: a model chemistry is a measuring instrument with its own zero. You can compare two readings from one balance after taring it, not a reading from one balance with a reading from another.
- Expected hands-on result: `check_setup.py` (xtb 6.7.1pre, Windows) prints converged, E = −5.070544 Eh, r(O–H) 0.9592 Å, angle 107.30°, frequencies 1538.8, 3643.1, 3651.6 cm⁻¹, IR intensities 133.3, 6.8, 16.7 km/mol, no imaginary modes; gap 14.39 eV. ORCA r2SCAN-3c water was not run by the author (no licence acceptance possible); expect E near −76.4 Eh, bend near 1600, stretches about 3700–3900 cm⁻¹. Worked example: 3.8e−5 Eh = 0.10 kJ/mol, Boltzmann factor 0.96; cost estimate 4–17 h for 40 atoms.
- Extension: run `xtb water.xyz --ohess --alpb water` (implicit water solvent) and see how geometry and frequencies change; or time xtb and ORCA r2SCAN-3c on a 20-atom molecule built in Avogadro and compare with the $N^3$–$N^4$ estimate.
