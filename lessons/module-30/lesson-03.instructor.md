# Instructor notes — 30.3 Speed of sound and the heat-capacity ratio of air

- Objective: the learner measures c in a closed tube with phyphox, removes the end correction with a length series, converts c to γ with the moist-air molar mass, and builds the error budget.
- Likely confusion: displacement vs pressure nodes at the two ends; why only odd harmonics; why γ (adiabatic) and not 1 (isothermal).
- Misconception: "the end correction is a small detail". It is 4 % in c and 8 % in γ for a short wide tube.
- Analogy: the open end is like a springboard that keeps bending past its edge; the wave "feels" a slightly longer board than you measure.
- Expected hands-on result (example data): peaks 168.8, 506.2, 843.6, 1181.1 Hz; all 15 resonances c = 345.44 ± 0.75 m/s, e = 11.0 ± 1.1 mm (theory 12.3); fundamentals only 345.3 ± 1.1; naive 332–340 m/s; M 28.812 g/mol; γ 1.3963 ± 0.0067; Cv 2.52 R; dry-air check 1.3997. Real home data usually give γ 1.38–1.42. Pencil: −0.7 %; +1.3 % (1.418); helium γ ≈ 1.64.
- Extension: measure c at two temperatures (a cold balcony and a warm room) and check c ∝ √T; or compare with the Clément–Desormes result of 23.1 in a short discussion of which method is limited by what.
