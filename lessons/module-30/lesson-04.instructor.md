# Instructor notes — 30.4 Electronic spectra of conjugated dyes: particle in a box (module practical)

- Objective: the learner applies Kuhn's model to the 1,1′-diethyl-2,2′-cyanines, fits ℓ and α with uncertainties and correlation, tests a blind prediction on cryptocyanine, explains the polyene failure, and writes the module report.
- Likely confusion: what p counts (carbons on the N…N path, including the ring carbons C2/C2′); N = p + 3; why α is in bonds.
- Misconception: "a good fit proves the model". Three points, two parameters, correlation −0.99.
- Analogy: calibrating α on one dye is like setting a bathroom scale with one weight; it can still be wrong for every other weight if its slope is off.
- Expected hands-on result: bare box 328 / 453 / 579 nm; α (one dye) 1.58 → 523 / 650 / 777; fit ℓ 0.1199 ± 0.0025 nm, α 2.77 ± 0.22, corr −0.988, residuals −2.6 / +5.4 / −2.8 nm; cryptocyanine predicted 795 vs 708 nm (+12 %), p_eff 7.1; β-carotene box 1341 nm, V0 1.91 eV. Kitchen extension: carrot oil yellow-orange, tomato oil redder. Sim part depends on quantum-box `dye` (not built yet; needs a "polyene alternation" switch).
- Data caveat: λmax values are from supplier specs and a textbook value (521–526, 602–610, 705–710 nm across sources). Encourage learners to rerun the fit with the other ends of these ranges and see that the conclusions do not change.
- Extension: add the 3,3′-diethylthia series (thiacarbocyanine 557 nm in MeOH, TCI) as a second family and fit its own α; or compute the dyes with TD-DFT in Module 31.
