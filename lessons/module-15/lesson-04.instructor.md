# Instructor notes — 15.4 Curved arrows, acids and bases in organic chemistry

- Objective: the learner draws correct curved arrows for proton transfer, nucleophilic attack, leaving-group loss and rearrangement; names nucleophiles/electrophiles (Lewis bases/acids); computes K for proton transfers from pKa; ranks acids by atom, resonance, induction and orbital.
- Likely confusion: arrows that start at H⁺ or a positive charge; which pKa goes first in K; water 15.7 versus 14.0; why HI is stronger than HF although F is more electronegative.
- Misconception: "arrows show atoms moving"; "lies to the left means no reaction" (enolate chemistry depends on tiny equilibrium amounts).
- Analogy: a curved arrow is a bank transfer slip: it says where the money (the electron pair) comes from and which account it lands in; the totals must balance.
- Expected hands-on result: log K acetic acid + HO⁻ 10.9 (ΔG° −62.4 kJ/mol); phenol + HO⁻ 5.7; acetic acid + HCO₃⁻ 1.6 (K 40); phenol + HCO₃⁻ −3.6; ethyne + HO⁻ −9.3; ethyne + NH₂⁻ +10.0; acetone + HO⁻ −4.3; NH₄⁺ + HO⁻ 6.4; HCl + H₂O 5.3. ARIO: CH₃SH > CH₃OH > CH₃NH₂; Cl₂CHCOOH > ClCH₂COOH > CH₃COOH; phenol > cyclohexanol.
- Extension: use the Bordwell DMSO table (Reich collection) to redo ethanol + amide in DMSO and discuss why pKa values change with solvent; or add Hammett-style substituted benzoic acids from PubChem and see induction fall off with distance (Module 28 returns to this).
