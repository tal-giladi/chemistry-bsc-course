# Instructor notes — 15.3 Stereochemistry and chirality

- Objective: the learner finds stereocentres, assigns R/S and E/Z with the CIP rules, classifies pairs (identical, enantiomers, diastereomers, constitutional isomers), recognises meso compounds, and computes specific rotation and ee.
- Likely confusion: first point of difference versus "biggest group"; duplicated atoms for C=O; reversing the answer when H points towards the viewer; L/D versus R/S.
- Misconception: "R means (+)"; "two stereocentres always means four stereoisomers"; "enantiomers have different boiling points".
- Analogy: hands and gloves. Two hands are identical in every measurement except handedness, and only a chiral object (a glove, a receptor) tells them apart.
- Expected hands-on result: carvone smells differ for most people (spearmint vs caraway). Models: 2-butanols do not superimpose; meso-2,3-dibromobutane mirror plane visible in the eclipsed conformation. Script: (R)-carvone R, L-alanine S, E/Z correct; pairs enantiomers, enantiomers, diastereomers, diastereomers, constitutional; tartaric acid and 2,3-dibromobutane 3 stereoisomers (one meso), 2-bromo-3-chlorobutane 4. Drill: (a) S, (b) R; aldotetrose 4 (erythrose, threose, D and L); cis-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane achiral (meso-like by ring flip).
- Extension: look up in PubChem the two enantiomers of ibuprofen or of limonene, read their SMILES and assign them by hand; or enumerate the stereoisomers of glucose's open chain (four stereocentres, 16) with `stereoisomers()`.
