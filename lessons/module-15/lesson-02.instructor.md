# Instructor notes — 15.2 Conformations and strain

- Objective: the learner draws Newman projections, ranks conformations by torsional and steric strain, converts energy gaps to populations (and back) with the Boltzmann factor, draws both chairs of substituted cyclohexanes and predicts the dominant one, and checks a force-field scan against measured values.
- Likely confusion: conformation versus configuration; the degeneracy factor 2 for gauche; which chair positions are "up" on carbons 1,2 versus 1,3 versus 1,4; why eclipsed forms are not populated.
- Misconception: "a ring flip turns cis into trans", and "the larger group always wins whatever the energies" (it does, but only because of the numbers; learners should compute).
- Analogy: conformations are like the positions of a door on its hinge; the door is the same door in every position, and some positions (wide open against a wall) are uncomfortable.
- Expected hands-on result: MMFF94 butane gauche 3.5, CH₃/H eclipsed 16.6, syn 21.8 kJ/mol; gauche factor 0.24; methylcyclohexane 25 chairs (20 eq, 5 ax), Δ 5.7 kJ/mol, 91 % equatorial. Dihedrals: equatorial CH₃–C1–C2–C3 about 175–180°, axial about 70–75°. Drill: 1,2-dichloroethane 4.7 kJ/mol; trans-1,2-dimethyl more stable; bromocyclohexane 69 % equatorial.
- Extension: scan the C2–C3 bond of 2,3-dimethylbutane or the O–C–C–O dihedral of ethylene glycol (an intramolecular H-bond makes gauche lower than anti) and compare with butane.
