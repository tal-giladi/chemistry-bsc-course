# Instructor notes — 12.3 Tunnelling

- Objective: the learner computes $\kappa$, decay lengths and barrier transmissions, explains the exponential sensitivity behind STM and H/D isotope effects, and gets a double-well splitting from the numerical solver.
- Likely confusion: $e^{-\kappa d}$ (amplitude) versus $e^{-2\kappa d}$ (probability); $V_0$ versus $V_0 - E$ in $\kappa$.
- Misconception: "The particle borrows energy to get through." Energy is conserved; the wavefunction simply is not zero in the forbidden region. Also: "tunnelling only matters for electrons" — it matters for H nuclei too.
- Analogy: frustrated total internal reflection: light totally reflected inside glass still leaks into a second piece of glass brought within a wavelength.
- Expected hands-on result: κ = 10.25 nm⁻¹, ×7.8 per 0.1 nm; T = 0.777, 0.169, 0.0235, 1.42e−4; H/D 2.18e−4/3.75e−6 = 58; double well NH3 0.870 cm⁻¹ (26.1 GHz), ND3 0.049 cm⁻¹ (1.46 GHz), ratio 18; measured 23.79 and 1.59 GHz.
- Extension: tune $a$ and $V_b$ so that both measured splittings are reproduced at once, and discuss whether the two parameters are well determined; or compute $T(E)$ above the barrier and find the resonances where $T = 1$.
