# Instructor notes — 12.4 The harmonic oscillator and molecular vibrations

- Objective: the learner computes harmonic and Morse levels from $\omega_e$ and $\omega_e x_e$, predicts fundamentals, overtones and isotope shifts, and connects zero-point energy to kinetic isotope effects.
- Likely confusion: $\omega_e$ versus the observed fundamental; scaling of $\omega_e x_e$ with mass; why $\mu \approx m_\mathrm{H}$ for any X–H bond.
- Misconception: "At absolute zero the molecule stops vibrating." The zero-point energy is permanent. Also: "Overtones are at exact multiples."
- Analogy: a playground swing with chains that get slack at large amplitude: big swings take relatively less energy per step, so the steps shrink.
- Expected hands-on result: μ = 0.979593 u, D_e = 42 342 cm⁻¹ (5.25 eV), a = 1.7519 Å⁻¹, k = 516 N/m; Morse levels 1482.3, 4367.5, 7147.2, 9821.2, 12389.5, 14852.2 cm⁻¹; fundamental 2885.3 (NIST 2886.0), overtone 5664.9 (NIST 5668.5); ⟨r⟩ 1.290 → 1.389 Å; DCl fundamental 2090.8 cm⁻¹. Part A: hot band 2779.7 cm⁻¹, H³⁷Cl shift 2.2 cm⁻¹.
- Extension: fit $\omega_e$, $\omega_e x_e$ to the observed HCl band origins of several overtones (NIST WebBook references), or solve the Morse potential for HI and compare the number of bound levels $\approx 2D_e/\omega_e$.
