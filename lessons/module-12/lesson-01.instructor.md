# Instructor notes — 12.1 Why classical physics fails: photons, spectra and waves

- Objective: the learner links sharp spectral lines to quantised levels, uses the Rydberg formula in both directions, and judges "quantum or classical" from the de Broglie wavelength against the confinement size.
- Likely confusion: air versus vacuum wavelengths, and $R_\infty$ versus $R_\mathrm{H}$. Both are few-hundred-ppm effects that only matter in precise work, but the lab makes them visible.
- Misconception: "Bohr orbits are what atoms look like." Bohr's model is a stepping stone that happens to give hydrogen's energies; it fails for helium and has no orbitals' shapes.
- Analogy: a guitar string can only vibrate at notes that fit whole half-waves between its ends; an electron confined near a nucleus can only take energies whose waves fit.
- Expected hands-on result: vacuum wavelengths 656.4665, 486.2719, 434.1682, 410.2898 nm; $R_\mathrm{H}$ fit $1.096797\times10^7$ m⁻¹ ± ~100, +19 ppm from $1.0967758\times10^7$; +311 ppm without air correction; IE 13.598 eV; series limit 364.7 nm. CD spectroscope: continuous for incandescent, lines (546, 436 nm Hg) for fluorescent.
- Extension: add the Paschen lines (NIST, infrared, n₁ = 3) to the fit with a second x-variable, or compute the He⁺ Balmer-like lines (Z² scaling) and compare with NIST.
