# Instructor notes — 04.2 Oscillations and waves

- Objective: ω = √(k/m), the pendulum, the reduced mass and the force constant from an IR wavenumber, isotope shifts, v = fλ and the wavenumber; home measurement of g.
- Likely confusion: ω versus f, and units of c when the wavenumber is in cm⁻¹.
- Misconception: "A heavier atom makes a stronger bond, so it vibrates faster." The force constant is set by electrons and does not change; only the mass does.
- Analogy: a diving board. A stiff short board twangs fast; put a heavy diver on it and it swings slowly. Same board, different mass, different frequency.
- Expected hands-on result (example data): g = 9.783 ± 0.075 m/s², intercept +0.010 s², periods 1.106–1.912 s; HCl 8.97e13 Hz, 2991 cm⁻¹, 11.2 fs. Sim target k = 7.9 N/m for m = 0.20 kg, T = 1.00 s. Real data: 1–2 % from 9.80 m/s²; half-swing counting gives 4× too large.
- Safety: light bob only, secure anchor, small swings, CAUTION box. Phone-as-bob only over a bed in a padded bag.
- Extension: time 20 swings at 10°, 20° and 30° at one length and compare with T(θ) ≈ T0(1 + θ²/16); or record the amplitude every 50 swings and estimate the damping time and Q.
