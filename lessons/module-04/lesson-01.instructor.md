# Instructor notes — 04.1 Forces, energy and momentum

- Objective: F = ma, work as an integral, F = −dU/dx, momentum transfer, and the three energy scales (J per molecule, kJ/mol, eV); applied to N2 speeds and the HCl Morse well.
- Likely confusion: per molecule versus per mole. Make the learner write the unit after every number, including "per molecule".
- Misconception: "Energy from many collisions adds up until the bond breaks." Each collision can add or remove energy; what matters is the Boltzmann chance of one large share (Module 21, 22).
- Analogy: the potential well is a valley; the force is how steep the ground is under your feet, always pushing you downhill.
- Expected hands-on result: F = 0 at 0.1275 nm; largest restoring force 6.91 nN at 0.1646 nm; W(0.01 nm) = 2.13e-20 J (exact 2.15e-20) = 5.2 kT; harmonic 2.58e-20 J (+21 %); kT = 0.56 % of De. Pencil: 3.4e-5 mol, Ar 431 m/s.
- Safety: none (pencil and Python only).
- Extension: add a Lennard-Jones curve for two argon atoms and compare its well depth with kT; or plot U(r) for DCl and note that the curve is identical (the potential does not depend on isotope; lesson 04.2 shows the vibration does).
