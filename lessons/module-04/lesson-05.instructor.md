# Instructor notes — 04.5 Light as an electromagnetic wave and as photons (module practical)

- Objective: c = λν, wavenumber, E = hν, eV↔nm and kJ/mol↔nm shortcuts, spectral regions mapped to molecular gaps, photon counting; module practical h from LED turn-on voltages.
- Likely confusion: brightness versus photon energy; per-photon versus per-mole energies.
- Misconception: "Strong enough light of any colour will break a bond." One photon must carry the bond energy (single-photon chemistry); intensity only changes the rate.
- Analogy: a vending machine that takes only one coin at a time; a pile of small coins (red photons) never buys the item, one big coin (UV photon) does.
- Expected hands-on result (example data): V0 = 1.941 (red 630), 2.028 (amber 595), 2.127 (yellow-green 570), 2.595 V (blue 470); h = (6.57 ± 0.19)e-34 J s (−0.8 %), intercept −0.026 V. Real LEDs: 5–20 % from 6.626e-34; InGaN blue/green may pull the slope. Pencil: 4.88 eV / 471 kJ/mol; 280 nm; 12.2 cm and 1.0e-5 eV; 2.7e15 photons/s.
- Safety: 3 × AA only, series resistor always in, visible LEDs only, no lasers, no staring at LEDs; WARNING box. Maximum current with knob at zero is about 17 mA (red), within normal LED ratings.
- Extension: build a CD-grating spectroscope (Module 25 preview) to measure each LED's actual peak wavelength and refit; or cool an LED in the fridge and watch V0 rise slightly.
