# Instructor notes — 04.3 Electrostatics: charge, field and potential

- Objective: Coulomb force and energy, field, potential and voltage, the eV and 96.485 kJ/mol per volt, relative permittivity, dipoles and partial charge, superposition and the Madelung constant.
- Likely confusion: volt versus electronvolt, and the 1/r versus 1/r² scaling of energy versus force.
- Misconception: "Water dissolves salt because it reacts with it." It mostly screens the attraction (ε_r ≈ 78) and solvates the ions; no new bonds to the ions' nuclei form.
- Analogy: the solvent is a crowd between two people shouting to each other; the louder the crowd (higher ε_r), the weaker the message that gets through.
- Expected hands-on result: U(vacuum) = −9.77e-19 J = −6.10 eV = −589 kJ/mol; water −7.5 kJ/mol = −3.0 kT; chain 1.29127 (n=10), 1.37634 (100), 1.38619 (10000); rock salt Evjen 1.75177 (2), 1.74772 (4), 1.74757 (8, 16); crystal Coulomb energy −861 kJ/mol. Pencil: 145 kJ/mol, 1.2e-11 N, 1.92 D.
- Safety: none (pencil and Python only).
- Extension: compute the energy of an ion–dipole pair (Na⁺ and a water molecule, μ = 1.85 D, at 0.24 nm) and compare with the ion–ion value; or sum the rock-salt series shell by shell without weights and watch it fail to converge.
