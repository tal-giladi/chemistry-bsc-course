# Instructor notes — 14.4 From coupled masses to the vibrations of CO2

- Objective: the learner derives the normal modes of CO2 from a three-mass model, fits k and the interaction k12 to the measured stretches, predicts 13C and 18O shifts, and explains IR activity from the mode shapes.
- Likely confusion: why there are four vibrations but only two IR fundamentals; why nu1 ignores the carbon mass; units (u to kg, cm^-1 to rad/s with c in cm/s).
- Misconception: "Non-polar molecules cannot absorb IR." Absorption needs a changing dipole, not a permanent one. Also: "The 1333 band is missing from the IR spectrum because it is weak." It is absent by symmetry.
- Analogy: two people holding hands with a child in the middle. If both adults step outward together the child stays still (nu1); if the child runs one way and both adults the other, the middle moves most (nu3).
- Expected hands-on result: one-constant fit to nu3, k = 1418 N/m, nu1 predicted 1227 cm^-1; two-constant fit k = 1547 N/m, k12 = 128 N/m, k_delta = 0.769 aJ/rad^2; shapes (-1, 0, 1) and (0.375, -1, 0.375); ZPE 2508 cm^-1 = 30.0 kJ/mol; 13C16O2 1333.0, 648.0, 2282.1 cm^-1 (measured nu3 2282.4 in ice); 12C18O2 1256.6, 656.8, 2313.0 cm^-1. NIST WebBook spectrum: strong bands at 2349 and 667, none at 1333.
- Extension: build the mechanical analogue from three pendulums with outer bobs 160 g and middle bob 120 g and check the frequency order of the stretch-like modes; or fit k and k12 to the CCCBDB harmonic values and compare with the fundamental-based fit.
