# Instructor notes — 07.4 VSEPR, polarity and intermolecular forces

- **Objective:** shape and bond angles from the Lewis structure; polarity as a vector sum; IMF type and boiling-point ranking; the size of the hydrogen-bonding anomaly in real data.
- **Likely confusion:** electron-domain geometry versus molecular shape. Have learners always say both ("tetrahedral domains, bent molecule").
- **Misconception:** boiling breaks bonds inside the molecule. Ask what steam is made of; then compare an O–H bond (about 460 kJ/mol) with the enthalpy of vaporisation of water (about 41 kJ/mol).
- **Analogy:** balloons tied together at their knots arrange themselves into the VSEPR shapes; a fatter balloon (lone pair) squeezes the others.
- **Expected hands-on result:** Part A 5–6/6, ClF₃ and XeF₂ are the usual slips (lone pairs put axial). Part B anomalies about −26, +80, +179 and +123 °C for CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, HF; the CH₄ value shows how crude a two-point extrapolation is.
- **Extension:** add the noble gases (Ne to Xe) from PubChem to the plot as a pure-dispersion reference, or compare the boiling points of n-pentane, isopentane and neopentane and relate them to surface contact.
