# Instructor notes — 07.7 Lab: weighing, glassware and making solutions (module practical)

- **Objective:** weigh by difference; choose glassware by tolerance; read a meniscus correctly; prepare a standard by mass and a serial dilution; build and read an uncertainty budget.
- **Likely confusion:** tolerance versus standard uncertainty ($t/\sqrt{3}$), and relative versus absolute uncertainty when adding terms.
- **Misconception:** "more decimal places on the balance always gives a better standard". Worked example 1 shows purity dominating; a better balance changes nothing.
- **Analogy:** a chain is as strong as its weakest link; a budget is as good as its largest term, because the terms are squared.
- **Expected hands-on result:** simulation by-difference mass within 0.2 mg; example data $c = 0.8845 \pm 0.0078$ mol/L, salt mass about 85 % of the variance, serial 1/32 at 0.64 % versus 6.3 % direct. Home density check within about ±0.005 g/mL of 1.034 g/mL with a 0.1 g scale.
- **Extension:** redo Part B with 10 g in 200 g and show the budget halves; or photograph the dilution cups, average the red, green and blue values of each cup with Python and look for a straight line of $-\log$(channel) against dilution (a preview of Beer–Lambert in Module 19).
- **Scope:** say once that the simulation and the kitchen bench do not train real pipetting or analytical-balance technique.
