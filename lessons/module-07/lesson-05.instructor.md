# Instructor notes — 07.5 Stoichiometry and limiting reagents

- **Objective:** balance equations; find the limiting reagent with $n_i/\nu_i$; theoretical and percent yield; measure vinegar strength from a mass-loss break point.
- **Likely confusion:** "the reagent there is less of is limiting". Use 2 H₂ + O₂ with 3 mol H₂ and 2 mol O₂ until learners divide by coefficients without being asked.
- **Misconception:** coefficients are mass ratios. Ask how many grams of O₂ burn 2 g of H₂ (16 g, not 1 g).
- **Analogy:** sandwiches: 2 slices of bread per slice of cheese. With 10 slices of bread and 8 of cheese the bread limits at 5 sandwiches, although there is more bread.
- **Expected hands-on result:** example data give a break point of 3.6 g and 0.86 mol/L (5.2 % w/v; true 5.0 %). Home results within ±10 % of the label with a 0.1 g scale; a 1 g scale with 100 mL portions gives ±15–20 %. Foam spilling over and forgetting the spoon are the common failures.
- **Extension:** repeat with citric acid (food grade, 3 NaHCO₃ per citric acid) or lemon juice and show the break point moves by the coefficient ratio; or estimate the uncertainty of the break point by refitting with one point left out at a time.
