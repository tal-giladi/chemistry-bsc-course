# Instructor notes — 07.6 Gases: ideal and real

- **Objective:** ideal gas law with consistent units; Dalton's law; Z from van der Waals and from NIST data; judging when the ideal law is good enough.
- **Likely confusion:** which value of R to use. Make learners write the units of every quantity before substituting.
- **Misconception:** "real gases take up more room, so Z is always above 1". The CO₂ data show Z falling to 0.31 first; attractions dominate until the molecules are pressed into contact.
- **Analogy:** people in a hall. In a nearly empty hall they move freely (ideal). In a crowded party they cluster in groups (attractions, Z < 1). In a packed train nobody can move closer (excluded volume, Z > 1).
- **Expected hands-on result:** simulation slope gives R within 2 %; Python table with Z(NIST) 0.996, 0.771 and a minimum of 0.314 at 120 bar; van der Waals within 2 % up to about 80 bar and too high above 100 bar.
- **Extension:** fetch the 280 K CO₂ isotherm from the NIST fluid tables (below Tc = 304 K), plot the van der Waals loop and discuss which of the three cubic roots is gas, liquid and unphysical; this leads into phase diagrams in Module 13.
