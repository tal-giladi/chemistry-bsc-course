# Instructor notes — 07.2 Electron configuration and periodic trends

- **Objective:** configurations to Kr (with Cr, Cu and the 4s-first ion rule), radius and IE trends from Z_eff, the two IE dips, valence electrons from IE jumps.
- **Likely confusion:** "4s fills first but leaves first". Explain that orbital energies depend on how many electrons are present; in the ion 3d is lower.
- **Misconception:** electrons as planets on fixed circular orbits. Orbitals are probability regions; Module 12 makes this precise.
- **Analogy:** shielding is like listening to a speaker from the back of a crowded hall: the people in front (core electrons) block most of the sound; people beside you (same shell) block very little.
- **Expected hands-on result:** dips Li, B, O, Na, Al, S, K, V, Co, Ni, Ga, Se; Na/K 1, Mg/Ca 2 valence electrons. Learners often call the d-block dips "important"; they are under 0.25 eV.
- **Extension:** add IE4 for Al (119.99 eV, NIST ASD) to the CSV and confirm 3 valence electrons; plot IE1 against 1/n² × Z_eff estimates; compare Pauling electronegativity with (IE + EA)/2 (Mulliken).
