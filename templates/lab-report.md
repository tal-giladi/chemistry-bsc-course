# Lab report

Use this for every lab in the course: simulated, computational or at home. Keep it short: a good report is 2–4 pages.

## Title, date, author

One line that names what was measured, for example "Acetic acid content of household vinegar by titration".

## Aim

One or two sentences: the quantity you measured and why.

## Background

The equation or principle the measurement relies on, with symbols defined. Cite the lesson or source.

## Materials and method

- Materials with their grade or source (for example "household vinegar, 5% label value").
- Equipment with its resolution (for example "10 mL syringe, 0.2 mL graduations").
- The procedure as numbered steps, written so someone else could repeat it.
- If it was a simulation: its name, preset and seed.

## Safety

The hazards (GHS pictograms where relevant) and what you did about each one.

## Results

Raw data in a table with units. Every derived number with its uncertainty. One figure per main result, with labelled axes and units.

## Analysis

How you got from raw data to the result: equations, the fit used, the error propagation. Use the [error analysis](error-analysis.md) template.

## Discussion

- Compare with the expected or literature value. Is the difference inside your uncertainty?
- The largest source of error and how you would reduce it.
- What the simulation or home version could not show compared with a real lab.

## Conclusion

The result as a single sentence with value, uncertainty and units.
