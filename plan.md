# Self-taught Chemistry Degree — Technion B.Sc. track

This curriculum mirrors the Technion B.Sc. in Chemistry (Schulich Faculty of Chemistry, program 012042-1-000). That degree is 124 credit points over 3 years, full time. Here it is turned into a part-time self-study plan of about 10–12 hours a week.

The wet labs are replaced by interactive simulations that we build inside the courses, plus real published data and a few safe home experiments. This follows Tal's rule: a simulation counts if you learn everything the lab teaches apart from the physical hand practice.

You will not get a degree. You will not be trained to work at a lab bench. See "What this plan does not train" below. Read it before you start.

Source for every course number and credit value below: the official Technion catalog for 2025/2026 (תשפ"ו), chemistry chapter: https://ugportal.technion.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/12-הפקולטה-לכימיה-תשפ״ו.pdf. It is listed on the catalog page https://ugportal.technion.ac.il/מידע-לסטודנטים/קטלוג-לימודים/. ### How much of it is lab

- Pure lab courses that are required: **14.5 points** (01240212, 01240611, 01240610, 01240911, 01240214, 01240613).
- Required within the electives: at least **6 points** of advanced lab or research project.
- So at least **20.5 of 124 points (about 17%)** are lab or research work. Counting the two Foundations courses that include lab sessions, it is up to 26.5 points (about 21%).
- The two research projects need real lab hours: 01240353 is 180 hours in a research lab; 01240355 is about 240 hours in a Technion research group.

## Toolkit used throughout

**Free courses (verify the current run on the platform):**
- MIT OpenCourseWare: 5.111SC Principles of Chemical Science, 5.12 and 5.13 Organic Chemistry, 5.60 Thermodynamics and Kinetics, 5.61 and 5.62 Physical Chemistry, 5.07SC Biological Chemistry. ocw.mit.edu.
- MIT OCW 5.301 Chemistry Laboratory Techniques. Videos of every basic technique. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-301-chemistry-laboratory-techniques-january-iap-2012/
- MIT OCW 5.310 Laboratory Chemistry and 5.35 Introduction to Experimental Chemistry. Real lab manuals, with procedures and the reasoning behind them.
- Yale Open Courses, CHEM 125 Freshman Organic Chemistry. https://oyc.yale.edu/chemistry/chem-125a
- LibreTexts Chemistry, free textbooks for every subject: https://chem.libretexts.org/
- OpenStax Chemistry 2e, free: https://openstax.org/details/books/chemistry-2e

**Real data (this is how you learn to read instruments):**
- SDBS spectral database (IR, NMR, MS, Raman), AIST Japan: https://sdbs.db.aist.go.jp/
- NIST Chemistry WebBook (IR, mass spectra, UV-Vis, thermodynamic data): https://webbook.nist.gov/chemistry/
- NMRDB, NMR prediction: https://www.nmrdb.org/
- PubChem, with safety data for each compound: https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/
- MassBank, mass spectra (site did not load when checked; verify).

**Simulations and software, free:**
- ChemCollective virtual labs (Carnegie Mellon): https://chemcollective.org/vlabs
- PhET chemistry simulations: https://phet.colorado.edu/
- Avogadro (molecule builder): https://avogadro.cc/. ORCA (quantum chemistry, free for academic and personal use; check the licence): https://www.orcasoftware.de/tutorials_orca/. xtb: https://xtb-docs.readthedocs.io/. WebMO: https://www.webmo.net/
- phyphox, a phone app that turns your phone sensors into lab instruments: https://phyphox.org/

**Hebrew gap.** Free Hebrew chemistry lectures at university level are rare. The Technion course descriptions name the topics. You learn each topic from MIT, Yale or LibreTexts in English. The math and physics courses are standard. Any good free course will do.

**Safety rule for home work.** Use kitchen and pharmacy materials only. Do not buy lab reagents. In Israel many chemicals need a permit under the Hazardous Substances Law 1993 (חוק החומרים המסוכנים, התשנ"ג-1993), and home benches have no fume hood. Every home experiment below uses food-grade or household items.

## Year 1 — Math, physics and chemistry foundations

**1.1 Calculus 1 and 2** (01040003, 01040004)
- Course: MIT 18.01 and 18.02 (OCW). Khan Academy for gaps.
- Practice: do every problem set. Check against the posted solutions.

**1.2 Linear algebra** (01040019)
- Course: MIT 18.06 (Strang).

**1.3 Ordinary differential equations** (01040131)
- Course: MIT 18.03.
- Practice: model a first-order reaction and a damped oscillator.

**1.4 Physics 1 and 2** (01140077, 01140052)
- Covers: mechanics, special relativity basics, electricity, magnetism, waves.
- Course: MIT 8.01 and 8.02.

**1.5 Topics in modern biology** (01340127)
- Course: MIT 7.012 Introduction to Biology.

**1.6 Foundations of chemistry A and B** (01240117, 01240118)
- Covers: atoms, bonding, orbitals, stoichiometry, gases, thermodynamics basics, equilibrium, acids and bases, redox, the Nernst equation.
- Course: MIT 5.111SC. OpenStax Chemistry 2e.
- Lab part: simulations S1 and S2 (see "Labs as simulations").

**1.7 Python for scientists** (02340128)
- Course: any free Python course. Then use Python for every data analysis in the plan.
- Practice: write a script that fits a straight line to data and reports the error.

**1.8 Analytical chemistry 1** (01240220)
- Covers: experimental error, error propagation, standard deviation, confidence intervals, linear regression, detection limits, titration theory, buffers, gravimetry.
- Read: LibreTexts "Analytical Chemistry 2.1" (Harvey).

## Year 2 — The chemistry core

**2.1 Quantum theory in chemistry** (01240408)
- Covers: the Schrödinger equation, particle in a box, tunnelling, the harmonic oscillator, rotors, selection rules.
- Course: MIT 5.61.

**2.2 Chemical thermodynamics** (01240415)
- Course: MIT 5.60.

**2.3 Organic chemistry 1 and 2** (01240708, 01240711)
- Covers: bonding, stereochemistry, substitution and elimination, alkenes, alkynes, carbonyls, aromatic chemistry, amines, sugars, heterocycles. Reading NMR, IR and mass spectra.
- Course: Yale CHEM 125, MIT 5.12 and 5.13.
- Practice: identify 50 unknowns from real SDBS spectra.

**2.4 Inorganic chemistry** (01240305)
- Covers: the periodic table, structure and properties of inorganic compounds.
- Read: LibreTexts inorganic chemistry books.

**2.5 Analytical chemistry 2** (01240213)
- Covers: spectrophotometry, flame and plasma analysis, fluorimetry, extraction, gas and liquid chromatography, ion exchange, electroanalysis, cyclic voltammetry.

**2.6 Molecular spectroscopy** (01240417)
- Covers: absorption and emission, rotational and vibrational spectra, Raman, electronic spectra, Franck-Condon, symmetry and group theory.

**2.7 Statistical thermodynamics** (01240413) and **chemical kinetics** (01240414)
- Course: MIT 5.62 and 5.60 (kinetics part).

**2.8 Technical English** (03240033)
- If your English is already strong, replace with reading one review article a week.

**2.9 Labs this year:** S3 to S7.

## Year 3 — Advanced core and electives

**3.1 Bio-inorganic chemistry** (01240210)
- Covers: bonding in transition-metal complexes, their magnetic and optical properties, metals in respiration and enzymes.

**3.2 Electromagnetism and matter** (01240416)
- Covers: Maxwell's equations, multipoles, intermolecular forces, polarisability, dielectrics.

**3.3 Structure and reactivity in organic chemistry** (01240703)
- Covers: potential energy surfaces, reaction mechanisms, Hammond postulate, Hammett equation, reactive intermediates.

**3.4 Biochemistry and enzymology** (01340019)
- Course: MIT 5.07SC.

**3.5 Electives (19.5 points in the real degree).** You must include:
- (a) At least 6 points of advanced lab or research. In this plan: 01260603 Advanced Lab in Computational Chemistry (3.0), done for real at home, plus a 180-hour home research project in place of 01240353 (4.0). See S10 and S11.
- (b) At least 2 courses from the faculty list. Good choices you can learn at home: 01270427 Solid State (3.5), 01270453 Electrochemistry (3.0), 01270438 Symmetry and its Uses in Chemistry (4.0), 01270454 Numerical Simulation in Chemical Physics (3.0), 01270446 Intro to Molecular Quantum Technology (3.5), 01270730 Structure Determination by Physical Methods (2.5).

## Labs as simulations

For each real lab: what it teaches, the simulation to build, real data to use, and a safe home experiment where one exists. Every simulation must teach five things: the procedure, why each step is done, the safety reasoning, what can go wrong, and how to analyse the data and its error.

**S1–S2. Foundations of Chemistry A and B, lab part** (01240117, 01240118)
- The catalog says only that the lab meets 3 times a semester. The exact experiments are not public (unverified).
- Simulate: weighing, volumetric glassware, making solutions, a first titration, simple calorimetry.
- Home: make a red-cabbage pH indicator. Test vinegar, baking soda, soap and lemon juice.

**S3. Analytical Chemistry Lab 1** (01240212) — 11 experiments
- Teaches: solutions and solubility; acid-base titrations; visual and potentiometric redox titrations; manganese in steel; precipitation titrations; ion-selective electrodes; gravimetry and complexometry; electrogravimetry and coulometry; water testing.
- Build: a titration bench. You pick the indicator, read the burette, and add drops near the endpoint. The sim adds realistic noise, parallax and over-shooting. Then add a potentiometric version with electrode drift, a gravimetry module (losses in filtering and drying), and a coulometry module.
- Use: ChemCollective titration labs, to check against.
- Home: titrate vinegar with baking-soda solution. Use the cabbage indicator and a medical syringe as a burette. Repeat 5 times and compute the mean and standard deviation.

**S4. Intro Lab in Chemical Physics** (01240611)
- Teaches: harmonic and anharmonic oscillators, normal modes of a three-body system, time signals and computer processing, simple circuits, forced current, resonance width and Q factor, the vibrational spectrum of a triatomic molecule.
- This lab is mostly doable at home for real.
- Home: masses on springs, filmed or measured with phyphox. A low-voltage RLC circuit on a breadboard (battery or USB power only). Fit the resonance curve in Python.
- Build: a coupled-oscillator sim that links three masses on springs to the vibrations of CO2.

**S5. Physical Chemistry Lab 1** (01240610)
- Teaches: thermodynamics, kinetic theory of gases, thermochemistry, chemical equilibrium, electrochemistry and potentiometry, kinetics, spectrophotometry, surface chemistry.
- Build: one sim per topic. Each one has realistic instrument noise and a full error analysis.
- Home:
  - Coffee-cup calorimeter: citric acid with baking soda (cold), and dissolving salts.
  - Kinetics: fading of food dye in dilute household bleach, tracked with phone-camera colour readings. Small amounts, good airflow, never mix bleach with acids or ammonia.
  - Electrochemistry: a lemon or salt-water battery with zinc and copper. Measure it with a cheap multimeter.
  - Surface chemistry: count drops of water with more and more detergent to see surface tension fall.

**S6. Organic Chemistry Lab 1** (01240911)
- Teaches: simple syntheses and the core techniques: reflux, extraction, recrystallization, distillation, TLC, melting point. Also the mechanism of each reaction.
- This lab cannot be done at home. It needs organic solvents and a fume hood.
- Build: a virtual bench for 6 classic syntheses (for example aspirin, an SN1 or SN2 reaction, a Grignard, an aldol). At each step you choose (solvent, heating rate, when to stop) and the sim shows what happens: oiling out, bumping, emulsions, low yield, a wet product. It ends with yield, melting point, TLC and real spectra of the product from SDBS.
- Watch: MIT 5.301 technique videos for each step.
- Home: recrystallize alum or sugar. Run paper chromatography of food dyes and markers. These train the same ideas (solubility, purity, separation) at kitchen level.

**S7. Analytical Chemistry Lab 2** (01240214)
- Teaches: UV-Vis spectrophotometry and mixtures; fluorescence (quinine in drinks, aspirin); atomic absorption and emission; gas chromatography (homologous series, limonene); GC-MS; HPLC; cyclic voltammetry.
- Build: instrument sims. A Beer-Lambert spectrometer with stray light and a calibration curve. A chromatogram generator (column, temperature program, retention, peak overlap). A GC-MS step that matches your peaks against real NIST spectra. A cyclic voltammetry sim.
- Use: NIST WebBook and SDBS for real spectra.
- Home: build a phone spectrometer from a cardboard box and a piece of a DVD. Shine a UV flashlight on tonic water to see quinine fluoresce. Measure food-dye solutions at several dilutions and plot Beer-Lambert.

**S8. Physical Chemistry Lab 2** (01240613)
- Teaches: advanced measurements in kinetic gas theory, thermochemistry, equilibrium, kinetics, electrochemistry, surfaces, electromagnetism of matter, UV, visible and IR spectroscopy, electron spin resonance, thermal and acoustic measurements.
- Build: IR and ESR spectrometer sims that start from the physics (selection rules, hyperfine splitting) and end with real published spectra to interpret.
- Home: measure the speed of sound in air with a phone and a tube. Then compute the heat-capacity ratio of air. This is a classic physical chemistry experiment.
- This plan picks 01240613 over 01240912 (Organic Lab 2) because more of it can be simulated from first principles.

**S9. Organic Lab 2 (optional, 01240912)**
- Teaches: multi-step synthesis with IR, UV and NMR checks.
- Build: a multi-step version of S6 with spectra at each step.

**S10. Advanced Lab in Computational Chemistry** (01260603) — done for real
- Teaches: DFT, time-dependent DFT, molecular mechanics, Hartree-Fock, conformer search, transition states, calculated IR and NMR spectra, excited states.
- Home: this is the same work as in the real course. Use ORCA, xtb and Avogadro on a laptop.

**S11. Home research project** (in place of 01240353, 180 hours)
- A computational or data project. Examples: compare calculated and measured IR spectra for 30 molecules; or build a model that predicts a property from public data.
- Output: a written report and a poster, as the Technion asks.
- Honest note: the real project is supervised by a researcher. Try to find an academic who will read your report.

## What this plan does not train

State this plainly to anyone who asks:
- Hand skills at a real bench: weighing on an analytical balance, pipetting and burette technique, assembling reflux and distillation setups, separating funnels, filtering and drying a product.
- Safe handling of real corrosive, flammable and toxic reagents. Working at a fume hood. Chemical waste disposal.
- Operating real instruments: preparing NMR samples, injecting into a GC, caring for an HPLC column.
- Working in a shared lab under supervision, and keeping a lab notebook that someone checks.

You will understand every experiment, the safety reasoning and the data. You will not have the hands. An employer would still have to train you at the bench.

## Time estimate

The real degree is about 20 points a semester of full-time work. At 10–12 hours a week it takes much longer:

| Stage | Duration (10–12 h/week) |
|---|---|
| Year 1 (math, physics, foundations) | 2 years |
| Year 2 (chemistry core) | 2 years |
| Year 3 (advanced core, electives, S10–S11) | 1.5–2 years |
| **Total** | **~5.5–6 years** |

At 18–20 hours a week it is about 3.5–4 years. Math and physics take the longest. If you already know them, take off about a year.

**Fast "understand chemistry" route (~18 months):** 1.6, 1.8, 2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.6, with simulations S3, S5, S6 and S7.
